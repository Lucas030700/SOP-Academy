## 1. Topic Overview

### What is a Feasible Supply Plan?

Imagine na may manufacturing company na nakatanggap ng customer demand na **10,000 units next month**.

Ang initial reaction ng production planner ay gumawa ng production schedule na 10,000 units.

Pero may ilang problema:

- Kaya lang gumawa ng factory ng 8,000 units within the available production time.
- May raw materials lang para sa 7,500 units.
- May machine na kailangan ng maintenance.
- Kulang ang available manpower.
- Ang supplier ng critical material ay may 30-day lead time.

Technically, may production plan na 10,000 units.

Pero realistically, hindi kayang gawin ng company ang 10,000 units gamit ang available resources.

Dito pumapasok ang concept ng **Feasible Supply Plan**.

**Definition:**

A Feasible Supply Plan is a supply plan that has been evaluated and validated against the company's actual operational capabilities, resource availability, material availability, production capacity, inventory position, and timing requirements.

Sa madaling salita:

> Hindi sapat na alam natin kung gaano karaming products ang kailangan gawin. Kailangan din nating patunayan na kaya talaga itong gawin ng company.

### Bakit mahalaga ito sa S&OP?

Ang purpose ng S&OP ay hindi lamang magkaroon ng demand forecast at production plan.

Ang objective ay magkaroon ng isang coordinated business plan na:

1. Makakatugon sa expected customer demand.
2. Kayang suportahan ng manufacturing operations.
3. May sapat na raw materials at resources.
4. Financially acceptable sa company.
5. Realistic sa timing at delivery commitments.

Ang feasible supply plan ang nagsisilbing bridge between **what the business wants to achieve** and **what the business can realistically deliver**.

## 2. Layman's Explanation

### 2.1 Proposed Supply Plan vs. Feasible Supply Plan

Let's use a simple example.

May bakery na nakatanggap ng order para sa 1,000 loaves of bread.

Ang bakery owner ay gumawa ng proposed plan:

- Produce 1,000 loaves.
- Deliver all orders on Friday.

Pero pagkatapos niyang i-check ang operations:

- Oven capacity: 700 loaves.
- Available flour: enough for 800 loaves.
- Available workers: enough for 750 loaves.
- Available production time: enough for 700 loaves.

Hindi feasible ang original plan.

Kaya kailangan niyang mag-adjust.

Possible solutions:

- Mag-overtime.
- Magdagdag ng production shift.
- Bumili ng additional flour.
- Gumamit ng additional oven.
- I-adjust ang delivery schedule.

After evaluating these options, nakagawa siya ng revised plan na kayang suportahan ng actual resources.

### Proposed vs. Feasible

**Proposed Supply Plan**

- 1,000 units
- Based on expected requirements
- Not yet validated

**Initial Feasible Capacity**

- 700 units
- Supported by available production resources
- Capacity-based only; still requires material, manpower, and timing validation

Illustrative example. The 700-unit figure is capacity-based and still requires material, manpower, and timing validation.

**Important distinction:** A proposed supply plan is a plan that has been developed. A feasible supply plan is a plan that has passed the necessary validation.

Hindi ibig sabihin na kapag feasible sa production capacity ay automatically feasible na rin overall. Kailangan ma-check lahat ng critical requirements.

### 2.2 The Four Basic Questions of Supply Feasibility

Every time na nagva-validate tayo ng supply plan, dapat masagot natin ang apat na questions:

1. **Can we produce it?** May available production capacity ba?
2. **Do we have the resources?** May machines, manpower, at materials ba?
3. **Can we produce it on time?** Pasok ba sa lead times at production schedule?
4. **Can we afford the plan?** Acceptable ba ang additional costs and financial implications?

Kapag may isang critical requirement na hindi satisfied, maaaring hindi feasible ang plan.

## 3. Technical Explanation

### 3.1 Supply Plan Feasibility

Sa professional S&OP environment, feasibility is evaluated across multiple dimensions.

Hindi lang ito simpleng comparison ng production quantity versus machine capacity.

Mayroong interconnected requirements na kailangang mag-align.

### Supply Plan Feasibility Framework

**Demand Requirements**

Forecast + Customer Orders + Backlog

↓

**Supply Plan Validation**

- **Capacity:** Machines and hours
- **Resources:** Manpower and skills
- **Materials:** Raw materials and suppliers
- **Timing:** Lead times and schedules
- **Inventory + Operational Constraints:** Opening stock, WIP, bottlenecks, maintenance, yield

↓

**Feasible Supply Plan**

Validated quantities, timing, constraints, and supply commitments.

### 3.2 Five Dimensions of Feasibility

| Dimension | Main question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity feasibility | Kaya ba ng equipment ang required output? | 10,000 units required, 8,000 capacity |
| Resource feasibility | Sapat ba ang manpower at supporting resources? | 3 operators required, 2 available |
| Material feasibility | Sapat ba ang raw materials? | 10,000 units required, materials for 7,500 |
| Time feasibility | Available ba ang supply before the required date? | Material arrives after production deadline |
| Inventory feasibility | Sapat ba ang existing and projected inventory? | Beginning stock can cover part of demand |

May isang importanteng concept dito:

**Feasibility is time-phased.**

Meaning, hindi sapat na may available capacity or materials within the entire month. Kailangan available ang mga ito sa tamang period.

For example:

- Demand requirement: Week 1.
- Raw material arrival: Week 3.

May material nga ang company, pero hindi ito available when production needs it.

Therefore, hindi pa rin feasible ang Week 1 supply requirement.

### 3.3 Important Feasibility Requirements

Let's examine each requirement more closely.

**A. Production Capacity and Machine Availability**

Kailangan malaman kung ilang units ang kayang gawin ng manufacturing facility within a given period.

Considerations:
- Available operating hours.
- Machine production rate.
- Planned maintenance.
- Unplanned downtime.
- Changeover and setup time.
- Production efficiency.
- Yield and scrap rate.
- Existing production commitments.

Example:

A machine operates for 20 hours per day, with a production rate of 50 units per hour.

[
20 	imes 50 = 1,000 	ext{ units/day}
]

Pero kung may 2 hours of maintenance at 90% operating efficiency:

[
(20-2)	imes50	imes0.90
]

[
=810	ext{ units/day}
]

Hindi dapat gamitin ang theoretical capacity na 1,000 units kung 810 units lang ang realistic expected output under these assumptions.

**B. Manpower and Resource Availability**

Kahit available ang machines, kailangan ding available ang qualified operators.

Examples:
- Insufficient machine operators.
- Limited technicians.
- Employee absences.
- Required technical certifications.
- Limited maintenance personnel.
- Shared resources between production lines.

Important: May pagkakataon na available ang machine capacity pero manpower ang nagiging bottleneck.

**C. Raw Materials and Supplier Constraints**

Hindi makakapag-produce kung kulang ang required raw materials.

Kailangan i-evaluate:
- Current raw material inventory.
- Open purchase orders.
- Supplier delivery schedules.
- Supplier production capacity.
- Minimum order quantities (MOQ).
- Material quality and approval status.
- Supplier reliability.

Example:

Required production: 5,000 units.

Material consumption: 2 kg per unit.

[
5,000 	imes 2 = 10,000	ext{ kg}
]

Kung 8,000 kg lang ang available material, may shortage na 2,000 kg.

**D. Inventory Availability**

Inventory is an important part of supply feasibility because production is not always required to cover the entire demand.

May existing finished goods inventory na puwedeng gamitin.

Example:

| Item | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Customer demand | 5,000 |
| Beginning finished goods inventory | 1,000 |
| Required production | 4,000 |

Assuming no other inventory adjustments:

[
5,000-1,000=4,000
]

Hindi kailangang gumawa ng 5,000 units kung may 1,000 units na available inventory.

Pero kailangang i-check kung:
- Available and released for shipment.
- Not allocated to another customer.
- Not on quality hold.
- Within product shelf life, if applicable.

**E. Production and Procurement Lead Times**

Lead time determines when a resource or product becomes available.

For example:

- Raw material procurement lead time: 30 days.
- Production lead time: 5 days.
- Shipping lead time: 3 days.

Kung kailangan ng customer ang product within 10 days, hindi automatic na feasible ang supply kahit may approved purchase request.

Kailangan i-consider ang complete timeline from procurement to customer delivery.

**F. Bottlenecks and Operational Limitations**

Remember Topic 4.6: Bottleneck Analysis.

A bottleneck is the operation or resource that restricts the output of the overall production process.

Example:

| Process | Maximum daily capacity |
|---|---:|
| Casting | 1,000 units |
| Stacking | 900 units |
| Cutting | 1,200 units |
| Firing | 800 units |
| Sorting | 1,100 units |

The effective line capacity is limited to 800 units/day, assuming these are sequential processes without additional parallel capacity or buffers.

Kahit 1,200 units/day ang capacity ng Cutting, hindi nito kayang i-compensate automatically ang 800 units/day limit ng Firing.

Ito ang dahilan kung bakit mahalaga ang bottleneck analysis sa feasibility validation.

## 4. Supply Plan Development and Validation

Now, let's move from understanding the concept to actually developing a supply plan.

### 4.1 Step 1: Understand the Demand Requirements

First, kailangan malaman kung gaano karaming products ang kailangan ng business.

Demand may consist of:

- Sales forecast.
- Confirmed customer orders.
- Existing backlog.
- Additional demand requirements.
- Strategic inventory build requirements.

Important distinction:

**Demand plan is not automatically equal to production requirement.**

Bakit?

Dahil maaaring may existing finished goods inventory, scheduled production, or other available supply.

### 4.2 Step 2: Calculate Net Production Requirements

Net Production Requirement is the quantity that needs to be produced after considering available inventory and other planned supply.

A simplified formula:

[
NPR=D+TEI-BI-PS
]

Where:

| Variable | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| NPR | Net Production Requirement | Units |
| D | Expected demand | Units |
| TEI | Target Ending Inventory | Units |
| BI | Beginning Inventory | Units |
| PS | Other available planned supply | Units |

Example:

- Demand = 5,000 units.
- Target ending inventory = 500 units.
- Beginning inventory = 1,000 units.
- Other scheduled supply = 500 units.

[
NPR=5,000+500-1,000-500
]

[
oxed{NPR=4,000	ext{ units}}
]

Therefore, 4,000 additional units ang kailangang i-produce.

Note: This is a simplified planning formula. Ensure that planned supply is not double-counted and that its timing and availability are valid.

### 4.3 Step 3: Translate Production Requirements into Resource Requirements

Once we know the production quantity, kailangan nating malaman kung ano ang resources na required.

Example:

Required production = 4,000 units.

Assumptions:

- Machine rate = 100 units/hour.
- Material consumption = 2 kg/unit.
- Labor requirement = 0.05 labor-hours/unit.

Calculate:

**Machine hours required**

[
4,000/100=40	ext{ hours}
]

**Raw materials required**

[
4,000	imes2=8,000	ext{ kg}
]

**Labor hours required**

[
4,000	imes0.05=200	ext{ labor-hours}
]

Now we have three requirements to validate:

| Resource | Required |
|---|---:|
| Machine hours | 40 hours |
| Raw materials | 8,000 kg |
| Labor hours | 200 labor-hours |

### 4.4 Step 4: Compare Requirements Against Available Resources

Let's assume:

| Resource | Required | Available | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Machine hours | 40 | 35 | Shortage of 5 hours |
| Raw materials | 8,000 kg | 9,000 kg | Sufficient |
| Labor hours | 200 | 180 | Shortage of 20 hours |

Interpretation:

- Materials are sufficient.
- Machine capacity is insufficient.
- Manpower is insufficient.

Therefore, the production plan is not fully feasible.

Kahit may enough raw materials, hindi pa rin guaranteed ang production output.

### 4.5 Step 5: Validate Timing and Inventory Projections

The final step is to check whether the plan can deliver the required supply in the correct period.

For example:

| Activity | Schedule |
|---|---|
| Raw material arrival | March 5 |
| Production start | March 7 |
| Production completion | March 12 |
| Customer delivery | March 15 |

This schedule may be feasible if all materials are available by March 7, production can finish by March 12, and the finished goods can be shipped by March 15.

But if the supplier delivers on March 10, the entire schedule may need to change.

**Key principle:** A supply plan must be feasible in quantity AND in timing.

### 4.6 Planning Assumptions and Constraints

Every supply plan is built using assumptions.

Examples:

| Planning assumption | Possible risk |
|---|---|
| Machine efficiency is 90% | Actual efficiency falls to 75% |
| Supplier delivers on schedule | Material arrives late |
| No major equipment breakdown | Unexpected downtime |
| Yield remains at 98% | Higher scrap rate |
| Manpower remains available | Absenteeism or turnover |

As an S&OP analyst, kailangan mong distinguish ang:

- **Assumption:** Something expected to happen.
- **Constraint:** Something that limits the plan.
- **Risk:** Something that may negatively affect the expected result.
- **Mitigation:** An action to reduce or address the risk.

Hindi dapat ituring na guaranteed supply ang isang quantity na nakadepende pa sa unconfirmed supplier delivery.

## 5. Demand-Supply Reconciliation

Ito ang isa sa pinakamahalagang activities sa supply planning.

The objective is to determine whether the available supply can satisfy the expected demand.

### 5.1 Understanding Supply vs. Demand

Let's use a simple example.

| Item | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Customer demand | 10,000 |
| Beginning inventory | 2,000 |
| Planned production | 7,000 |
| Total available supply | 9,000 |

[
Available Supply=BI+Production
]

[
=2,000+7,000=9,000
]

[
Supply-Demand Gap=9,000-10,000
]

[
oxed{-1,000	ext{ units}}
]

The negative result means there is a supply shortage of 1,000 units.

### 5.2 Three Possible Outcomes

| Condition | Meaning | Business implication |
|---|---|---|
| Supply = Demand | Balanced | Demand can be covered |
| Supply greater than Demand | Surplus | Potential excess inventory |
| Supply less than Demand | Shortage | Potential unfulfilled demand |

However, kailangan nating tandaan na ang surplus is not always bad, at ang shortage is not always avoidable.

For example:
- Building inventory ahead of a seasonal peak may be intentional.
- A shortage may be temporarily acceptable if management approves a delivery prioritization strategy.

### 5.3 Backlog vs. Unfulfilled Demand

Magkaiba ang concepts na ito.

**Backlog** refers to customer demand or orders that have not yet been fulfilled and remain outstanding.

**Unfulfilled demand** refers to demand that cannot be satisfied within the required period.

Example:

| Item | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Customer orders | 10,000 |
| Available supply | 8,000 |
| Unfulfilled demand | 2,000 |

If the 2,000 units are confirmed orders that remain open, they become backlog.

But if the 2,000 units represent forecast demand that was never converted into customer orders, they are not automatically backlog.

This distinction is important when reporting customer service and order fulfillment performance.

### 5.4 Prioritizing Customers and Products

Kapag limited ang supply, hindi lahat ng demand ay necessarily ma-kocover.

Possible prioritization criteria:

1. Customer contractual commitments.
2. Customer importance and strategic value.
3. Order due dates.
4. Product criticality.
5. Revenue and profitability.
6. Penalties associated with late delivery.
7. Availability of alternative products.

Example:

May available supply na 800 units.

| Customer | Required quantity | Priority |
|---|---:|---|
| Customer A | 500 | High |
| Customer B | 400 | Medium |
| Customer C | 300 | Low |
| **Total** | **1,200** | |

Kung 800 units lang ang available, kailangan ng management-approved allocation decision.

Hindi automatic na highest revenue customer ang dapat unahin. Dapat kasama ang contractual, customer service, and strategic considerations.

## 6. Scenario Analysis and Supply Alternatives

Kapag hindi feasible ang initial supply plan, hindi ibig sabihin na wala nang solution.

Dito pumapasok ang Topic 4.7: Supply Planning Strategies.

Ang objective ay mag-develop ng alternative scenarios at i-evaluate kung alin ang pinaka-practical at beneficial sa business.

### 6.1 Common Supply Alternatives

| Alternative | How it helps | Potential disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| Overtime | Increases available production hours | Higher labor cost and fatigue |
| Additional shift | Increases operating time | Additional manpower and operating expenses |
| Subcontracting | Transfers part of production externally | Higher cost and quality risks |
| Alternative supplier | Provides additional material source | Qualification and approval requirements |
| Inventory build-ahead | Creates stock before peak demand | Higher inventory holding cost |
| Production rescheduling | Moves production to prioritize critical demand | May delay other products |
| Capacity expansion | Adds long-term production capability | Capital expenditure and implementation time |

### 6.2 Understanding Operational and Financial Trade-offs

Let's compare two possible strategies.

Assume a shortage of 1,000 units.

**Scenario A: Overtime**

- Additional production capacity: 1,000 units.
- Additional cost: ₱50 per unit.
- Total additional cost: ₱50,000.

**Scenario B: Subcontracting**

- External production capacity: 1,000 units.
- Additional cost: ₱80 per unit.
- Total additional cost: ₱80,000.

| Evaluation | Overtime | Subcontracting |
|---|---:|---:|
| Additional supply | 1,000 | 1,000 |
| Additional cost/unit | ₱50 | ₱80 |
| Total additional cost | ₱50,000 | ₱80,000 |
| Capacity relief | Internal | External |
| Additional consideration | Labor availability | Supplier qualification |

Based only on these assumptions, overtime is cheaper.

But what if the internal machine is already running at maximum capacity?

Then overtime may not be physically possible.

In that case, subcontracting may become the more feasible option despite the higher cost.

**Important lesson:** The cheapest option is not necessarily the most feasible option.

### 6.3 Scenario Evaluation Framework

An S&OP analyst should evaluate alternatives using multiple dimensions.

| Evaluation criteria | Question |
|---|---|
| Capacity | Can it close the production gap? |
| Materials | Are required materials available? |
| Timing | Can supply arrive before the deadline? |
| Cost | How much additional cost is required? |
| Inventory | Will it create excess or insufficient inventory? |
| Customer service | Will it improve order fulfillment? |
| Risk | What could prevent the scenario from succeeding? |

The recommended scenario should balance operational feasibility and business objectives.

## 7. Formulas and Calculations

Ito ang quantitative foundation ng topic.

We'll organize the calculations into seven areas.

### 7.1 Net Production Requirements

**Formula:**

[
NPR=D+TEI-BI-PS
]

Example:

- Demand = 10,000 units.
- Target ending inventory = 1,000 units.
- Beginning inventory = 2,000 units.
- Other planned supply = 1,000 units.

[
NPR=10,000+1,000-2,000-1,000
]

[
oxed{NPR=8,000	ext{ units}}
]

Interpretation: 8,000 additional units ang kailangang i-produce, assuming the other planned supply is valid and available within the planning period.

### 7.2 Available Supply

**Formula:**

[
AS=BI+PR+SR
]

Where:

- AS = Available Supply.
- BI = Beginning Inventory.
- PR = Planned Production.
- SR = Scheduled Receipts.

Example:

| Component | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Beginning inventory | 2,000 |
| Planned production | 7,000 |
| Scheduled receipts | 1,000 |

[
AS=2,000+7,000+1,000
]

[
oxed{AS=10,000	ext{ units}}
]

Important: Scheduled receipts must be expected to arrive within the period and be available for use.

### 7.3 Projected Ending Inventory

**Formula:**

[
PEI=BI+PR+SR-D
]

Where PEI = Projected Ending Inventory.

Example:

- Beginning inventory = 2,000.
- Production = 7,000.
- Scheduled receipts = 1,000.
- Demand = 9,000.

[
PEI=2,000+7,000+1,000-9,000
]

[
oxed{PEI=1,000	ext{ units}}
]

Interpretation: After satisfying demand, the company expects to retain 1,000 units.

Note: This simplified calculation assumes no additional inventory adjustments such as scrap, quality holds, transfers, or write-offs.

### 7.4 Supply-Demand Gap

**Formula:**

[
Gap=Available Supply-Demand
]

Example:

- Available supply = 8,500.
- Demand = 10,000.

[
Gap=8,500-10,000
]

[
oxed{Gap=-1,500	ext{ units}}
]

Interpretation: Supply is short by 1,500 units.

A positive result indicates a potential surplus, while a negative result indicates a shortage.

### 7.5 Capacity Feasibility

**Formula:**

[
Capacity Utilization=
rac{Required Capacity}{Available Capacity}
	imes100
]

Example:

- Required production hours = 900.
- Available production hours = 1,000.

Interpretation: The required production consumes 90% of available capacity.

If required hours exceed available hours, the plan is not feasible under the current capacity assumptions.

[

[

]

**Formula:**

[

Beginning Backlog+New Orders-Fulfilled Orders
]

Example:

- Beginning backlog = 500 units.
- New customer orders = 2,000 units.
- Fulfilled orders = 1,800 units.

[

]

[
oxed{Ending Backlog=700	ext{ units}}
]

Interpretation: Outstanding customer orders increased to 700 units.

Important distinction: This formula applies to order backlog, not necessarily to forecast demand that has not become an order.

### Formula Reference

| Calculation | Formula |
|---|---|
| Net production requirement | (D+TEI-BI-PS) |
| Available supply | (BI+PR+SR) |
| Projected ending inventory | (BI+PR+SR-D) |
| Supply-demand gap | (AS-D) |
| Capacity utilization | (RC/AC	imes100) |
| Material requirement | (PQ	imes CR) |
| Material shortage | (MR-AM) |
| Ending backlog | (BB+NO-FO) |

Where RC = Required Capacity, AC = Available Capacity, PQ = Production Quantity, CR = Consumption Rate, MR = Material Requirement, AM = Available Material, BB = Beginning Backlog, NO = New Orders, and FO = Fulfilled Orders.

**Calculation reminder:** These formulas are simplified planning models. In an actual S&OP system, calculations must be time-phased, account for production yield and scrap where relevant, and avoid double-counting inventory or scheduled receipts.

## 8. Practical Manufacturing Application: Building a Feasible Supply Plan

Now, let's apply everything to a realistic manufacturing scenario.

Gagamit tayo ng hypothetical manufacturing company producing electronic components, similar to the type of manufacturing environment you are familiar with.

The purpose is to simulate how an S&OP analyst evaluates a supply plan before presenting it to management.

### 8.1 Business Scenario

ABC Electronics manufactures Product A.

The Sales Department submitted the following forecast:

| Planning period | Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Forecast demand | 1,000 | 1,200 | 1,400 |

The company currently has:

- Beginning finished goods inventory: 200 units.
- Desired ending inventory: 100 units.
- Production capacity: Limited by available machine hours.
- Raw material consumption: 2 kg per finished unit.
- Raw material beginning inventory: 2,000 kg.

Additional information:

| Parameter | Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Normal production capacity | 900 | 1,100 | 1,200 |
| Planned raw material receipts (kg) | 1,800 | 2,200 | 2,400 |
| Raw material consumption (kg/unit) | 2 | 2 | 2 |

Assumptions:

1. Production and demand are measured in finished units.
2. All finished goods produced are assumed saleable.
3. No scrap, quality losses, or other inventory adjustments.
4. All scheduled raw material receipts arrive as planned.
5. No additional finished goods receipts.
6. Production and demand are evaluated within each month.
7. Beginning finished goods inventory is 200 units.

### 8.2 Step 1: Calculate the Required Production

First, determine how much production is needed to meet demand while maintaining 100 units of ending inventory.

[
NPR=D+TEI-BI
]

**Month 1**

[
NPR=1,000+100-200
]

[
=900	ext{ units}
]

**Month 2**

Beginning inventory is now 100 units.

[
NPR=1,200+100-100
]

[
=1,200	ext{ units}
]

**Month 3**

[
NPR=1,400+100-100
]

[
=1,400	ext{ units}
]

Our initial production requirements:

| Month | Demand | Beginning FG | Target Ending FG | Required production |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| M1 | 1,000 | 200 | 100 | 900 |
| M2 | 1,200 | 100 | 100 | 1,200 |
| M3 | 1,400 | 100 | 100 | 1,400 |
| **Total** | **3,600** | | | **3,500** |

The initial production requirement is 3,500 units across three months.

### 8.3 Step 2: Validate Production Capacity

Compare required production with normal production capacity.

| Month | Required production | Available capacity | Capacity gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| M1 | 900 | 900 | 0 |
| M2 | 1,200 | 1,100 | 100 |
| M3 | 1,400 | 1,200 | 200 |
| **Total** | **3,500** | **3,200** | **300** |

Formula:

[
Capacity Gap=Required-Available
]

Interpretation:

- M1: Fully supported by normal capacity.
- M2: Short by 100 units.
- M3: Short by 200 units.

**Production capacity validation**

| Month | Required | Available | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Month 1 | 900 | 900 | Feasible |
| Month 2 | 1,200 | 1,100 | 100 units short |
| Month 3 | 1,400 | 1,200 | 200 units short |

Green = supported by normal capacity. Amber = additional capacity required.

### 8.4 Step 3: Validate Raw Material Availability

Next, check whether the company has enough raw materials.

Material requirement:

[
MR=Production	imes2	ext{ kg}
]

Using the required production quantities:

| Month | Required production | Material requirement |
|---|---:|---:|
| M1 | 900 | 1,800 kg |
| M2 | 1,200 | 2,400 kg |
| M3 | 1,400 | 2,800 kg |
| **Total** | **3,500** | **7,000 kg** |

Now calculate available material.

| Month | Beginning RM | Receipts | Total available | Required RM | Ending RM |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| M1 | 2,000 | 1,800 | 3,800 | 1,800 | 2,000 |
| M2 | 2,000 | 2,200 | 4,200 | 2,400 | 1,800 |
| M3 | 1,800 | 2,400 | 4,200 | 2,800 | 1,400 |

Formula:

[
Ending RM=Beginning RM+Receipts-Consumption
]

Result: Sufficient ang raw materials to support the required production across all three months, based on the stated assumptions.

Notice something important:

**Our production capacity is insufficient, but our raw material supply is sufficient.**

Ibig sabihin, capacity ang current limiting factor, hindi raw material availability.

### 8.5 Step 4: Build the Initial Feasible Supply Plan

Since normal production capacity is limited, let's assume the company decides to produce only within normal capacity.

Calculate projected finished goods inventory.

[
PEI=BI+Production-Demand
]

| Month | Beginning FG | Production | Demand | Projected ending FG |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| M1 | 200 | 900 | 1,000 | 100 |
| M2 | 100 | 1,100 | 1,200 | 0 |
| M3 | 0 | 1,200 | 1,400 | -200 |

A negative projected inventory is a signal of an unmet supply requirement, not physical negative inventory.

Let's translate that into actual operations:

| Month | Available FG + Production | Demand | Unfulfilled demand | Ending FG |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| M1 | 1,100 | 1,000 | 0 | 100 |
| M2 | 1,200 | 1,200 | 0 | 0 |
| M3 | 1,200 | 1,400 | 200 | 0 |

**Supply gap identified**

By Month 3, the company has 200 units of unfulfilled demand.

If these are confirmed orders that remain open, they become backlog.

This is our initial supply plan:

- Month 1: Feasible.
- Month 2: Feasible for current demand, but no ending inventory buffer.
- Month 3: Not feasible for full demand fulfillment.

### 8.6 Step 5: Evaluate Alternative Scenarios

Now, management wants to eliminate the 300-unit cumulative capacity gap.

Let's compare three scenarios.

**Scenario A: Normal Capacity Only**

| Month | Production | Capacity |
|---|---:|---:|
| M1 | 900 | 900 |
| M2 | 1,100 | 1,100 |
| M3 | 1,200 | 1,200 |
| Total | 3,200 | 3,200 |

Result: 200 units of unfulfilled demand by Month 3.

**Scenario B: Overtime**

Assume the company can increase capacity through overtime:

- M2: Additional 100 units.
- M3: Additional 200 units.

Additional cost: ₱50 per unit.

| Month | Normal capacity | Overtime capacity | Revised production |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| M1 | 900 | 0 | 900 |
| M2 | 1,100 | 100 | 1,200 |
| M3 | 1,200 | 200 | 1,400 |
| **Total** | **3,200** | **300** | **3,500** |

Additional cost:

[
300	imes ₱50=oxed{₱15,000}
]

Projected ending inventory:

| Month | Beginning FG | Production | Demand | Ending FG |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| M1 | 200 | 900 | 1,000 | 100 |
| M2 | 100 | 1,200 | 1,200 | 100 |
| M3 | 100 | 1,400 | 1,400 | 100 |

Result: All forecasted demand is fulfilled, with the desired 100 units of ending inventory.

But remember: We still need to verify whether the machines, operators, and material supply can support the additional production.

**Scenario C: Subcontracting**

Assume the company outsources the additional 300 units.

Additional subcontracting cost: ₱80 per unit.

[
300	imes ₱80=oxed{₱24,000}
]

Result: The same production requirement can be supported, assuming the subcontractor has approved capacity, materials, quality capability, and delivery availability.

### 8.7 Step 6: Compare and Select the Recommended Scenario

| Criteria | Scenario A | Scenario B | Scenario C |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Total production | 3,200 | 3,500 | 3,500 |
| Unfulfilled demand | 200 | 0 | 0 |
| Additional cost | ₱0 | ₱15,000 | ₱24,000 |
| Ending inventory M3 | 0 | 100 | 100 |
| Demand fulfillment | Incomplete | Complete | Complete |

**Illustrative recommendation**

**Scenario B: Overtime** is the preferred option under the given assumptions.

Why?

1. It closes the entire 300-unit capacity gap.
2. It maintains the target ending inventory.
3. It eliminates unfulfilled demand.
4. Its incremental cost is lower than subcontracting.
5. The raw material position is sufficient.

However, before recommending this to management, we need to validate:

- Whether overtime is actually available.
- Whether the machines can sustain the increased operating hours.
- Whether qualified manpower is available.
- Whether maintenance requirements are affected.
- Whether the supplier receipts are confirmed.

If any of these assumptions fail, Scenario B may no longer be feasible.

### 8.8 Step 7: Final Recommended Supply Plan

Our final proposed recommendation:

| Planning metric | M1 | M2 | M3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Demand | 1,000 | 1,200 | 1,400 |
| Recommended production | 900 | 1,200 | 1,400 |
| Normal capacity | 900 | 1,100 | 1,200 |
| Additional capacity | 0 | 100 | 200 |
| Ending FG inventory | 100 | 100 | 100 |
| Ending RM inventory (kg) | 2,000 | 1,800 | 1,400 |

**Management recommendation:**

Approve the overtime capacity arrangement for Months 2 and 3, subject to confirmation of manpower, machine availability, and maintenance requirements.

This recommendation supports full forecast fulfillment while maintaining the desired finished goods inventory and avoiding the higher cost of subcontracting.

This is how an S&OP analyst moves from a basic production requirement into a business recommendation supported by data.

## 9. Business Perspective and S&OP Decisions

Dito na natin titingnan kung paano ginagamit ang Feasible Supply Plan sa actual S&OP process, particularly during Supply Review and Pre-S&OP.

### 9.1 Role During Supply Review

Supply Review is where the organization evaluates whether the current supply plan can support the expected demand.

As an S&OP analyst, hindi lang basta magre-report ng production quantities.

Kailangan mong maipakita:

| Information | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Demand forecast | Establish required supply |
| Production plan | Show planned output |
| Available capacity | Identify production limitations |
| Material availability | Identify procurement constraints |
| Inventory projections | Determine future inventory position |
| Supply-demand gap | Identify shortages and surpluses |
| Backlog projection | Understand potential customer impact |
| Scenario alternatives | Present possible solutions |
| Financial impact | Evaluate costs and business implications |

The output should be a supply plan with clearly identified constraints, risks, and recommended actions.

### 9.2 Role During Pre-S&OP

After Supply Review, the organization needs to reconcile supply and demand.

For example:

- Sales wants 3,600 units fulfilled.
- Operations can normally produce 3,200 units.
- Procurement confirms sufficient raw materials.
- Manufacturing proposes overtime.
- Finance evaluates the additional ₱15,000 cost.

The Pre-S&OP team evaluates whether the proposed solution is acceptable and prepares recommendations for executive decision-making.

The important part is that everyone understands the same business situation.

### 9.3 What Should an S&OP Analyst Communicate?

Let's compare two reporting approaches.

**Incorrect reporting:**

"Our production capacity is insufficient in Months 2 and 3."

This identifies the problem but doesn't help management make a decision.

**Better reporting:**

"Our current production capacity is insufficient by 300 units across Months 2 and 3. Without additional capacity, approximately 200 units of Month 3 demand will remain unfulfilled. Raw material availability is sufficient. We recommend utilizing overtime to close the capacity gap at an estimated additional cost of ₱15,000, subject to manpower and machine availability confirmation."

Notice the difference?

The second report includes:

1. The identified problem.
2. The magnitude of the gap.
3. The operational cause.
4. The expected business impact.
5. The recommended solution.
6. The financial implication.
7. The assumptions requiring validation.

This is the kind of communication expected from an S&OP analyst.

### 9.4 Executive Decision-Making

Management must balance several competing objectives.

| Business objective | Possible conflict |
|---|---|
| Maximize customer fulfillment | May require overtime or outsourcing |
| Minimize production cost | May limit available capacity |
| Maintain sufficient inventory | Increases inventory holding cost |
| Maximize machine utilization | May create unnecessary inventory |
| Minimize inventory | Increases risk of shortages |
| Protect profitability | May require prioritizing certain products |

There is no single strategy that always produces the best outcome.

The objective is to identify the most appropriate balance based on company priorities.

### 9.5 What Should Be Presented to Executives?

A good executive supply presentation should contain:

**1. Overall Supply Position**
- Total demand.
- Total planned supply.
- Capacity utilization.
- Projected inventory.

**2. Major Constraints**
- Capacity shortages.
- Material shortages.
- Supplier delivery risks.
- Bottleneck operations.

**3. Business Impact**
- Potential backlog.
- Delayed customer orders.
- Revenue exposure.
- Additional operating costs.

**4. Alternative Scenarios**
- Normal production.
- Overtime.
- Additional shifts.
- Subcontracting.
- Alternative sourcing.

**5. Recommended Decision**
- Proposed action.
- Expected benefits.
- Additional costs.
- Risks and assumptions.
- Required management approval.

The goal is to make management decisions easier by converting operational data into business information.

## 10. Key Takeaways

Let's consolidate everything we've learned.

| Concept | What you should remember |
|---|---|
| Feasible Supply Plan | A supply plan validated against actual business capabilities |
| Proposed Supply Plan | An initial plan that has not necessarily passed feasibility validation |
| Capacity Feasibility | Whether production resources can support required output |
| Material Feasibility | Whether required raw materials are available at the correct time |
| Resource Feasibility | Whether manpower and supporting resources are sufficient |
| Time Feasibility | Whether production and supply can meet required dates |
| Demand-Supply Reconciliation | Comparing expected demand against available supply |
| Supply Gap | Difference between available supply and demand |
| Backlog | Outstanding customer orders that remain unfulfilled |
| Scenario Analysis | Evaluating alternative solutions to supply constraints |
| Supply Review | Evaluating supply capabilities and constraints |
| Pre-S&OP | Reconciling supply and demand and preparing management recommendations |

### Five principles to remember

1. **A production plan is not automatically a feasible supply plan.** It must be validated against all critical resources and constraints.
2. **Capacity is not the only limiting factor.** Materials, manpower, suppliers, inventory, and timing also matter.
3. **Feasibility must be time-phased.** Having enough resources overall does not guarantee availability when needed.
4. **Supply gaps require business decisions.** Overtime, subcontracting, inventory adjustments, and prioritization are possible solutions.
5. **The best supply plan balances operational feasibility and business objectives.** Lowest cost alone does not necessarily mean the best decision.
