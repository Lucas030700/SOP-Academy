# Module 4 – Supply Planning
## Topic 4.4: Resource Planning

**Level:** Beginner to Professional

**Main learning objective:** Matutunan natin kung paano tinutukoy ng isang manufacturing company kung sapat ba ang manpower, machines, materials, equipment, at supporting resources para maisagawa ang production plan.

Sa previous topic, pinag-aralan natin ang **Capacity Planning (4.3)**. Ngayon, mas magiging specific tayo: hindi lang kung gaano karami ang kayang i-produce ng factory, kundi kung mayroon ba itong lahat ng kinakailangang resources para magawa iyon.

---

# 1. Topic Overview

## 1.1 What is Resource Planning?

Ang **Resource Planning** ay proseso ng pagtukoy, pagkalkula, pagsusuri, at paglalaan ng resources na kailangan para maisagawa ang isang production plan.

Sa madaling salita:

> Resource Planning answers the question: "Do we have the right resources, in the right quantity, at the right time, to execute our production plan?"

Halimbawa, may production plan ang isang factory:

- Product A: 10,000 units
- Product B: 8,000 units
- Product C: 5,000 units

Hindi sapat na malaman lang na kailangan nating gumawa ng 23,000 units.

Kailangan din nating malaman:

- Ilang operators ang kailangan?
- Ilang machine-hours ang required?
- Sapat ba ang raw materials?
- May available bang molds, fixtures, at tools?
- Sapat ba ang operating hours?
- May maintenance schedule ba na makakaapekto sa production?
- May available bang trained personnel para sa bawat machine?

Dito pumapasok ang Resource Planning.

## 1.2 Why is Resource Planning Important?

Imagine na may confirmed customer orders ang company at mayroon nang approved production plan.

Pero pagdating sa actual execution:

- Kulang pala ang machine operators.
- May isang machine na under maintenance.
- Hindi sapat ang available raw materials.
- Walang trained personnel para sa specialized process.
- Hindi available ang required production tools.

Kahit technically achievable ang production quantity, hindi pa rin ma-execute ang production plan.

Ito ang pangunahing problemang gustong maiwasan ng Resource Planning.

| Without proper resource planning | With proper resource planning |
|---|---|
| Unexpected manpower shortages | Planned manpower allocation |
| Production delays | Better production execution |
| Excessive overtime | Controlled labor requirements |
| Machine scheduling conflicts | Better equipment utilization |
| Material shortages | Planned procurement |
| Unplanned operating expenses | Better cost control |

**Important:** Ang production plan ay hindi automatic na executable dahil lamang may production target. Kailangan munang ma-verify kung available ang resources.

## 1.3 Why is this included in Module 4?

Ang Module 4 ay tungkol sa Supply Planning.

Sa Supply Planning, hindi lang natin tinutukoy kung gaano karaming products ang kailangang gawin. Kailangan din nating malaman kung paano ito gagawin gamit ang available resources.

Ang relationship ng topics natin:

1. **4.2 Production Planning:** What and how much should we produce?
2. **4.3 Capacity Planning:** Can our production system handle the required production volume?
3. **4.4 Resource Planning:** Do we have the manpower, machines, materials, and supporting resources?
4. **4.5 Lead Time Management:** Can we obtain and deliver the required resources and products within the required time?

Magkakaugnay ang apat na topics na ito, pero magkakaiba ang focus.

# 2. Layman's Explanation

## 2.1 Resource Planning Through a Simple Example

Imagine na magluluto ka ng pagkain para sa 50 people.

Mayroon kang menu:

- 50 servings of fried rice
- 50 servings of chicken
- 50 servings of vegetables

Alam mo na kung ano ang lulutuin at ilang servings ang kailangan.

Pero bago ka magsimula, kailangan mong malaman kung mayroon kang:

| Resource | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Manpower | 3 cooks and 2 assistants |
| Equipment | 2 rice cookers, 3 frying pans |
| Materials | Rice, chicken, vegetables, oil |
| Tools | Knives, chopping boards, utensils |
| Time | 4 hours preparation and cooking |

Ngayon, imagine na 2 cooks lang pala ang available.

Or kaya naman 50 servings ang kailangan pero 30 servings lang ang available na chicken.

Mayroon kang production plan, pero may resource constraints.

Kailangan mong mag-adjust:

- Magdagdag ng manpower.
- Gumamit ng additional equipment.
- Bumili ng additional ingredients.
- Mag-extend ng preparation time.

Ganyan din ang Resource Planning sa manufacturing, pero mas complicated dahil maraming products, production lines, machines, materials, employees, at production schedules ang kailangang i-coordinate.

## 2.2 The Three Fundamental Questions

Kapag Resource Planning ang pinag-uusapan, tandaan ang tatlong tanong:

**1. What do we need?**

Anong resources ang kailangan para sa production plan?

**2. How much do we need?**

Ilang operators, machine-hours, kilograms of materials, at operating hours ang required?

**3. Do we have enough?**

Sapat ba ang available resources para ma-execute ang production plan?

Ang pinakaimportanteng concept dito ay ang comparison between:

- Resource Requirements
- Resource Availability

Kapag mas mataas ang requirements kaysa availability, mayroon tayong resource shortage.

Kapag mas mababa ang requirements kaysa availability, mayroon tayong resource surplus.

# 3. Technical Explanation

## 3.1 Resource Planning vs. Capacity Planning vs. Production Planning

Ito ang isa sa pinakamahalagang distinctions sa topic na ito.

Magkakaugnay sila, pero hindi sila interchangeable.

| Planning Type | Main Question | Primary Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Production Planning | What and how much should we produce? | Production quantities and timing |
| Capacity Planning | Can our production system handle the required volume? | Available production capacity |
| Resource Planning | What resources are required, and are they available? | Manpower, machines, materials, tools, and other resources |

Let's use one manufacturing example.

A factory needs to produce 10,000 units of Product A.

**Production Planning**

Determines that 10,000 units must be produced during November.

**Capacity Planning**

Determines whether the production line has sufficient capacity to manufacture 10,000 units within November.

**Resource Planning**

Determines whether the factory has enough:

- Operators to run the production line.
- Machine-hours to manufacture the products.
- Raw materials to complete production.
- Tools and equipment to support operations.

### Important distinction

Capacity Planning often evaluates the productive capability of a system, while Resource Planning examines the individual resources required to support that capability.

For example:

A machine may have 200 available operating hours, but only 120 hours of qualified operator coverage.

The machine capacity may be sufficient, but the manpower resource is insufficient.

This is why capacity and resources must be evaluated together.

## 3.2 Resource Planning in the S&OP Process

Sa S&OP, Resource Planning helps determine whether the proposed supply plan is operationally feasible.

Consider this simplified relationship:

**Demand Plan → Production / Supply Plan → Resource Planning → Feasibility Assessment**

Resource Planning calculates manpower, machine, material, and other resource requirements. If resources are sufficient, the production plan is resource-feasible. If a shortage exists, corrective action or management decisions are required.

The resource evaluation provides information to different departments:

- **Production:** Can we execute the production schedule?
- **HR:** Do we have sufficient manpower and required skills?
- **Maintenance:** Are machines and equipment available?
- **Procurement:** Can suppliers provide the required materials?
- **Finance:** What additional costs will be incurred?
- **Sales:** Are customer commitments achievable?

Resource Planning therefore supports cross-functional decision-making, which is one of the central purposes of S&OP.

## 3.3 Types of Production Resources

Sa manufacturing, maraming klase ng resources ang kailangan para makagawa ng finished goods.

Hindi lang manpower at machines ang resources. Kasama rin ang materials, tools, utilities, at time.

### A. Manpower and Labor Skills

Ito ang human resources na directly or indirectly involved sa production.

Examples:

- Machine operators
- Production technicians
- Quality inspectors
- Material handlers
- Maintenance technicians
- Production supervisors
- Warehouse personnel

**Important concept: Headcount vs. Available Labor Hours**

Magkaiba ang number of employees at actual available working hours.

Example:

May 10 production operators ang isang department.

Hindi ibig sabihin nito na available silang lahat for 8 hours every day.

Possible reasons:

- Absences
- Training
- Meetings
- Breaks
- Leave of absence
- Assignment to other production activities

Kaya sa Resource Planning, mas useful ang pag-measure ng available labor in terms of **labor-hours**.

Another important consideration is skill availability.

Halimbawa, may 10 operators pero 3 lang ang trained to operate a specialized machine.

Kahit marami ang employees, maaaring manpower-constrained pa rin ang specific production process.

### B. Machines and Production Equipment

Ito ang physical equipment na ginagamit para maisagawa ang production activities.

Examples:

- Mixing machines
- Casting machines
- Cutting machines
- Pressing machines
- Firing furnaces
- Assembly equipment
- Inspection machines

Ang machine resources ay commonly measured using **machine-hours**.

Example:

A production machine operates for 8 hours per day, 20 days per month.

8 × 20 = 160 machine-hours

Pero hindi automatic na 160 hours ang effective availability.

Kailangan ding i-consider ang:

- Planned maintenance
- Unplanned breakdowns
- Setup and changeover
- Equipment cleaning
- Machine downtime

### C. Raw Materials and Components

Ito ang physical materials na consumed during production.

Examples:

- Raw materials
- Chemicals
- Powder materials
- Plastic resins
- Packaging materials
- Electronic components
- Semi-finished components

Common units of measurement:

| Material | Unit |
|---|---|
| Powder | kg |
| Liquid chemicals | Liters |
| Metal sheets | kg or sheets |
| Electronic components | Pieces |
| Packaging | Boxes or pieces |
| Film | Meters |

Sa material planning, mahalagang maintindihan ang **Bill of Materials (BOM)**.

Ang BOM ay listahan ng materials at corresponding quantities na kailangan para makagawa ng isang unit ng finished product.

Example:

To produce one unit of Product A:

| Material | Required Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Material X | 0.20 kg |
| Material Y | 0.10 kg |
| Material Z | 0.05 kg |

Kung 1,000 units ang production quantity, kailangan natin ng:

- Material X = 200 kg
- Material Y = 100 kg
- Material Z = 50 kg

Dito natin makikita kung paano nagiging material requirements ang production quantities.

### D. Tools and Supporting Resources

Hindi lahat ng resources ay directly consumed during production.

May mga resources na ginagamit para masuportahan ang production process.

Examples:

- Molds
- Dies
- Jigs and fixtures
- Cutting tools
- Inspection gauges
- Measuring instruments
- Spare parts
- Material handling equipment

Halimbawa, may available na production machine pero kulang ang required molds.

Hindi pa rin makakapag-produce ang machine kahit technically available ito.

This is an example of a supporting resource constraint.

### E. Utilities and Infrastructure

May mga manufacturing processes na dependent sa supporting utilities.

Examples:

- Electricity
- Compressed air
- Water
- Cooling systems
- Steam
- Nitrogen
- HVAC systems

Halimbawa, may available na firing furnace pero insufficient ang electrical power supply para mapatakbo ito at required operating conditions.

Therefore, utility availability must also be considered.

### F. Working Hours, Shifts, and Resource Availability

Ang operating schedule ay may malaking impact sa available resources.

Common production arrangements:

| Schedule | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 shift | One operating shift per day |
| 2 shifts | Two operating shifts per day |
| 3 shifts | Three operating shifts per day |
| 24/7 operation | Continuous operation, subject to maintenance and staffing arrangements |

Example:

A factory operates:

- 2 shifts per day
- 8 hours per shift
- 20 working days per month

Theoretical operating time:

2 × 8 × 20 = 320 hours

Pero kung may maintenance at downtime, mas mababa ang effective available hours.

**Remember:** Ang schedule ng factory ay hindi automatically equal sa actual resource availability.

# 4. Detailed Discussion

## 4.1 Resource Requirements

Ngayon, alam na natin kung ano ang different types of resources.

Next question:

Paano natin malalaman kung gaano karaming resources ang kailangan para makagawa ng specific production quantity?

Ang process na ito ay tinatawag na **Resource Requirements Calculation**.

The basic principle:

> Production Quantity × Resource Consumption Rate = Required Resources

Example:

A company needs to produce 5,000 units of Product A.

Each unit requires:

- 0.05 labor-hours
- 0.04 machine-hours
- 0.20 kg of raw materials

Therefore:

| Resource | Consumption Rate | Production Quantity | Requirement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Labor | 0.05 hr/unit | 5,000 | 250 labor-hours |
| Machine | 0.04 hr/unit | 5,000 | 200 machine-hours |
| Material | 0.20 kg/unit | 5,000 | 1,000 kg |

This is the foundation of quantitative Resource Planning.

### Understanding Resource Consumption Rates

Ang Resource Consumption Rate ay nagsasabi kung gaano karaming resources ang ginagamit para makagawa ng isang unit of product.

For example:

**Labor consumption rate**

0.05 labor-hours per unit.

Ibig sabihin, bawat unit ay nangangailangan ng 0.05 hour of labor.

**Machine consumption rate**

0.04 machine-hours per unit.

Ibig sabihin, bawat unit ay gumagamit ng 0.04 hour of machine operating time.

**Material consumption rate**

0.20 kg per unit.

Ibig sabihin, bawat unit ay nangangailangan ng 0.20 kg of raw material.

These rates may come from:

- Standard production times
- Historical production data
- Engineering specifications
- Routing data
- Bill of Materials
- Industrial engineering studies

**Important distinction:** Ang standard consumption rate ay planning assumption. Sa actual production, maaaring magkaiba ang consumption dahil sa scrap, rework, inefficiencies, at actual operating conditions.

## 4.2 Factors Affecting Resource Availability

Hindi permanent ang resource availability. Maaari itong magbago depende sa operational conditions.

| Factor | Impact on Resources |
|---|---|
| Absenteeism | Reduces available manpower |
| Machine breakdown | Reduces machine-hours |
| Preventive maintenance | Reduces scheduled operating time |
| Setup and changeover | Consumes production time |
| Supplier delays | Reduces available materials |
| Training | Reduces available productive labor hours |
| Quality problems | Increases material and labor consumption |
| Utility interruptions | Restricts equipment operation |
| Scrap and rework | Requires additional resources |

### Example: Absenteeism

May 10 operators ang isang department.

Each operator is scheduled to work 8 hours.

Theoretical labor availability:

10 × 8 = 80 labor-hours

Pero 2 operators ang absent.

Actual scheduled availability:

8 × 8 = 64 labor-hours

Nabawasan ng 16 labor-hours ang manpower availability.

At kung specialized ang skills ng absent operators, maaaring mas malaki pa ang operational impact.

### Example: Machine Maintenance

A machine is scheduled to operate for 160 hours per month.

Planned maintenance requires 12 hours.

Expected breakdown downtime is estimated at 8 hours.

Effective available machine time:

160 − 12 − 8 = 140 hours

Ang 140 hours ang mas realistic na planning availability, assuming na hindi pa kasama ang other losses.

## 4.3 Resource Availability vs. Resource Requirements

Ito ang pinakaimportanteng comparison sa Resource Planning.

Dalawang bagay ang kailangan nating malaman:

**Resource Requirements:** Gaano karaming resources ang kailangan?

**Resource Availability:** Gaano karaming resources ang available?

Then we compare them.

| Condition | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Requirements = Availability | Fully utilized resource |
| Requirements less than Availability | Resource surplus |
| Requirements greater than Availability | Resource shortage |

Example:

A production line requires 500 machine-hours.

Available machine-hours are 450.

500 − 450 = 50

**50 machine-hour shortage**

Meaning, hindi kayang i-execute ng available machine resource ang entire production plan under the current assumptions.

Possible responses:

- Overtime
- Additional shifts
- Additional equipment
- Production rescheduling
- Outsourcing

Hindi pa natin agad binabago ang production quantity. First, kailangan nating malaman kung anong resource ang constrained at kung anong options ang available.

## 4.4 Resource Allocation

Ang Resource Allocation ay proseso ng pag-assign ng available resources sa different production activities.

Imagine na mayroon kang:

- Product A: 5,000 units
- Product B: 3,000 units
- Product C: 2,000 units

Pero limited ang available machine-hours.

Hindi automatic na lahat ng products ay makakakuha ng required machine time.

Kailangan nating magdesisyon kung paano ipamamahagi ang resources.

### Common Resource Allocation Considerations

**1. Customer Priority**

May products na kailangan unahin dahil sa customer commitments.

**2. Delivery Requirements**

Products with earlier due dates may receive higher priority.

**3. Product Profitability**

In some business situations, management may consider contribution margin or profitability.

**4. Resource Compatibility**

Hindi lahat ng machines at operators ay qualified para sa lahat ng products.

**5. Production Efficiency**

Sometimes, grouping similar products can reduce setup and changeover time.

**6. Material Availability**

Walang sense na mag-allocate ng machine time sa product na wala pang available materials.

### Example: Competing Products

A factory has only 100 available machine-hours.

| Product | Required Machine-Hours |
|---|---:|
| Product A | 60 |
| Product B | 50 |
| Product C | 40 |
| **Total** | **150** |

Shortage:

150 − 100 = 50 hours

Hindi natin kayang gawin ang lahat ng planned production using the current machine availability.

Possible allocation:

| Product | Planned Hours | Allocated Hours | Uncovered Hours |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 60 | 60 | 0 |
| B | 50 | 30 | 20 |
| C | 40 | 10 | 30 |
| **Total** | **150** | **100** | **50** |

Illustrative allocation lamang ito, hindi automatic na best decision.

Kailangan pang suriin ang customer demand, due dates, inventory position, and business priorities.

**Professional insight:** Resource allocation is not simply distributing hours. It is deciding which business requirements should receive priority when resources are limited.

## 4.5 Resource Shortage vs. Resource Surplus

### Resource Shortage

Nangyayari kapag mas mataas ang resource requirements kaysa available resources.

Examples:

- 600 labor-hours required vs. 500 available.
- 300 machine-hours required vs. 250 available.
- 5,000 kg material required vs. 4,000 kg available.

Potential consequences:

- Production delays
- Missed delivery commitments
- Increased overtime expenses
- Higher procurement costs
- Production schedule changes

### Resource Surplus

Nangyayari kapag mas mataas ang available resources kaysa required resources.

Examples:

- 500 labor-hours required vs. 650 available.
- 200 machine-hours required vs. 300 available.
- 5,000 kg material required vs. 7,000 kg available.

Potential consequences:

- Underutilized manpower
- Idle machines
- Excess inventory
- Higher holding costs
- Unnecessary operating expenses

Important: Hindi lahat ng surplus ay necessarily bad.

May companies na intentionally maintain excess capacity or safety stocks to absorb unexpected demand and supply disruptions.

Ang objective ay hindi palaging 100% utilization. Ang objective ay appropriate resource availability at an acceptable cost and service level.

# 5. Practical Examples and Applications

Ngayon, pagsasamahin natin ang mga concepts sa isang realistic manufacturing scenario.

## 5.1 Manufacturing Scenario: Three-Product Production Plan

Imagine na ang isang manufacturing company ay gumagawa ng three different products.

For November, ito ang approved production plan:

| Product | Planned Production |
|---|---:|
| Product A | 4,000 units |
| Product B | 3,000 units |
| Product C | 2,000 units |
| **Total** | **9,000 units** |

The factory needs to evaluate whether it has sufficient manpower, machines, and raw materials.

### Production Resource Consumption Standards

Assume na ito ang established resource consumption rates:

| Product | Labor-Hours / Unit | Machine-Hours / Unit | Material / Unit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 0.05 | 0.04 | 0.20 kg |
| B | 0.08 | 0.06 | 0.30 kg |
| C | 0.06 | 0.05 | 0.25 kg |

These are illustrative engineering standards for this exercise.

### Available Resources

The company operates under the following conditions:

| Resource | Availability Assumptions |
|---|---|
| Manpower | 5 qualified operators |
| Working hours | 8 hours/day |
| Working days | 20 days |
| Labor availability factor | 75% |
| Machines | 3 compatible machines |
| Machine schedule | 8 hours/day |
| Working days | 20 days |
| Machine availability factor | 90% |
| Raw material | 2,400 kg |

Assume all three products can use the same machine pool and that all calculated labor-hours can be covered by the qualified operator pool.

These assumptions are important because, in real manufacturing, resource compatibility and skill requirements may be more complicated.

## 5.2 Calculate Required Labor-Hours

Formula:

Required Labor-Hours = Q × LH

Where:

- Q = Production quantity (units)
- LH = Labor-hours per unit

Product A:

4,000 × 0.05 = 200

Product B:

3,000 × 0.08 = 240

Product C:

2,000 × 0.06 = 120

Total:

200 + 240 + 120 = 560

**Total required labor-hours: 560 hours**

For all three products combined.

## 5.3 Calculate Available Labor-Hours

Formula:

Available Labor-Hours = N × H × D × A

Where:

- N = Number of qualified operators
- H = Working hours per day
- D = Working days
- A = Labor availability factor

Substitute:

5 × 8 × 20 × 0.75

= 600 labor-hours

Comparison:

| Description | Labor-Hours |
|---|---:|
| Available | 600 |
| Required | 560 |
| Surplus | 40 |

Therefore, sufficient ang available manpower under our assumptions.

## 5.4 Calculate Required Machine-Hours

Formula:

Required Machine-Hours = Q × MH

Where:

- Q = Production quantity
- MH = Machine-hours per unit

Product A:

4,000 × 0.04 = 160

Product B:

3,000 × 0.06 = 180

Product C:

2,000 × 0.05 = 100

Total:

160 + 180 + 100 = 440

**Total required machine-hours: 440 hours**

## 5.5 Calculate Available Machine-Hours

Formula:

Available Machine-Hours = M × H × D × A

Where:

- M = Number of machines
- H = Operating hours per day
- D = Working days
- A = Machine availability factor

Substitute:

3 × 8 × 20 × 0.90

= 432 machine-hours

Comparison:

| Description | Machine-Hours |
|---|---:|
| Available | 432 |
| Required | 440 |
| Shortage | 8 |

**Machine resource constraint identified: 8 machine-hour shortage.**

The production plan requires 440 machine-hours, but only 432 machine-hours are available.

May shortage tayo na 8 machine-hours.

## 5.6 Calculate Required Material

Formula:

Material Requirement = Q × MR

Where:

- Q = Production quantity
- MR = Material requirement per unit

Product A:

4,000 × 0.20 = 800 kg

Product B:

3,000 × 0.30 = 900 kg

Product C:

2,000 × 0.25 = 500 kg

Total:

800 + 900 + 500 = 2,200 kg

Available material = 2,400 kg.

Therefore:

2,400 − 2,200 = 200 kg

May 200 kg material surplus tayo.

Assumption: Ang consumption rates ay already inclusive of the applicable expected material losses. Kung hindi, kailangan pang idagdag ang expected scrap or yield loss.

## 5.7 Consolidated Resource Evaluation

Ito na ang consolidated resource assessment.

| Resource | Required | Available | Shortage / Surplus |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Labor-hours | 560 | 600 | +40 surplus |
| Machine-hours | 440 | 432 | -8 shortage |
| Material | 2,200 kg | 2,400 kg | +200 kg surplus |

Interpretation:

- Manpower: Sufficient.
- Machines: Insufficient by 8 hours.
- Raw materials: Sufficient.

**Overall conclusion:** The production plan is not yet fully resource-feasible because machine-hours are insufficient.

Ito ang value ng Resource Planning. Hindi sapat na i-check ang isang resource. Kailangan nating i-evaluate ang lahat ng critical resources.

# 6. Formulas and Calculations

Let's consolidate the important formulas para maging familiar ka sa calculations na ginagamit ng S&OP analysts.

## 6.1 Required Labor-Hours

LH_R = Q × LH_U

Where:

- LH_R = Required labor-hours
- Q = Production quantity (units)
- LH_U = Labor-hours per unit

**Purpose:** To determine how many working hours are needed from production personnel.

Example:

- Production quantity = 5,000 units
- Labor requirement = 0.08 hour/unit

5,000 × 0.08 = 400 hours

Interpretation: Kailangan ng 400 labor-hours para matapos ang production requirement.

## 6.2 Required Machine-Hours

MH_R = Q × MH_U

Where:

- MH_R = Required machine-hours
- Q = Production quantity
- MH_U = Machine-hours per unit

Example:

5,000 × 0.06 = 300 machine-hours

Interpretation: Kailangan ng 300 hours of machine operation.

## 6.3 Required Material

MR = Q × MC

Where:

- MR = Total material requirement
- Q = Production quantity
- MC = Material consumption per unit

Example:

5,000 × 0.25 = 1,250 kg

Interpretation: Kailangan ng 1,250 kg of raw materials.

## 6.4 Available Labor-Hours

LH_A = N × H × D × A

Where:

- LH_A = Available labor-hours
- N = Number of qualified operators
- H = Working hours per day
- D = Working days
- A = Labor availability factor

Example:

5 operators, 8 hours/day, 20 days, and 75% availability:

5 × 8 × 20 × 0.75 = 600 hours

**Important:** The availability factor accounts for expected nonproductive or unavailable time based on the company's planning assumptions. Make sure hindi na nadeduct separately ang downtime or breaks na included na sa factor.

## 6.5 Available Machine-Hours

MH_A = M × H × D × A

Where:

- MH_A = Available machine-hours
- M = Number of machines
- H = Operating hours per day
- D = Working days
- A = Machine availability factor

Example:

3 machines, 8 hours/day, 20 days, and 90% availability:

3 × 8 × 20 × 0.90 = 432 hours

## 6.6 Resource Utilization

Resource utilization measures how much of the available resource capacity is consumed by the production requirements.

Formula:

Utilization = (Required Resources ÷ Available Resources) × 100

Using our manufacturing example:

**Labor utilization:**

(560 ÷ 600) × 100 = 93.33%

**Machine utilization:**

(440 ÷ 432) × 100 = 101.85%

**Material utilization:**

(2,200 ÷ 2,400) × 100 = 91.67%

### How to interpret utilization

| Utilization | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Below 100% | Available resources exceed requirements |
| Equal to 100% | Requirements equal availability |
| Above 100% | Requirements exceed available resources |

Important professional consideration:

A 95% utilization rate does not automatically mean efficient operations.

Very high utilization can reduce operational flexibility, increase queues, and leave little room for unexpected breakdowns.

Also, utilization should be calculated using comparable units and the correct resource pool. Huwag pagsamahin ang different machine types or labor skills into one utilization figure if they are not interchangeable.

## 6.7 Resource Shortage or Surplus

Formula:

Resource Balance = Available − Required

Interpretation:

- Positive result = Surplus
- Negative result = Shortage
- Zero = Exact balance

Using our example:

**Labor:**

600 − 560 = +40 hours

**Machines:**

432 − 440 = −8 hours

**Materials:**

2,400 − 2,200 = +200 kg

This formula is particularly useful when creating a resource planning summary in Excel.

### Quick Formula Reference

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| Required labor | Quantity × Labor-hours/unit |
| Required machine time | Quantity × Machine-hours/unit |
| Material requirement | Quantity × Material/unit |
| Available labor | Operators × Hours × Days × Availability |
| Available machines | Machines × Hours × Days × Availability |
| Utilization | Required ÷ Available × 100 |
| Resource balance | Available − Required |

# 7. Practical Manufacturing Application: From Resource Analysis to Production Feasibility

Balikan natin ang manufacturing scenario.

We already established that:

- Labor has 40 hours of surplus.
- Machines have an 8-hour shortage.
- Materials have a 200 kg surplus.

Now, paano gagamitin ng S&OP analyst ang information na ito?

## 7.1 Identify the Constrained Resource

Ang constrained resource ay resource na naglilimita sa ability ng company to execute its production plan.

Sa example natin, machine-hours ang constraint.

Why?

Because:

- Manpower is sufficient.
- Materials are sufficient.
- Machine-hours are insufficient.

Kahit may available na operators at raw materials, hindi nila matatapos ang production plan without additional machine availability.

## 7.2 Evaluate the Available Options

Suppose may options ang management:

| Option | Operational Impact | Financial Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Overtime | Adds machine operating time, subject to staffing and equipment limits | Additional overtime costs |
| Additional shift | Increases operating availability | Additional shift premiums and utilities |
| Equipment sharing | Uses machines from another production line | Transfer and setup costs |
| Production rescheduling | Moves production to another available period | Possible delivery delays |
| Outsourcing | Transfers some production to an external supplier | Outsourcing and logistics costs |
| Additional equipment | Adds long-term resource availability | Capital expenditure |

Hindi ibig sabihin na lahat ng options ay applicable sa bawat situation.

For example, kung machine ang constraint, hindi sapat ang pagdagdag ng manpower lamang.

## 7.3 Example: Using Overtime to Resolve the Machine Shortage

Assume that management allows additional machine operation through overtime.

Required additional machine-hours:

440 − 432 = 8 hours

Assume overtime operating cost is ₱500 per machine-hour.

Additional cost:

8 × ₱500 = ₱4,000

Therefore:

- Additional machine-hours required = 8 hours.
- Additional overtime cost = ₱4,000.

If overtime can be scheduled and the required operators and utilities are available, the production plan can become resource-feasible.

Pero hindi pa automatic na approved ang overtime.

Kailangan ding i-consider:

- Operator availability.
- Maintenance requirements.
- Equipment condition.
- Safety restrictions.
- Product delivery deadlines.
- Financial approval.

## 7.4 Resource Planning Is Not Just About Adding Resources

Consider another situation.

Suppose may 100 hours of machine shortage.

Management immediately proposes buying another machine.

Pero after investigation, nalaman na:

- 30 hours are lost due to unnecessary changeovers.
- 25 hours are lost due to avoidable downtime.
- 20 hours are lost due to poor scheduling.
- 25 hours are genuinely caused by insufficient machine availability.

In this situation, purchasing additional equipment may not be the first or most economical solution.

Operational improvement may recover some of the lost hours.

This is where your manufacturing engineering background becomes useful: resource planning can connect production data, process efficiency, downtime analysis, and business decisions.

# 8. Resource Planning Decisions

Kapag may resource shortage or surplus, kailangan ng management na pumili ng appropriate response.

Let's discuss the common strategies.

## 8.1 Manpower Reassignment

Paglilipat ng available employees from one department or production line to another.

Example:

Line A has 3 idle operators, while Line B has a manpower shortage.

Management may reassign qualified operators from Line A to Line B.

**Benefits:**
- Uses existing workforce.
- May avoid additional hiring.
- Can address temporary shortages.

**Risks:**
- May create shortages in the original department.
- Employees may lack required qualifications.
- Can disrupt existing production schedules.

## 8.2 Overtime

Extending employees' working hours beyond their normal schedules, subject to applicable labor rules and company policies.

**Benefits:**
- Fast response to temporary resource shortages.
- Does not necessarily require permanent hiring.
- Useful for short-term demand increases.

**Risks:**
- Higher labor costs.
- Fatigue and safety concerns.
- Possible reduction in productivity.
- Not sustainable for long-term shortages.

## 8.3 Additional Shifts

Adding another production shift to increase available operating time.

Example:

From 2 shifts per day to 3 shifts per day.

Theoretical machine operating hours increase from:

2 × 8 = 16 hours/day

To:

3 × 8 = 24 hours/day

However, this assumes that the equipment is designed for continuous operation and that maintenance, staffing, and utility constraints are properly managed.

**Benefits:**
- Increases available operating time.
- Can support sustained higher demand.

**Risks:**
- Additional labor and utility expenses.
- Additional maintenance requirements.
- Shift staffing challenges.

## 8.4 Cross-Training

Training employees to perform additional production activities.

Example:

An operator originally assigned to Machine A is also trained to operate Machine B.

**Benefits:**
- Improves workforce flexibility.
- Reduces dependence on specific employees.
- Helps address absenteeism-related shortages.

**Risks:**
- Training requires time and resources.
- Productivity may initially decrease.
- Not all tasks can be cross-trained due to qualification requirements.

## 8.5 Equipment Sharing

Using equipment from another department or production line.

**Benefits:**
- Improves utilization of existing equipment.
- May avoid equipment purchases.
- Can address temporary machine shortages.

**Risks:**
- Scheduling conflicts.
- Equipment compatibility issues.
- Additional setup and transportation time.

## 8.6 Alternative Materials

Using approved substitute materials when the original material is unavailable.

Example:

A supplier cannot provide the required raw material on time.

The company evaluates an alternative material with equivalent technical and quality requirements.

**Benefits:**
- Can prevent production interruptions.
- Reduces dependence on a single supplier.

**Risks:**
- Requires engineering and quality approval.
- May require customer approval.
- Could affect product performance.
- May require additional testing or qualification.

Important: Hindi puwedeng basta palitan ang material dahil lamang may available na alternative. Kailangan munang ma-verify ang technical and quality requirements.

## 8.7 Procurement Adjustments

Adjusting purchasing quantities and delivery schedules based on production requirements.

Example:

Required material = 2,200 kg.

Available material = 1,800 kg.

Shortage:

2,200 − 1,800 = 400 kg

Possible procurement actions:

- Expedite existing purchase orders.
- Request partial deliveries.
- Increase purchase quantities.
- Identify alternative suppliers.
- Negotiate delivery schedules.

This connects Resource Planning with procurement and supplier management.

# 9. Business Perspective and Decision-Making

## 9.1 How Does an S&OP Analyst Use Resource Planning?

Ang S&OP analyst ay hindi necessarily ang taong direktang nag-a-assign ng operators or nagpapatakbo ng machines.

Instead, the analyst consolidates information from different departments and evaluates whether the supply plan is achievable.

Typical responsibilities include:

1. Obtain the approved production plan.
2. Gather resource consumption standards.
3. Calculate required resources.
4. Obtain available resource information.
5. Compare requirements against availability.
6. Identify resource constraints.
7. Evaluate potential corrective actions.
8. Present the findings to relevant stakeholders.

## 9.2 Cross-Functional Decision-Making

Imagine na may resource shortage.

Hindi dapat Production Department lang ang magdedesisyon.

| Department | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Production | Confirms production requirements and scheduling options |
| Engineering | Evaluates process changes and technical alternatives |
| HR | Evaluates manpower availability and recruitment |
| Maintenance | Confirms machine availability and maintenance schedules |
| Procurement | Evaluates material availability and supplier commitments |
| Finance | Evaluates additional operating costs |
| Sales | Confirms customer priorities and delivery commitments |

Ang goal ng S&OP ay magkaroon ng aligned decision across departments.

## 9.3 Impact on Business Performance

| Business Area | Impact of Resource Planning |
|---|---|
| Demand | Helps determine whether demand can be fulfilled |
| Supply | Determines whether the supply plan is executable |
| Inventory | Prevents shortages and unnecessary accumulation |
| Production | Improves production schedule feasibility |
| Customer Service | Helps protect delivery commitments |
| Cost | Helps control overtime, procurement, and operating costs |
| Capacity | Identifies resource-related production constraints |
| Financial Performance | Supports cost-effective supply decisions |

## 9.4 What Should an S&OP Analyst Recommend?

Balikan natin ang scenario natin.

**Resource Assessment**

| Resource | Status |
|---|---|
| Manpower | Sufficient |
| Machines | Short by 8 hours |
| Materials | Sufficient |

**Recommended initial action**

Evaluate whether the 8-hour machine shortage can be addressed through overtime, schedule adjustment, or equipment sharing.

Before recommending the final solution, confirm the associated cost, availability of qualified operators, equipment condition, and delivery impact.

This is an example of moving from resource calculation to business recommendation.

Hindi sapat na sabihin:

"Machine-hours are insufficient."

A more professional recommendation is:

"The current production plan requires 440 machine-hours against 432 available machine-hours, resulting in an 8-hour shortage. Overtime or equipment sharing should be evaluated to close the gap while maintaining the planned production quantities and customer delivery commitments."

That is the type of analytical communication expected from an S&OP analyst.

# 10. Connection to the Next Topic: Lead Time Management (4.5)

May isang importanteng distinction na kailangan nating maintindihan bago tayo mag-move forward.

**Resource availability does not automatically mean resource availability at the required time.**

Example:

May available na raw material supplier.

Pero ang delivery lead time ay 30 days.

If production needs the material in 10 days, hindi pa rin ito available when required.

Similarly:

- May available na machine pero scheduled maintenance ito next week.
- May available na operator pero naka-assign siya sa ibang production line.
- May available na alternative material pero kailangan pa ng 2 weeks for qualification.

Dito papasok ang Lead Time Management.

| Topic | Main Question |
|---|---|
| Resource Planning (4.4) | What resources do we need, and are they available? |
| Lead Time Management (4.5) | When will the required resources or products become available? |

Resource Planning identifies what is needed and whether sufficient resources exist.

Lead Time Management evaluates timing, delays, and the time required to obtain or produce those resources.

Magkaugnay sila dahil ang resource availability ay kailangang naka-align sa production schedule.

# 11. Key Takeaways

Ito ang mga concepts na gusto kong ma-retain mo from this lesson.

### A. Fundamental Concepts

1. **Resource Planning** determines the resources needed to execute the production plan.
2. Production Planning determines what and how much to produce.
3. Capacity Planning evaluates whether the production system can handle the required production volume.
4. Resource Planning evaluates the manpower, machines, materials, tools, and other resources needed.
5. A production plan is not necessarily executable just because production capacity appears sufficient.

### B. Technical Concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Labor-Hours | Total working hours required from personnel |
| Machine-Hours | Total equipment operating hours required |
| Material Consumption Rate | Material quantity needed per unit |
| Resource Availability | Resources accessible for production |
| Resource Requirements | Resources needed to execute production |
| Resource Allocation | Assignment of resources to activities |
| Resource Utilization | Percentage of available resources being consumed |
| Resource Constraint | Resource that limits production execution |
| Cross-Training | Training employees to perform additional tasks |

### C. Most Important Formulas

Required Resources = Q × Consumption Rate

Resource Availability = Number of Resources × Available Time

Utilization = (Required ÷ Available) × 100

Resource Balance = Available − Required

### D. Professional Mindset

As an S&OP analyst, hindi sapat na marunong ka lang mag-compute.

You should be able to:

- Identify which resources are constrained.
- Understand why the shortage exists.
- Determine the operational consequences.
- Evaluate possible solutions.
- Estimate the financial implications.
- Recommend actions that support customer demand and business objectives.
