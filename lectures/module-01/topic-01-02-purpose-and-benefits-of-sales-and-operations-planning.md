# Module 1 – S&OP Fundamentals
## Topic 1.2: Purpose and Benefits of Sales & Operations Planning (S&OP)

**Central question of this lesson:**

Why do companies need S&OP, and how does it help a business operate more efficiently, satisfy customers, control costs, and achieve its financial objectives?

Sa Topic 1.1, we established that S&OP is a cross-functional business planning process that connects different departments and aligns demand, supply, and business objectives.

Now, let's go one level deeper.

Hindi lang natin aalamin kung ano ang S&OP. Pag-aaralan natin kung anong business problems ang sinusubukan nitong solusyunan, bakit mahalaga ang coordination, at paano nagiging actual business results ang magandang planning process.

---

# 1. Topic Overview

## 1.1 What will we discuss?

This lesson focuses on three fundamental questions:

1. **Why?** Why does a company need S&OP?
2. **What?** What improvements and benefits should S&OP deliver?
3. **How?** How does S&OP translate better planning into better business performance?

Importante na maintindihan natin ang distinction between having a plan and having an aligned plan.

A company can have:
- A Sales Forecast
- A Production Plan
- A Procurement Plan
- An Inventory Plan
- A Financial Budget

Pero kahit kumpleto ang mga documents na ito, hindi ibig sabihin na coordinated na ang business.

Puwedeng maganda ang individual plans ng bawat department pero conflicting naman kapag pinagsama-sama.

## 1.2 Why is this topic important?

Imagine a manufacturing company that produces electronic components.

- Sales expects 100,000 units of customer demand.
- Production prepares to manufacture 70,000 units.
- Procurement orders materials for 120,000 units.
- Finance budgets for only 80,000 units.

May apat na plans, pero iisa lang ang company.

The problem is not necessarily that one department is doing its job incorrectly. The problem is that they are working with different assumptions and expectations.

This creates a business planning gap.

**S&OP exists to close that gap.**

# 2. Layman's Explanation

## 2.1 Imagine running a manufacturing company without S&OP

Let's use a simple analogy.

Imagine that you are organizing a large family gathering.

You assign different responsibilities:

- Person A handles the food.
- Person B handles the budget.
- Person C invites the guests.
- Person D prepares the venue.
- Person E purchases the ingredients.

Now imagine that everyone works independently.

Person C invites 150 guests.

Person A prepares food for 100 guests.

Person B allocates a budget for 80 guests.

Person E buys ingredients good for 200 guests.

What happens?

- Some guests might not have enough food.
- Some ingredients might go to waste.
- The budget might be exceeded.
- The venue might not accommodate everyone.

Everyone performed their assigned responsibility, but the overall plan failed.

**That's essentially what happens in a business when departments plan independently.**

The company has people doing their jobs, but their plans are not necessarily supporting the same business objective.

## 2.2 What does S&OP do?

S&OP brings everyone to the same planning table.

Instead of allowing each department to operate based on its own assumptions, the company develops an agreed understanding of:

- How much customers are expected to buy.
- How much the company can manufacture.
- How much inventory is available.
- How many raw materials need to be purchased.
- How much money will be required.
- What actions are necessary to meet business objectives.

For example:

Sales expects 100,000 units of demand.

Production confirms that it can manufacture 85,000 units with existing capacity.

The company has 10,000 units of usable finished goods inventory.

That means the company has a potential supply of:

\[
85,000+10,000=95,000\text{ units}
\]

There is still a 5,000-unit gap.

Instead of discovering the problem when customers start placing orders, departments can discuss possible solutions in advance.

Possible actions:

- Increase production capacity.
- Authorize overtime.
- Use an alternative production line.
- Allocate available inventory to priority customers.
- Negotiate adjusted delivery dates.
- Evaluate whether additional supply is economically justified.

This is the practical value of S&OP.

**Hindi nito magic na pinaparami ang resources ng company. Ginagawa nitong visible ang problema early enough so management can make informed decisions.**

## 2.3 The most important idea

**One Business, One Agreed Plan**

The central purpose of S&OP:

- **Demand:** What customers are expected to buy.
- **Supply:** What the company can provide.
- **Finance:** What the business can afford and expects to achieve.
- **Management:** What decisions and priorities should be agreed upon.

These come together into **one coordinated business plan**, with aligned assumptions, priorities, and decisions.

# 3. Technical Explanation

Now that we understand the concept in simple terms, let's introduce the professional terminology.

## 3.1 The formal business purpose of S&OP

Sales & Operations Planning is a cross-functional management process designed to balance demand and supply, align operational plans with financial objectives, and establish a coordinated business plan that supports organizational goals.

Let's break this definition into its important components.

| Technical term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Cross-functional alignment | Different departments work toward common business objectives. |
| Demand-Supply Balancing | Matching expected customer demand with available or achievable supply. |
| Business Plan Alignment | Making sure departmental plans support the same overall plan. |
| Financial Integration | Connecting operational decisions with revenue, costs, margins, and financial targets. |
| Decision Support | Providing management with information to evaluate alternatives and make decisions. |

## 3.2 Five main purposes of S&OP

### Purpose 1: Aligning Demand and Supply

This is one of the most fundamental purposes of S&OP.

**Demand** represents what customers want or are expected to purchase.

**Supply** represents what the company can deliver using its available resources.

The goal is to understand whether the company can satisfy expected demand.

Example:

| Item | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Forecast demand | 100,000 units |
| Available inventory | 15,000 units |
| Planned production | 75,000 units |
| Total potential supply | 90,000 units |
| Supply gap | 10,000 units |

The company has a potential shortage of 10,000 units.

S&OP makes this imbalance visible and allows the business to evaluate corrective actions.

**Professional term: Demand-Supply Gap**

This is the difference between expected demand and available or planned supply.

A positive gap indicates insufficient supply when demand exceeds supply.

A negative gap can indicate excess supply when supply exceeds demand.

Important: In actual planning, usable inventory, production timing, quality status, allocations, and customer delivery dates must also be considered. Hindi sapat ang total quantity lang.

### Purpose 2: Establishing One Agreed Business Plan

Each department may have its own operational plan, but those plans must be consistent with the company's overall direction.

This is commonly associated with the concept of a **Single Set of Numbers**.

Meaning:

Instead of Sales using one demand assumption, Operations using another, and Finance using a third, the company works toward one agreed planning baseline.

Example:

| Department | Independent assumption |
|---|---:|
| Sales | 100,000 units |
| Production | 75,000 units |
| Procurement | 120,000 units |
| Finance | 90,000 units |

Through S&OP discussions, the company investigates the differences and agrees on a coordinated plan.

One important distinction:

A single agreed plan does not mean that all departments must have identical numbers for every metric.

Sales will still have sales targets, Production will still have capacity targets, and Finance will still have financial targets.

What must be aligned are the underlying assumptions, timing, constraints, and agreed business commitments.

### Purpose 3: Connecting Operational Plans with Financial Objectives

A manufacturing company does not operate only to produce units.

It must also achieve financial objectives.

For example:

- Revenue targets
- Gross profit targets
- Operating cost targets
- Inventory investment limits
- Cash flow objectives

Consider this situation:

Sales wants to increase production to meet higher demand.

Production proposes overtime.

Procurement needs to purchase additional materials.

Finance evaluates whether the expected revenue justifies the additional costs.

This is called **Financial Integration**.

It ensures that operational decisions are evaluated not only based on production requirements but also based on their financial consequences.

For example, increasing production might improve customer service but increase labor costs, material costs, and inventory investment.

The question is not simply:

"Can we produce more?"

It becomes:

"Should we produce more, considering the expected demand, operational feasibility, and financial impact?"

### Purpose 4: Improving Cross-Functional Coordination

S&OP encourages departments to communicate and coordinate rather than operate as separate organizations.

Consider the relationships:

| Department | Main contribution to S&OP |
|---|---|
| Sales | Customer demand, market information, sales expectations |
| Operations | Production capacity, manufacturing constraints |
| Procurement | Material availability, supplier commitments, lead times |
| Inventory / Warehouse | Available stocks, inventory risks |
| Finance | Revenue, costs, margins, financial targets |
| Management | Priorities, resource allocation, business decisions |

Each department contributes information that other departments need.

For example:

Procurement might know that a critical raw material has a 12-week supplier lead time.

That information can affect:

- Production scheduling
- Customer delivery commitments
- Inventory requirements
- Cash flow planning

Without communication, Sales might promise a delivery date that Procurement and Production cannot support.

### Purpose 5: Supporting Management Decision-Making

S&OP is not just a reporting activity.

It is a management decision-making process.

Its value comes from presenting business issues, evaluating alternatives, and agreeing on actions.

Example:

Demand increases by 20%, but production capacity remains unchanged.

Management may consider:

1. Authorizing overtime.
2. Adding a production shift.
3. Outsourcing some production.
4. Prioritizing high-margin customers.
5. Negotiating delivery adjustments.

Each option has different operational and financial consequences.

S&OP helps management understand those consequences before committing to a decision.

**Key professional concept: Trade-off Management**

A trade-off happens when improving one business objective may negatively affect another.

For example:

- Higher inventory can improve product availability but increase holding costs.
- Lower inventory can reduce working capital but increase shortage risk.
- Overtime can increase production capacity but increase labor expenses.

S&OP helps the business evaluate these competing priorities.

# 4. Detailed Discussion

This section is particularly important because we will examine the actual business problems that S&OP addresses and the benefits that companies expect to achieve.

## 4.1 Why Companies Need S&OP

Let's first understand what happens when a company operates without an integrated planning process.

### A. Departmental planning conflicts

Imagine a manufacturing company where each department has its own priorities.

| Department | Individual priority | Potential conflict |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | Maximize sales and customer orders | May promise more than production can deliver. |
| Production | Maximize production efficiency | May prefer longer production runs even when demand is low. |
| Procurement | Ensure sufficient raw materials | May purchase more materials than necessary. |
| Finance | Control costs and cash flow | May restrict spending needed to increase capacity. |
| Warehouse | Maintain manageable inventory | May struggle with excess finished goods or raw materials. |

Notice something important.

**Each department can achieve its own target while the company performs poorly overall.**

For example:

- Procurement achieves its purchasing cost target by buying materials in bulk.
- Production achieves its output target by manufacturing large quantities.
- Sales fails to sell the manufactured products.
- Warehouse ends up holding excess inventory.
- Finance has money tied up in unsold products.

Individually, some departments may report good performance.

But from the company's perspective, the result is inefficient.

This is called **Suboptimization**.

**Suboptimization** happens when a department optimizes its own performance without considering the impact on the overall business.

In S&OP, the objective is to improve total business performance rather than simply maximize individual departmental performance.

### B. Lack of demand visibility

Without proper coordination, different departments may use different demand assumptions.

For example:

- Sales expects increasing demand.
- Production continues using last month's forecast.
- Procurement uses historical purchasing quantities.
- Finance assumes revenue will remain unchanged.

The problem is that there is no common understanding of what the business actually expects to sell.

Consequences include:

- Underproduction
- Overproduction
- Incorrect purchasing quantities
- Unnecessary inventory
- Missed customer deliveries

This is why demand visibility is important.

**Demand Visibility** refers to having a clear and shared understanding of expected customer demand, including its quantity, timing, and relevant changes.

### C. Reactive instead of proactive management

Another major problem is reactive decision-making.

Without S&OP, companies may only recognize problems after they happen.

Examples:

| Problem | Reactive response |
|---|---|
| Material shortage | Expedite materials after production is interrupted. |
| Excess inventory | Offer discounts after stocks accumulate. |
| Production shortage | Authorize emergency overtime. |
| Missed customer delivery | Explain delays after the committed date. |
| Sudden demand increase | Rush procurement and production. |

These actions are sometimes necessary, but they are often expensive.

With S&OP, the company tries to identify potential problems before they affect operations.

This is called **Proactive Planning**.

Instead of asking, "What went wrong?", management can ask, "What might go wrong next month, and what should we do now?"

### D. Lack of accountability

Imagine that Sales says production failed to meet demand.

Production says the forecast was inaccurate.

Procurement says the materials were not available.

Finance says additional spending was not approved.

Everyone has an explanation, but nobody has a coordinated action plan.

S&OP improves accountability by establishing:

- Agreed planning assumptions
- Defined responsibilities
- Identified business gaps
- Assigned corrective actions
- Follow-up on previous decisions

The important point is that S&OP is not about blaming departments.

It is about making business issues visible and assigning ownership for resolving them.

## 4.2 Key Benefits of S&OP

Now let's discuss the benefits one by one.

I want you to understand both the benefit itself and the business mechanism that creates it.

### Benefit 1: Better Demand Visibility and Forecast Alignment

**Direct benefit**

**What does it mean?**

Mas nagiging malinaw sa buong organization kung ano ang expected customer demand.

Sales, Production, Procurement, and Finance can work with a common demand assumption.

**Why is this important?**

Because demand is the starting point of many operational decisions.

For example:

If expected demand increases, the company may need to:

- Increase production.
- Purchase additional materials.
- Increase labor availability.
- Prepare additional inventory.
- Review financial requirements.

If the forecast decreases, the company may need to:

- Reduce production.
- Delay material purchases.
- Avoid unnecessary inventory accumulation.
- Adjust labor and capacity requirements.

**How does S&OP create this benefit?**

Through cross-functional discussion and alignment of demand information.

Sales provides market intelligence and customer information. Other departments evaluate the operational implications.

**Business impact:**

- Better planning assumptions
- Earlier visibility of demand changes
- Reduced surprises
- More consistent departmental decisions

Remember: Better demand visibility does not automatically mean a more accurate forecast. Visibility and forecast accuracy are related but different concepts.

### Benefit 2: Improved Inventory Management

**Direct benefit**

Inventory management is one of the most important areas affected by S&OP.

Why?

Because inventory is directly influenced by the relationship between demand, production, procurement, and supply availability.

Let's consider two situations.

**Situation A: Excess inventory**

The company manufactures 100,000 units, but actual customer demand is only 60,000 units.

The company has 40,000 units remaining, assuming no other inventory movements.

Possible consequences:

- Higher inventory holding costs
- More warehouse space required
- Increased working capital tied up in inventory
- Risk of obsolescence
- Potential write-offs

**Situation B: Insufficient inventory**

The company manufactures only 60,000 units, but customer demand reaches 100,000 units.

Potential consequences:

- Customer orders cannot be fulfilled.
- Delivery commitments may be missed.
- Emergency production may be required.
- Additional logistics costs may occur.
- Customers may purchase from competitors.

**How does S&OP help?**

By aligning expected demand with planned production, inventory availability, and replenishment requirements.

It helps management evaluate whether inventory levels are appropriate relative to expected demand and business priorities.

**Business impact:**

- Lower risk of excess stocks
- Lower risk of shortages
- Better inventory investment
- Improved inventory positioning

Important distinction: S&OP supports inventory decisions, but detailed inventory replenishment policies and stock calculations are handled through other planning activities. We will distinguish these concepts as we progress through the curriculum.

### Benefit 3: Better Production and Capacity Utilization

**Direct benefit**

**Capacity** refers to the amount of production output a company can achieve using its available resources within a specified period.

These resources may include:

- Machines
- Production lines
- Labor
- Working hours
- Equipment availability
- Production facilities

Imagine that a factory has a monthly production capacity of 100,000 units.

However, the expected demand is only 60,000 units.

If the company continues manufacturing 100,000 units, it may produce unnecessary inventory.

Now consider the opposite.

Expected demand is 120,000 units, but production capacity is only 100,000 units.

The company has a potential capacity shortage of 20,000 units.

S&OP helps identify these situations before they become operational problems.

**How does it create the benefit?**

By comparing expected demand with available production capacity and evaluating possible adjustments.

Possible decisions include:

- Additional shifts
- Overtime
- Alternative production lines
- Outsourcing
- Capacity expansion
- Production prioritization

**Business impact:**

- Better resource utilization
- Reduced unnecessary production
- Earlier capacity planning
- Better production readiness
- Reduced emergency operational adjustments

### Benefit 4: Reduced Shortages and Excess Inventory

**Direct and indirect benefit**

This benefit is closely connected to demand-supply balancing.

There are two opposite inventory problems:

| Situation | Meaning | Business consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Stockout | Insufficient inventory to satisfy demand | Lost sales, delayed orders, production interruption |
| Overstock | Inventory exceeds what is reasonably required | Higher holding costs, working capital consumption, obsolescence risk |

S&OP helps the company evaluate both risks together.

This is important because solving one problem can sometimes create the other.

For example:

Management wants to eliminate stockouts, so it instructs Production to manufacture significantly more products.

Stockouts decrease, but excess inventory increases.

The company solved one problem by creating another.

The goal is not simply to maximize inventory.

The goal is to maintain an appropriate balance between product availability and inventory investment.

### Benefit 5: Improved Customer Service and Order Fulfillment

**Direct and indirect benefit**

Let's introduce an important professional term:

**Customer Service Level** refers broadly to how effectively a company meets customer requirements.

This can involve:

- Product availability
- Delivery reliability
- Order completeness
- Delivery timing
- Responsiveness to customer requirements

Imagine that a customer places an order for 5,000 units with a requested delivery date.

If the company has sufficient materials, production capacity, and finished goods availability, it has a better chance of fulfilling the order on time.

However, if the company discovers a material shortage only after receiving the order, fulfillment may be delayed.

S&OP improves the visibility of these potential constraints.

**Business impact:**

- Better delivery planning
- Reduced missed commitments
- Improved product availability
- More reliable customer communication
- Potential improvement in customer satisfaction and retention

An important concept here is **OTIF (On Time In Full)**.

OTIF measures whether customer orders are delivered:

- On time
- In the complete quantity requested

For example:

A customer orders 1,000 units.

The company delivers 1,000 units on the agreed date.

That order meets both conditions.

If it delivers only 800 units, even on time, the order does not meet the full OTIF requirement.

### Benefit 6: Better Cost Control and Financial Performance

**Direct and indirect benefit**

S&OP decisions affect multiple cost categories.

| Business activity | Potential cost impact |
|---|---|
| Excess inventory | Storage, handling, insurance, obsolescence |
| Emergency procurement | Expediting and premium purchasing costs |
| Unplanned overtime | Additional labor costs |
| Production inefficiency | Higher manufacturing cost per unit |
| Missed deliveries | Penalties and potential lost business |
| Excess capacity | Underutilized resources and fixed-cost absorption concerns |

Let's use an example.

A company expects an increase in demand next month.

If the company identifies the increase early, it can plan material purchases and production schedules in advance.

If it identifies the increase too late, it may need to:

- Pay premium freight.
- Purchase materials from alternative suppliers.
- Authorize emergency overtime.
- Reschedule existing orders.

These actions may increase operating expenses.

S&OP helps reduce the likelihood of avoidable emergency decisions.

**Business impact:**

- Better cost visibility
- Improved resource allocation
- Better inventory investment decisions
- Reduced avoidable operational expenses
- Improved financial planning

Important: S&OP does not guarantee lower total costs. Sometimes the best business decision requires spending more, such as increasing capacity to protect an important customer relationship.

### Benefit 7: Improved Communication and Accountability

**Direct benefit**

S&OP establishes a structured environment where departments exchange information and resolve conflicting assumptions.

Instead of receiving information only when a problem occurs, departments can discuss anticipated changes.

For example:

Procurement informs Production that a critical material may arrive two weeks later than expected.

Production informs Sales that the delay may affect certain customer orders.

Sales identifies which customers have the highest delivery priority.

Finance evaluates the cost of alternative supply arrangements.

Management decides which action to approve.

This is cross-functional coordination in practice.

**Business impact:**

- Reduced communication gaps
- Clearer responsibilities
- Better issue resolution
- Faster escalation of critical problems
- Improved execution of agreed decisions

### Benefit 8: More Proactive and Data-Driven Decisions

**Direct and indirect benefit**

S&OP encourages management to use information rather than rely exclusively on assumptions or departmental opinions.

For example, instead of saying:

"Parang kailangan nating dagdagan ang production."

The team can present:

- Forecast demand increased by 15%.
- Existing production capacity is 100,000 units.
- Expected demand is 115,000 units.
- Available inventory is 5,000 units.
- The remaining supply gap is 10,000 units.

Now management has a clearer basis for making decisions.

This is called **Data-Driven Decision-Making**.

It does not mean that every decision must be based purely on numbers.

Market intelligence, customer relationships, management judgment, and business strategy are also important.

The purpose is to combine reliable information with informed judgment.

## 4.3 Direct Benefits vs. Indirect Benefits

This distinction is important when evaluating S&OP performance.

Not every benefit occurs immediately after implementing S&OP.

Some are direct results of improved planning, while others happen because the direct improvements influence other areas of the business.

| Direct benefits | Indirect benefits |
|---|---|
| Better demand visibility | Improved customer satisfaction |
| Better demand-supply alignment | Potentially higher customer retention |
| Earlier identification of capacity constraints | Reduced emergency operating costs |
| Better inventory planning | Improved working capital |
| Better departmental coordination | Faster business decisions |
| Improved financial integration | Better financial performance |
| Clearer action ownership | Improved organizational accountability |

**Example of a benefit chain:**

1. Sales communicates an expected increase in demand.
2. Production identifies a future capacity constraint.
3. Management approves an additional production shift.
4. The company increases available supply.
5. Customer orders are fulfilled more reliably.
6. The company avoids potential lost sales.

The direct benefit is improved supply readiness.

The indirect benefits include improved customer service and potentially higher revenue.

This distinction will help you later when evaluating whether an S&OP initiative actually creates business value.

# 5. How S&OP Creates These Benefits

This section connects everything.

We already know the benefits. Now let's examine the mechanisms that produce them.

## 5.1 The S&OP Benefit Chain

Think of S&OP as a chain of connected business improvements.

1. **Better Information:** Demand, supply, inventory, capacity, and financial data.
2. **Better Alignment:** Departments work with common assumptions.
3. **Earlier Problem Identification:** Shortages, excess inventory, capacity gaps, and cost risks.
4. **Better Management Decisions:** Evaluate alternatives and agree on corrective actions.
5. **Improved Business Performance:** Service, inventory, capacity, cost, and financial outcomes.

This is the fundamental relationship between S&OP activities and business benefits.

## 5.2 How one department affects another

Let's trace the relationships using a manufacturing example.

Suppose Sales receives information that a major customer will increase its monthly orders.

**Sales**

Communicates a forecast increase from 80,000 to 100,000 units.

**Production**

Evaluates whether existing lines and available labor can support the additional 20,000 units.

**Procurement**

Checks whether suppliers can provide the additional raw materials on time.

**Inventory / Warehouse**

Checks existing raw materials and finished goods that can support the increase.

**Finance**

Evaluates the additional material investment, labor expenses, and expected revenue.

**Management**

Approves the appropriate response based on business priorities and constraints.

Notice that a change in Sales demand affects practically every department.

This is why S&OP cannot be treated as an isolated Sales or Production activity.

## 5.3 Why S&OP benefits are interconnected

Let's say a company improves its demand visibility.

What happens next?

1. Production receives better demand information.
2. Production plans the required output.
3. Procurement purchases materials according to the agreed requirements.
4. Inventory levels become more aligned with expected demand.
5. The company becomes better prepared to fulfill customer orders.
6. Emergency purchases and unplanned production adjustments may decrease.
7. Financial performance may improve.

One improvement creates several possible downstream benefits.

However, the reverse is also true.

Poor information can affect several departments simultaneously.

For example, an overstated demand forecast can lead to:

- Excessive material purchases
- Excessive production
- Excess finished goods
- Higher inventory costs
- Reduced cash availability

This is why S&OP is a system of interconnected decisions.

**A good S&OP process does not optimize each department separately. It optimizes decisions across the business.**

# 6. Practical Examples and Manufacturing Business Scenarios

Now let's apply the concepts to realistic manufacturing situations.

For consistency, imagine a hypothetical company manufacturing electronic components.

All numerical values below are illustrative assumptions, not industry standards.

## Scenario 1: Excess Inventory Despite Low Customer Demand

**Inventory imbalance**

### Business situation

A company manufactures electronic components.

For the current month:

| Item | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Planned production | 100,000 units |
| Actual customer demand | 60,000 units |
| Remaining inventory generated | 40,000 units |

Assume the company started with zero finished goods inventory and all 60,000 units were shipped.

### A. What is the problem?

The company manufactured more products than customers purchased.

There are 40,000 units remaining.

### B. What happens without proper S&OP?

Production may continue following its original plan even though demand has declined.

Procurement may continue purchasing materials based on the previous production requirements.

Finance may not anticipate the additional inventory investment.

Consequences:

- Increased storage requirements
- Higher working capital
- Potential product obsolescence
- Increased inventory holding costs
- Cash tied up in unsold products

### C. How does S&OP help?

Sales communicates the decrease in demand.

Production evaluates whether future production quantities should be reduced.

Procurement reviews whether upcoming material purchases can be adjusted.

Finance evaluates the impact of excess inventory on working capital.

Management agrees on actions.

Possible actions:

- Reduce the next production plan.
- Delay nonessential material purchases.
- Reallocate inventory to other customers or locations.
- Evaluate alternative sales opportunities.

### D. Expected impact

The company can reduce unnecessary future production and avoid additional inventory accumulation.

Note that S&OP does not automatically eliminate the existing 40,000 units. The company still needs an inventory disposition or consumption plan.

**Main lesson:** Production should not continue manufacturing based solely on outdated assumptions when actual demand has materially changed.

---

## Scenario 2: Sales Commits Beyond Production Capacity

**Capacity and customer commitment risk**

### Business situation

Sales receives an order for 120,000 units.

However, the factory has:

| Item | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Production capacity | 100,000 units |
| Available finished goods | 5,000 units |
| Customer requirement | 120,000 units |

Potential total supply:

\[
100,000+5,000=105,000
\]

Potential supply gap:

\[
120,000-105,000=15,000
\]

### A. What is the problem?

Sales has a customer requirement that exceeds the company's available and planned supply.

### B. What happens without proper S&OP?

Sales may commit to the customer without checking production capacity.

Production discovers the shortage only when the order needs to be fulfilled.

The company may need to:

- Authorize emergency overtime.
- Delay other customer orders.
- Outsource production.
- Pay additional logistics costs.
- Negotiate a revised delivery schedule.

Customer trust may also be affected.

### C. How does S&OP help?

The demand increase becomes visible during planning.

Production identifies the capacity gap.

Procurement checks material availability.

Finance evaluates the additional cost of increasing production.

Management decides whether to:

- Increase capacity.
- Authorize overtime.
- Prioritize the order.
- Negotiate delivery adjustments.

### D. Expected impact

The company has an opportunity to address the capacity shortage before it becomes a customer fulfillment failure.

**Main lesson:** Sales commitments must be evaluated against the company's actual ability to supply.

---

## Scenario 3: Production Manufactures Based on Outdated Forecasts

**Forecast alignment problem**

### Business situation

Last month, the demand forecast was 100,000 units.

However, the latest market information indicates that expected demand has decreased to 70,000 units.

Production is still following the previous 100,000-unit plan.

### A. What is the problem?

Production is operating with outdated demand information.

### B. What happens without proper S&OP?

Production manufactures 30,000 additional units relative to the latest demand forecast.

Possible consequences:

- Excess finished goods
- Unnecessary material consumption
- Higher inventory costs
- Additional warehouse requirements
- Reduced flexibility to manufacture other products

### C. How does S&OP help?

The latest demand information is communicated and evaluated.

Production reviews its future plan.

Procurement reviews material requirements.

Finance evaluates the impact of the production adjustment.

Management agrees on the updated business plan.

### D. Expected impact

The company can reduce unnecessary production and align resource utilization with the latest demand outlook.

**Main lesson:** An operational plan must reflect current business assumptions, not simply repeat historical plans.

---

## Scenario 4: Procurement Orders Materials Without Visibility of Actual Demand

**Procurement and inventory risk**

### Business situation

Procurement orders raw materials based on historical consumption.

However, customer demand has decreased.

The supplier has a minimum order quantity that encourages large purchases.

For illustration:

| Item | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Expected material requirement | 50,000 kg |
| Procurement order | 80,000 kg |
| Potential excess purchase | 30,000 kg |

Assume the figures are for the same planning horizon and that there are no other material requirements or existing stocks.

### A. What is the problem?

Procurement is purchasing based on historical consumption rather than the latest agreed requirements and available inventory.

### B. What happens without proper S&OP?

The company may accumulate unnecessary raw materials.

Consequences:

- Increased raw material inventory
- Higher storage and handling costs
- Cash tied up in materials
- Risk of material degradation or obsolescence
- Reduced warehouse capacity

### C. How does S&OP help?

Sales provides updated demand information.

Production evaluates the required output.

Procurement reviews the resulting material requirements, existing stock, supplier commitments, and purchasing constraints.

Finance evaluates the additional inventory investment.

Management can decide whether to adjust future orders, negotiate delivery schedules, or accept the purchase because of a justified business reason.

### D. Expected impact

Improved coordination between demand, production, and purchasing can reduce unnecessary material accumulation.

**Main lesson:** Procurement requirements should be connected to expected production needs, existing inventory, and supplier constraints.

## Scenario 5: Sudden Increase in Demand Creates a Supply Shortage

**Demand-supply imbalance**

### Business situation

A company experiences an unexpected increase in customer demand.

| Item | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Original demand forecast | 80,000 units |
| Updated expected demand | 120,000 units |
| Available production capacity | 90,000 units |
| Available finished goods | 10,000 units |

Potential supply:

\[
90,000+10,000=100,000
\]

Potential supply gap:

\[
120,000-100,000=20,000
\]

### A. What is the problem?

Demand has increased, but supply cannot immediately respond.

### B. What happens without proper S&OP?

The company may discover the issue too late.

Possible consequences:

- Material shortages
- Production bottlenecks
- Delayed customer orders
- Increased overtime
- Emergency procurement
- Lost sales opportunities

### C. How does S&OP help?

The company evaluates the demand increase and determines whether it is temporary or expected to continue.

Then:

- Production evaluates additional capacity.
- Procurement checks supplier availability.
- Inventory identifies available stocks.
- Finance evaluates the cost of additional supply.
- Management prioritizes customer and product requirements.

### D. Expected impact

The company can prepare a coordinated response and potentially protect important customer commitments.

**Main lesson:** S&OP helps businesses respond to demand changes by evaluating the entire supply chain rather than allowing individual departments to react independently.

# 7. Limitations and Misconceptions

Important ito dahil ayokong magkaroon ka ng impression na S&OP is a magic solution to every business problem.

It is a management process, not a guarantee of perfect business performance.

## 7.1 S&OP does not automatically guarantee accurate forecasts

**Misconception:** Once a company implements S&OP, its demand forecasts will become accurate.

Not necessarily.

S&OP improves the coordination and evaluation of demand information.

However, forecasts can still be inaccurate because of:

- Unexpected market changes
- Customer cancellations
- New competitors
- Economic conditions
- Product launches
- Changes in customer purchasing behavior
- Incomplete historical data

For example:

The company forecasts 100,000 units, but actual demand becomes 130,000 units because of an unexpected customer order.

Even with a good S&OP process, the forecast may still be wrong.

What S&OP improves is the organization's ability to recognize changes, evaluate their consequences, and respond accordingly.

## 7.2 S&OP does not automatically eliminate inventory problems

**Misconception:** If a company has S&OP, it will no longer experience excess inventory or shortages.

Not necessarily.

Inventory problems can also result from:

- Supplier unreliability
- Poor inventory records
- Quality rejection
- Long material lead times
- Production breakdowns
- Incorrect inventory parameters
- Unexpected demand fluctuations

S&OP helps coordinate decisions affecting inventory, but the actual inventory management processes must also work effectively.

## 7.3 S&OP cannot create additional physical capacity automatically

If a factory can only produce 100,000 units per month, an S&OP meeting cannot magically increase its capacity to 150,000 units.

The process can identify the shortage and recommend alternatives.

But implementing those alternatives requires actual resources, approvals, and execution.

For example:

- Hiring additional workers
- Purchasing new equipment
- Increasing production shifts
- Outsourcing manufacturing

These decisions have operational and financial implications.

## 7.4 S&OP cannot eliminate all supply chain disruptions

Suppliers can experience:

- Natural disasters
- Equipment failures
- Transportation delays
- Geopolitical disruptions
- Raw material shortages

S&OP helps companies evaluate potential exposure and prepare responses.

But it cannot prevent every external disruption.

## 7.5 Reliable data is essential

Imagine that Sales reports expected demand of 100,000 units, but the actual forecast data contains duplicate customer orders.

Production then plans based on incorrect information.

Procurement purchases additional materials.

Finance allocates unnecessary funds.

The entire plan becomes unreliable.

This demonstrates the importance of **Data Quality**.

Data quality refers to the reliability, accuracy, completeness, consistency, and timeliness of information used for planning.

Poor data can lead to poor decisions even when the company has a structured S&OP process.

## 7.6 Management commitment is essential

S&OP requires decisions that may affect multiple departments.

For example:

- Should the company increase production?
- Should it accept additional inventory investment?
- Should it prioritize one customer over another?
- Should it authorize overtime?
- Should it accept additional operating costs?

These decisions may require management authority.

If departments participate in meetings but nobody can resolve conflicts or approve actions, S&OP becomes a reporting exercise rather than an effective management process.

**Important professional principle:**

S&OP requires both organizational participation and management commitment.

# 8. Business Perspective and Decision-Making

Now let's move toward the role of an S&OP analyst.

This section is especially important for your professional development as a Senior Analyst in S&OP.

Your role is not simply to collect departmental numbers and prepare reports.

You need to understand what the numbers are telling the business, identify gaps, and help decision-makers understand the available options.

## 8.1 How an S&OP analyst identifies business problems

An S&OP analyst typically examines several areas.

| Area | Questions an analyst should ask |
|---|---|
| Demand | Is expected demand increasing or decreasing? |
| Supply | Can the company produce or obtain the required supply? |
| Inventory | Is inventory sufficient, excessive, or incorrectly positioned? |
| Capacity | Are production resources sufficient? |
| Procurement | Can suppliers provide materials on time? |
| Financials | What are the revenue and cost implications? |
| Customer service | Can customer commitments be fulfilled? |

The analyst must connect these findings.

### Example: Analyzing a potential supply shortage

Suppose you receive the following information:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Forecast demand | 150,000 units |
| Available finished goods | 20,000 units |
| Planned production | 100,000 units |
| Total potential supply | 120,000 units |
| Potential supply gap | 30,000 units |

A basic analyst might report:

"Potential supply shortage of 30,000 units."

A stronger S&OP analyst asks:

1. Is the demand increase supported by actual customer information?
2. Is the available inventory usable and available for allocation?
3. Can production increase its output?
4. Are raw materials sufficient?
5. Are supplier lead times a constraint?
6. Which customers or products should receive priority?
7. What is the financial impact of the different alternatives?

This is the difference between reporting a number and analyzing a business problem.

## 8.2 Understanding competing business priorities

One of the most important responsibilities of S&OP is helping management evaluate trade-offs.

Let's consider four common conflicts.

| Business priority | Potential conflict |
|---|---|
| Maximize customer service | May require higher inventory or additional capacity. |
| Minimize inventory | May increase shortage risk. |
| Minimize production cost | May encourage larger production batches. |
| Maximize production utilization | May create excess finished goods if demand is insufficient. |

There is rarely one solution that maximizes every objective simultaneously.

The company needs to decide which priorities are most important under the current business conditions.

### Example: Should management increase production?

Suppose:

- Customer demand is increasing.
- Production capacity is insufficient.
- Additional overtime is available.
- Overtime will increase labor expenses.
- Not meeting demand may result in lost sales.

Management needs to evaluate:

**Option A: Maintain existing production capacity**

Advantages:
- No additional overtime cost.
- No immediate increase in production expenses.

Disadvantages:
- Potential shortage.
- Delayed customer deliveries.
- Potential lost revenue.

**Option B: Authorize overtime**

Advantages:
- Higher available output.
- Better opportunity to meet customer demand.
- Potential protection of customer relationships.

Disadvantages:
- Higher labor costs.
- Potential employee fatigue.
- Possible increase in manufacturing cost.

The correct decision depends on the expected business value of the additional production relative to its cost and operational risks.

This is where S&OP provides value.

## 8.3 Connecting operational decisions to financial performance

Let's introduce three important financial concepts.

| Concept | Meaning | Why it matters in S&OP |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | Money generated from sales | Indicates expected business income. |
| Operating Costs | Expenses associated with business operations | Helps evaluate the cost of meeting demand. |
| Working Capital | Capital used to support day-to-day business operations | Inventory and other operating assets can consume available cash. |

Example:

A company decides to manufacture an additional 20,000 units.

This decision may increase:

- Revenue potential
- Raw material requirements
- Labor expenses
- Inventory investment
- Warehousing costs

An S&OP analyst should help quantify these effects so management can evaluate whether the decision makes business sense.

## 8.4 What should an S&OP analyst deliver?

At a practical level, your work should help management answer questions such as:

- What is expected to happen?
- What is different from the previous plan?
- Where are the demand-supply gaps?
- Which products or customers are at risk?
- What constraints are affecting supply?
- What are the financial consequences?
- What decisions are required?
- Who is responsible for the next action?

A useful distinction:

**Reporting tells management what happened or what the numbers show. Analysis explains why it matters. S&OP decision support helps management determine what to do.**

## 8.5 A professional mindset to develop

As you develop in S&OP, try to move through these three levels:

### Level 1: Understanding

"Demand is higher than available supply."

You recognize the business condition.

### Level 2: Technical Application

"Forecast demand is 150,000 units, while available and planned supply is 120,000 units, resulting in a potential 30,000-unit supply gap."

You can quantify and explain the problem.

### Level 3: Professional Analysis

"The 30,000-unit supply gap is primarily driven by a production capacity constraint and insufficient material availability. We should evaluate additional production shifts, supplier expediting, and customer allocation priorities based on their cost and service impact."

You can identify causes, evaluate alternatives, and recommend business actions.

That is the direction we want to develop throughout this learning program.

# 9. Formulas and Calculations

Although this topic is primarily conceptual, there are a few simple calculations that help quantify the benefits of S&OP.

These calculations will become useful when analyzing actual business plans.

## 9.1 Demand-Supply Gap

**Purpose:** Determine whether planned supply is sufficient to satisfy expected demand.

\[
\text{Supply Gap}=\text{Demand}-\text{Available Supply}
\]

Where:

- Demand = Expected customer requirements (units)
- Available Supply = Usable inventory + planned supply (units), within the relevant planning horizon

Example:

| Variable | Value |
|---|---:|
| Expected demand | 150,000 |
| Available inventory | 20,000 |
| Planned production | 100,000 |
| Total supply | 120,000 |

\[
150,000-120,000=30,000
\]

**30,000-unit shortage**

Interpretation: The company needs to evaluate how to address the potential 30,000-unit shortage.

A positive result means demand exceeds supply. A negative result means supply exceeds demand.

## 9.2 Demand Fulfillment Rate

**Purpose:** Determine what proportion of demand can potentially be covered by available supply.

\[
\text{Demand Fulfillment Rate}=
\frac{\text{Supply Available to Meet Demand}}{\text{Demand}}
\times100
\]

Example:

- Demand = 100,000 units
- Supply available = 90,000 units

\[
\frac{90,000}{100,000}\times100=90\%
\]

Interpretation: The company has enough supply to cover 90% of the expected demand, assuming the supply is available at the required time and can be allocated appropriately.

This is a planning calculation, not automatically the same as actual customer service or OTIF performance.

## 9.3 Excess Supply

**Purpose:** Identify how much planned supply exceeds expected demand.

\[
\text{Excess Supply}=\text{Total Supply}-\text{Demand}
\]

Example:

- Planned supply = 120,000 units
- Expected demand = 90,000 units

\[
120,000-90,000=30,000
\]

Interpretation: There is a potential 30,000-unit excess supply relative to demand.

However, this does not automatically mean all 30,000 units will become ending inventory. Actual inventory depends on beginning inventory, production timing, shipments, allocations, and other movements.

**Important:** These are simplified planning calculations. In actual S&OP analysis, timing matters just as much as quantity. A company may have sufficient total supply for the month but still experience a shortage during the first week.

# 10. Key Takeaways

Let's consolidate the most important concepts from this lesson.

## 10.1 Why companies need S&OP

Companies need S&OP because individual departments often have different plans, assumptions, priorities, and performance objectives.

Without coordination, these differences can create:

- Excess inventory
- Material shortages
- Production capacity problems
- Missed customer deliveries
- Unnecessary operating costs
- Poor financial performance

## 10.2 The five main purposes of S&OP

| Purpose | What you should remember |
|---|---|
| Demand-Supply Alignment | Match expected demand with achievable supply. |
| One Agreed Business Plan | Align departmental assumptions and commitments. |
| Financial Integration | Connect operational decisions with financial objectives. |
| Cross-Functional Coordination | Make departments work together. |
| Management Decision Support | Help management evaluate alternatives and make informed decisions. |

## 10.3 The eight key benefits

1. Better demand visibility and forecast alignment.
2. Improved inventory management.
3. Better production and capacity utilization.
4. Reduced shortages and excess inventory.
5. Improved customer service and order fulfillment.
6. Better cost control and financial performance.
7. Improved communication and accountability.
8. More proactive and data-driven decisions.

## 10.4 Important professional terminology

Make sure you understand these terms:

| Terminology | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cross-functional Alignment | Coordination across departments |
| Demand-Supply Gap | Difference between demand and supply |
| Suboptimization | Optimizing one department at the expense of overall business performance |
| Demand Visibility | Shared understanding of expected customer demand |
| Financial Integration | Connecting operational and financial plans |
| Trade-off Management | Evaluating competing business priorities |
| Proactive Planning | Identifying and addressing potential problems in advance |
| Data-Driven Decision-Making | Using reliable information to support decisions |
| Data Quality | Reliability and consistency of planning information |
| Working Capital | Capital supporting day-to-day business operations |

## 10.5 The most important lesson

I want you to remember this principle:

**S&OP is not simply about making better forecasts, producing more products, or maintaining more inventory. It is about making better coordinated business decisions.**

A company can have excellent individual departments but still perform poorly if those departments are not aligned.

The value of S&OP comes from connecting their decisions and ensuring that the business operates toward common objectives.
