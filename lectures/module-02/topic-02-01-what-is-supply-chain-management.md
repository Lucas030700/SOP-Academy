# Module 2 – Topic 2.1: What is Supply Chain Management (SCM)?

**Main objective:** Understand how a company manages the complete journey of a product, starting from raw material suppliers until the finished product reaches the end customer, and how Sales & Operations Planning (S&OP) helps coordinate this entire system.

For this lesson, gagamit tayo ng manufacturing company that produces electronic components, para relatable sa actual manufacturing environment.

---

# 1. Topic Overview

## 1.1 What will we learn?

Sa previous module, pinag-aralan natin ang fundamental concepts ng business planning, particularly:

- **Demand:** Ano at gaano karaming products ang kailangan ng customers?
- **Supply:** Gaano karaming products ang kayang i-provide ng company?
- **S&OP:** Paano iko-coordinate ang demand at supply para makabuo ng aligned business plan?

Ngayon, palalawakin natin ang perspective.

Hindi lang natin titingnan kung paano nagpa-plan ang company para makapag-produce ng products. Aalamin natin kung saan nanggagaling ang materials, paano ginagawa ang products, paano iniimbak, paano dinadala sa customers, at paano hinahandle ang problems along the way.

Dito pumapasok ang **Supply Chain Management (SCM).**

## 1.2 Why is SCM important?

Imagine na may customer na nag-order ng 100,000 electronic components.

Kahit capable ang manufacturing department na gumawa ng 100,000 units, hindi pa rin guaranteed na ma-deliver ito on time.

Bakit?

- Baka walang available raw materials.
- Baka delayed ang supplier.
- Baka insufficient ang production capacity.
- Baka may machine breakdown.
- Baka hindi pumasa sa quality inspection.
- Baka kulang ang warehouse capacity.
- Baka delayed ang transportation.

Makikita natin na hindi sapat na mahusay lang ang manufacturing department.

**The entire supply chain must work together to satisfy customer demand.**

## 1.3 Connection to Module 2

Ang Module 2 ay magbibigay sa atin ng foundation tungkol sa supply chain environment kung saan ginagamit ang S&OP.

Before we analyze individual planning activities, kailangan muna nating maintindihan ang bigger picture: the entire supply chain.

# 2. Layman's Explanation

## 2.1 What is a Supply Chain?

Let's start with a simple example.

Imagine na bibili ka ng smartphone.

Bago makarating sa hands mo ang smartphone, maraming activities at organizations ang involved.

### The journey of a smartphone

1. **Raw Material Suppliers**

   Nagbibigay ng materials tulad ng lithium, copper, aluminum, silica, at iba pa.

2. **Component Manufacturers**

   Gumagawa ng battery cells, semiconductor chips, display panels, at camera modules.

3. **Final Product Manufacturer**

   Ina-assemble ang iba't ibang components para maging complete smartphone.

4. **Distributor**

   Tumatanggap ng bulk shipments at namamahagi ng products sa iba't ibang locations.

5. **Retailer**

   Nagbebenta ng smartphone sa physical stores o online platforms.

6. **End Customer**

   Bumibili at gumagamit ng smartphone.

Ang buong network na ito, mula raw materials hanggang end customer, ang tinatawag na **Supply Chain**.

In simple terms:

**Supply Chain = The entire network of people, companies, resources, activities, and processes involved in delivering a product or service to the customer.**

Hindi lang ito isang company. Puwedeng maraming independent companies ang involved.

## 2.2 What is Supply Chain Management?

Ngayon, isipin mo naman na ikaw ang manager na responsible sa pag-coordinate ng buong smartphone supply chain.

Kailangan mong siguraduhin na:

- May sapat na raw materials.
- On time ang deliveries ng suppliers.
- Available ang manufacturing capacity.
- Tama ang production schedule.
- Sapat ang finished goods inventory.
- Available ang transportation.
- On time makarating ang products sa customers.

Hindi puwedeng kanya-kanyang desisyon lang ang bawat department.

Halimbawa:

Ang sales department ay nagbebenta ng 50,000 units, pero ang procurement department ay 30,000 units lang ang binili na materials.

Resulta? Potential shortage.

Or bumili naman ang procurement ng materials para sa 100,000 units kahit 30,000 units lang ang expected demand.

Resulta? Potential excess inventory.

Ang trabaho ng Supply Chain Management ay siguraduhin na coordinated ang activities na ito.

**Supply Chain Management = The process of planning, coordinating, executing, and controlling activities across the supply chain to deliver products and services to customers efficiently and effectively.**

## 2.3 Difference Between Supply Chain and Supply Chain Management

Importanteng maintindihan ang distinction na ito.

| Supply Chain | Supply Chain Management |
|---|---|
| The network itself | The management of that network |
| Includes suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, and customers | Coordinates their activities |
| Represents the entire system | Represents how the system is managed |
| Focuses on who and what is involved | Focuses on how everything works together |

**Simple analogy:**

Imagine a basketball team.

- Supply Chain = The players, coach, equipment, and supporting staff.
- Supply Chain Management = How the team organizes, coordinates, and manages everyone to achieve its objectives.

May team ka nga, pero kung walang coordination, mahihirapan kayong manalo.

Ganoon din sa supply chain.

# 3. Technical Explanation

Now that we understand the basic idea, let's introduce the professional concepts.

## 3.1 Formal Definition of Supply Chain Management

In professional business and supply chain environments:

**Supply Chain Management (SCM)** is the integrated management of the flow of materials, information, and finances across organizations, processes, and activities involved in transforming raw materials into finished products and delivering them to end customers.

May ilang importanteng words dito:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Integrated management | Coordinated ang different departments and organizations |
| Flow of materials | Movement of physical goods |
| Flow of information | Exchange of orders, forecasts, schedules, and other data |
| Flow of finances | Movement of money and financial transactions |
| Transformation | Converting raw materials into finished products |
| End customer | Final recipient or user of the product |

Notice na hindi lang physical movement of products ang SCM.

Kasama rin dito ang information and financial transactions na kailangan para gumana ang buong supply chain.

## 3.2 The Main Objectives of Supply Chain Management

Generally, may isang central objective ang supply chain:

**Satisfy customer requirements while optimizing the overall performance of the supply chain.**

Pero paano natin malalaman kung successful ang supply chain?

Let's break it down.

### A. Delivering the Right Product

Dapat ang product na matatanggap ng customer ay exactly what was ordered.

Example:

Customer ordered MLCC Model A, pero Model B ang na-deliver.

Even if on time ang delivery, failed pa rin ang customer requirement.

### B. Delivering the Right Quantity

Dapat tama ang quantity.

| Customer Order | Actual Delivery | Result |
|---:|---:|---|
| 10,000 units | 10,000 units | Complete |
| 10,000 units | 8,000 units | Shortage |
| 10,000 units | 12,000 units | Excess delivery |

Ang shortage ay puwedeng mag-cause ng production interruption sa customer.

Ang excess delivery naman ay puwedeng mag-create ng additional inventory and handling costs.

### C. Delivering to the Right Location

Dapat tama ang delivery destination.

Example:

May dalawang customer:

- Customer A: Laguna
- Customer B: Batangas

Kung sa Batangas na-deliver ang order na para sa Laguna, may logistics failure kahit complete ang shipment.

### D. Delivering at the Right Time

Timing is important.

Example:

Required ng customer ang 10,000 units on November 20.

Kung November 25 pa dumating, five days late ang delivery.

Possible consequence:
- Customer production delay.
- Missed customer commitments.
- Penalties, depending on the contract.
- Reduced customer satisfaction.

### E. Maintaining Product Quality

Hindi enough na ma-deliver ang product.

Dapat pasok din ito sa required specifications.

Example:

10,000 MLCC units ang na-deliver, pero 500 units ang defective.

Technically, may delivered quantity, pero hindi lahat ay usable ng customer.

### F. Managing Operational Costs

Kailangan efficient ang paggamit ng resources.

Examples:
- Procurement costs.
- Manufacturing costs.
- Warehouse costs.
- Transportation costs.
- Inventory carrying costs.
- Labor and operational expenses.

Hindi ibig sabihin na pinakamura lagi ang best option.

For example, choosing the cheapest supplier may create problems if that supplier frequently delivers late or has inconsistent quality.

### G. Maintaining Appropriate Inventory Levels

Kailangan magkaroon ng sapat na inventory para matugunan ang demand.

Pero hindi rin magandang sobrang dami ang inventory.

Too little inventory = potential shortages.

Too much inventory = tied-up capital, storage costs, and possible obsolescence.

### H. Meeting Customer Expectations

Ultimately, all these objectives contribute to customer satisfaction.

Customers generally expect:
- Correct products.
- Complete quantities.
- Consistent quality.
- Reliable delivery.
- Competitive pricing.
- Dependable service.

## 3.3 The Customer Service, Cost, and Inventory Balance

Ito ang isa sa pinakamahalagang concepts sa SCM.

Imagine three companies:

| Company | Inventory | Customer Service | Operational Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company A | Very low | Frequent shortages | Low inventory cost, potentially high disruption cost |
| Company B | Very high | High availability | High inventory cost |
| Company C | Optimized | Reliable availability | Balanced costs |

Ang goal ay hindi necessarily magkaroon ng pinakamababang inventory.

Ang goal ay magkaroon ng **appropriate inventory level that supports customer demand without unnecessarily increasing costs.**

Ito ang concept ng supply chain optimization.

# 4. Detailed Discussion

## 4.1 Major Activities in Supply Chain Management

Ang SCM ay composed of several interconnected activities.

For our foundation, gagamitin natin ang five major activity groups na binanggit mo.

### The five major SCM activity groups

1. **PLANNING**

   Determine what is needed, how much is needed, when it is needed, and how resources will be allocated.

2. **SOURCING / PROCUREMENT**

   Identify suppliers and obtain the materials and services required by the business.

3. **MANUFACTURING / PRODUCTION**

   Transform raw materials and components into finished products.

4. **LOGISTICS AND DISTRIBUTION**

   Store, handle, transport, and deliver products to customers.

5. **RETURNS MANAGEMENT**

   Handle products that are returned, defective, damaged, or require recovery.

Let's understand each one.

### A. Planning

Planning is the activity of determining what the company needs to do before executing its operations.

Hindi puwedeng basta bumili, mag-produce, at mag-deliver without understanding the expected requirements.

May iba't ibang planning activities:

**1. Demand Planning**

Determines how much product customers are expected to require.

Example:

A manufacturer expects customers to order 500,000 MLCC units next month.

The company needs to understand:
- Expected demand.
- Historical sales.
- Customer forecasts.
- Market trends.
- Seasonal changes.

**2. Supply Planning**

Determines how the company will satisfy the expected demand.

Example:

Expected demand = 500,000 units.

The company needs to determine whether available materials, suppliers, production capacity, and inventory can support this requirement.

**3. Inventory Planning**

Determines how much inventory should be maintained.

Example:

The company wants enough raw materials to support production without purchasing excessive quantities.

**4. Production and Capacity Planning**

Determines how much the manufacturing facility can produce within a specific timeframe.

Example:

Required production = 500,000 units.

Available capacity = 400,000 units.

May potential supply gap na 100,000 units.

Possible actions:
- Overtime.
- Additional production shifts.
- Capacity expansion.
- Subcontracting.
- Demand prioritization.

**Why is planning important?**

Because planning identifies potential problems before they become actual operational disruptions.

Instead of discovering a shortage kapag kailangan na ang production, puwede itong ma-identify earlier.

### B. Sourcing / Procurement

Once alam na ng company kung ano ang kailangan, kailangan naman nitong kumuha ng materials and services.

Dito pumapasok ang sourcing and procurement.

**Sourcing** generally focuses on identifying, evaluating, and selecting suppliers.

**Procurement** covers the broader purchasing process, including obtaining materials and managing purchasing transactions.

Example:

An electronic component manufacturer requires:

| Material | Required Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Ceramic powder | 5,000 kg |
| Nickel powder | 2,000 kg |
| PET film | 10,000 rolls |
| Packaging materials | 50,000 sets |

The procurement team must ensure that these materials are purchased and delivered according to the production requirements.

Important considerations:

- Supplier price.
- Material quality.
- Supplier reliability.
- Available supply.
- Minimum order quantity (MOQ).
- Lead time.
- Payment terms.
- Transportation requirements.

**Important term: Lead Time**

Lead time refers to the time required to complete a process from its starting point to its completion.

For procurement, it commonly refers to the time between placing a purchase order and receiving the materials, although companies may define the exact start and end points differently.

Example:

- Purchase order date: November 1.
- Material arrival: November 21.

Procurement lead time = 20 days.

Bakit important ito?

Kasi kung 20 days ang supplier lead time, hindi puwedeng November 15 pa lang mag-order kung kailangan na ang material sa November 18.

### C. Manufacturing / Production

Ito ang process kung saan kino-convert ang raw materials and components into finished goods.

Example:

Sa electronic component manufacturing:

1. Raw materials are prepared.
2. Materials undergo processing.
3. Components are formed.
4. Products undergo firing or other required processes.
5. Products are inspected and tested.
6. Finished products are prepared for storage or shipment.

Important manufacturing considerations:

**Production Planning**

Determines what products to produce, how many to produce, and when to produce them.

**Capacity Utilization**

Measures how much of the available production capacity is being used.

**Resource Availability**

Includes:
- Machines.
- Operators.
- Materials.
- Production lines.
- Utilities.
- Manufacturing space.

**Quality Management**

Ensures that products meet specifications and customer requirements.

Important distinction:

Producing more units does not automatically mean better supply chain performance.

If production increases but demand does not, the company may accumulate excessive finished goods inventory.

### D. Logistics and Distribution

Once finished products are available, kailangan naman itong i-store, i-handle, at i-deliver.

**Warehousing**

Storage and management of materials and finished goods.

**Transportation**

Physical movement of goods through trucks, ships, airplanes, or other transport methods.

**Distribution Planning**

Determines where products should be sent, in what quantities, and when.

**Order Fulfillment**

The complete process of receiving, processing, preparing, and delivering customer orders.

Example:

A manufacturer has 100,000 finished units.

- Customer A orders 40,000 units.
- Customer B orders 35,000 units.
- Customer C orders 25,000 units.

The distribution team must ensure that each customer receives the correct quantity at the correct destination and within the committed delivery schedule.

### E. Returns Management

Hindi natatapos ang supply chain kapag na-deliver na ang product.

May mga situations where products need to return to the manufacturer or another party.

Examples:
- Defective products.
- Damaged products.
- Incorrect shipments.
- Warranty claims.
- Product recalls.
- Reusable packaging.

Ang process ng paggalaw ng products pabalik sa supplier or manufacturer ay tinatawag na **Reverse Logistics**.

Possible actions include:

- Repair.
- Replacement.
- Refurbishment.
- Recycling.
- Recovery of reusable materials.
- Proper disposal.

Example:

A customer reports that 500 electronic components are defective.

The manufacturer may need to:
1. Receive the returned products.
2. Inspect and investigate the defects.
3. Determine whether repair or replacement is possible.
4. Process the return.
5. Identify the root cause.
6. Implement corrective actions.

Importante ito dahil ang returns data ay puwedeng mag-reveal ng quality issues, supplier problems, or manufacturing process weaknesses.

## 4.2 How Are These Activities Connected?

Let's say the company expects to sell 100,000 units.

Hindi independent ang five activity groups.

Ganito ang relationship:

| Activity | Main Question | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Planning | What do we need and when? | Operational plans |
| Procurement | Where will we get the materials? | Available materials |
| Manufacturing | How will we produce the products? | Finished goods |
| Logistics | How will we deliver the products? | Customer deliveries |
| Returns Management | What happens to returned products? | Recovered goods, replacements, corrective actions |

Consider this scenario:

1. Demand planning identifies 100,000 units of expected demand.
2. Supply planning determines that 100,000 units can be produced.
3. Procurement orders the necessary raw materials.
4. Manufacturing converts materials into finished goods.
5. Logistics delivers the products.
6. Customer feedback and returns provide information for future planning.

If procurement fails to provide materials, manufacturing may stop.

If manufacturing fails, logistics has no finished products to deliver.

If logistics fails, products may remain in the warehouse despite being available.

**The performance of the entire supply chain depends on the coordination of its activities.**

## 4.3 Understanding Supply Chain Participants

Now let's identify the organizations and parties involved.

Not all companies have the same supply chain structure. Some have many layers of suppliers and distributors, while others sell directly to customers.

| Participant | Primary Role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Raw Material Supplier | Provides basic materials | Metal, plastic, chemicals |
| Component Supplier | Produces parts used in another product | Electronic chips, battery cells |
| Manufacturer | Converts materials into finished goods | Electronics factory |
| Distributor | Purchases and distributes products | Regional distributor |
| Wholesaler | Buys products in bulk and resells them | Wholesale supplier |
| Retailer | Sells products to end customers | Electronics store |
| End Customer | Uses or consumes the product | Individual buyer |
| Logistics Provider | Handles transportation and logistics services | Trucking company, freight forwarder |

### Example: A Multi-Tier Supply Chain

Imagine an electronic components manufacturer.

**Example supply chain structure**

**Tier 2 Suppliers**

Provide basic raw materials: ceramic minerals, metals, chemicals.

↓

**Tier 1 Suppliers**

Provide processed materials and components: ceramic powder, electrodes, packaging.

↓

**Manufacturer**

Produces finished electronic components through production, inspection, and packaging.

↓

**Distributor / Logistics Provider**

Stores and distributes finished goods.

↓

**Customer**

Uses the electronic components in its products.

### What are Tier 1 and Tier 2 Suppliers?

These terms describe how close a supplier is to the manufacturer in the supply chain.

- **Tier 1 Supplier:** Directly supplies the manufacturer.
- **Tier 2 Supplier:** Supplies the Tier 1 supplier.
- **Tier 3 Supplier:** Supplies the Tier 2 supplier.

Example:

If your manufacturing company purchases ceramic powder directly from Supplier A, Supplier A is your Tier 1 supplier.

If Supplier B provides the minerals used by Supplier A to manufacture ceramic powder, Supplier B is your Tier 2 supplier.

Why does this matter?

Because a disruption can happen even if your direct supplier is operating normally.

For example, your Tier 1 supplier may be unable to deliver because its own raw material supplier has a shortage.

This is an example of **upstream supply chain risk**.

## 4.4 Understanding the Three Major Supply Chain Flows

This is one of the most important foundations in SCM.

A supply chain operates through three major flows:

1. Material Flow
2. Information Flow
3. Financial Flow

These three must be coordinated.

### A. Material Flow

This refers to the physical movement of goods throughout the supply chain.

It includes:

- Raw materials.
- Components.
- Work-in-Process (WIP).
- Finished Goods (FG).
- Returned products.

**Example:**

Forward material flow:

Raw Materials → Work-in-Process (WIP) → Finished Goods (FG) → Customer

Reverse material flow:

Customer Return / Defective Product → Inspection and Recovery / Repair / Disposal

The forward movement is commonly called **Forward Logistics**.

The movement of returned products back through the supply chain is called **Reverse Logistics**.

### B. Information Flow

Information flow refers to the exchange of data between different supply chain participants.

Examples:

| Information | Who Provides It? | Who Uses It? |
|---|---|---|
| Customer orders | Customer | Sales, planning |
| Demand forecast | Customer / Sales | Demand planning |
| Inventory levels | Warehouse | Planning, procurement |
| Production schedule | Production planning | Manufacturing, procurement |
| Material availability | Suppliers / Warehouse | Procurement, planning |
| Shipment status | Logistics provider | Customer service, sales |

Let's use a simple example.

A customer informs the manufacturer that it expects to purchase 100,000 units next month.

This information reaches:
- Sales.
- Demand planning.
- Supply planning.
- Procurement.
- Production planning.

Each department uses the information for its own activities.

Kung walang accurate information, puwedeng mali ang decisions kahit capable naman ang company.

**Important concept: Information must be timely, accurate, and shared with the right participants.**

### C. Financial Flow

Financial flow refers to the movement of money and financial obligations across the supply chain.

Examples:
- Purchasing payments.
- Supplier invoices.
- Customer payments.
- Credit terms.
- Transportation expenses.
- Inventory investment.
- Revenue collection.

Example:

1. The manufacturer purchases ₱2 million worth of raw materials.
2. The supplier delivers the materials.
3. The manufacturer processes the supplier's invoice.
4. The manufacturer pays according to the agreed credit terms.
5. The manufacturer sells finished goods to customers.
6. The customers pay according to their payment terms.

### Why Must the Three Flows Be Coordinated?

Imagine this situation:

- Information flow: The company receives an order for 50,000 units.
- Material flow: Only 30,000 units are available.
- Financial flow: The company has limited working capital to purchase additional materials.

The company cannot simply decide to produce 50,000 units.

It needs to evaluate material availability, procurement lead time, production capacity, and financial constraints.

This is why SCM requires coordination among all three flows.

## 4.5 Understanding Supply Chain Performance

Paano malalaman kung mahusay ang supply chain?

Hindi sapat na tingnan lang kung marami ang nagawang products.

We need to measure the performance of the entire system.

Here are important performance considerations:

| Performance Area | What It Measures | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Customer Service | Ability to satisfy customer requirements | Complete orders |
| Delivery Performance | Reliability of deliveries | On-time delivery |
| Inventory Availability | Availability of required materials and products | No stockouts |
| Lead Time | Time required to complete a process | Supplier delivery time |
| Supply Chain Cost | Total cost of supply chain activities | Procurement and logistics cost |
| Inventory Investment | Capital tied up in inventory | Raw materials and FG value |
| Operational Efficiency | How effectively resources are utilized | Production output per hour |
| Responsiveness | Ability to react to demand or supply changes | Responding to sudden orders |
| Reliability | Consistency in fulfilling commitments | Predictable deliveries |

### Supply Chain Trade-offs

Now, let's introduce an important professional concept: **Trade-offs**.

A trade-off occurs when improving one performance area may negatively affect another.

Hindi lahat ng business objectives puwedeng i-maximize simultaneously.

Let's examine some examples.

**Example 1: Inventory vs. Customer Service**

A company wants to improve product availability.

Option A: Maintain very low inventory.

- Lower storage costs.
- Higher risk of stockouts.
- Potentially lower customer service.

Option B: Maintain higher inventory.

- Better product availability.
- Higher storage and carrying costs.
- More capital tied up in stock.

The company must determine the appropriate balance.

**Example 2: Transportation Cost vs. Delivery Speed**

A company needs to deliver products to an overseas customer.

Option A: Sea freight.

- Generally lower transportation cost.
- Longer transit time.

Option B: Air freight.

- Generally faster delivery.
- Higher transportation cost.

If the customer urgently needs the products, air freight may be justified despite its higher cost.

**Example 3: Production Efficiency vs. Inventory**

A manufacturing company wants to maximize production efficiency.

It decides to produce 100,000 units in one large batch.

However, customer demand is only 40,000 units.

Potential result:
- Efficient production run.
- Excess finished goods inventory.
- Increased storage costs.
- Risk of obsolete products.

This demonstrates why maximizing production output alone does not necessarily maximize overall supply chain performance.

### The Bigger Principle: End-to-End Optimization

A company should evaluate the total supply chain rather than optimizing individual departments independently.

For example:

Procurement may reduce purchasing costs by ordering materials in bulk.

However, if the additional materials create excessive inventory, the overall business may spend more on storage, handling, and working capital.

**Local optimization** means improving one department or activity.

**End-to-end optimization** means improving the performance of the entire supply chain.

The latter is the broader objective of SCM.

# 5. Practical Examples and Applications

Let's now connect everything to an actual manufacturing environment.

## 5.1 Illustrative Electronic Components Manufacturer

Imagine a company called ABC Electronics.

Its business is manufacturing electronic components used by other electronics manufacturers.

Its customers include companies that produce:
- Smartphones.
- Computers.
- Automotive electronics.
- Industrial equipment.

ABC Electronics receives customer forecasts and purchase orders, purchases raw materials, manufactures products, and delivers finished components.

### Complete Supply Chain Journey

1. **Suppliers provide raw materials**

   *Supplier Management*

   Ceramic powder, metals, solvents, packaging materials, and other production requirements are provided by suppliers.

2. **Procurement purchases materials**

   *Procurement*

   The purchasing team issues purchase orders based on material requirements and supplier lead times.

3. **Materials arrive and are stored**

   *Warehousing / Inbound Logistics*

   Materials undergo receiving, inspection, inventory recording, and storage.

4. **Manufacturing converts materials into products**

   *Production*

   Production processes transform raw materials into electronic components.

5. **Finished products undergo quality inspection**

   *Quality Control*

   Products are tested to ensure compliance with specifications.

6. **Products are stored as finished goods**

   *Finished Goods Warehousing*

   Accepted products are recorded and made available for customer orders.

7. **Orders are processed and prepared for shipment**

   *Order Fulfillment*

   Customer orders are checked, allocated, picked, packed, and prepared.

8. **Logistics delivers products**

   *Outbound Logistics*

   Products are transported to the customer's designated location.

9. **Customer demand and feedback return to planning**

   *Feedback and Continuous Improvement*

   Orders, actual consumption, forecasts, delivery performance, and returns become inputs for future planning.

## 5.2 Stage-by-Stage Operational Analysis

| Stage | Responsible Department | Required Information | Potential Problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supplier provision | Supplier management | Material specifications, supplier capacity | Material shortage |
| Procurement | Purchasing | Purchase requirements, lead times, costs | Late purchasing |
| Receiving | Warehouse, Quality | Delivery documents, quantity, quality specs | Incorrect or defective materials |
| Manufacturing | Production | Production schedule, BOM, capacity | Machine breakdown |
| Quality inspection | Quality Control | Product specifications, test results | High defect rate |
| FG storage | Warehouse | Available inventory, lot status | Storage capacity limitation |
| Order fulfillment | Customer service, warehouse | Customer orders, allocation details | Incorrect shipment |
| Delivery | Logistics | Shipment schedules, destination | Transportation delay |
| Customer feedback | Sales, Quality, Planning | Demand, returns, complaints | Poor demand visibility |

### Let's examine how one problem affects the entire supply chain.

**Scenario: Supplier Delivery Delay**

ABC Electronics requires 5,000 kg of ceramic powder.

The supplier originally committed to deliver on November 10.

However, due to a production issue at the supplier's facility, delivery is delayed until November 20.

What happens?

1. Procurement identifies that the material will not arrive as scheduled.
2. Warehouse cannot receive the required quantity.
3. Production planning discovers that the material shortage will affect the production schedule.
4. Manufacturing may need to delay or reschedule production.
5. Finished goods availability may decrease.
6. Customer orders may be delayed.
7. Sales may need to communicate revised delivery commitments.
8. Financial performance may be affected by lost sales, expedited shipping, or other additional costs.

Notice something important.

The original problem happened at the supplier's facility.

Pero ang consequence ay puwedeng umabot hanggang customer delivery and financial performance.

**This is why supply chain problems must be analyzed beyond the department where they originate.**

## 5.3 Departmental Coordination

Let's identify how the departments work together.

| Department | Primary Responsibility | Supply Chain Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | Customer requirements and orders | Demand visibility |
| Demand Planning | Forecasting customer demand | Demand plan |
| Supply Planning | Evaluating available supply | Supply plan |
| Procurement | Purchasing materials | Material availability |
| Production Planning | Scheduling production | Production readiness |
| Manufacturing | Producing goods | Finished product supply |
| Quality | Ensuring product compliance | Product quality |
| Warehouse | Managing inventory | Inventory visibility |
| Logistics | Moving products | Delivery performance |
| Finance | Financial planning and control | Cost and profitability visibility |

Important distinction:

Each department has its own responsibilities, but their decisions affect one another.

For example:

- Sales increases the forecast.
- Demand planning communicates the change.
- Supply planning evaluates available capacity.
- Procurement evaluates additional material requirements.
- Manufacturing assesses production feasibility.
- Finance evaluates the financial implications.

This is a coordinated supply chain response.

# 6. Formulas and Calculations

For this introductory topic, hindi natin kailangan ng maraming formulas.

Ang primary objective ay maintindihan muna ang SCM structure and relationships.

However, may ilang basic calculations na useful para makita kung paano sinusukat ang supply chain performance.

## 6.1 Lead Time

Lead time measures the time required to complete a process.

For a simple procurement example:

**Procurement Lead Time = Receipt Date − Purchase Order Date**

Illustrative data:

- Purchase order date: November 1.
- Receipt date: November 21.

21 − 1 = 20 days

**Result: 20 days**

Interpretation:

The company needs to account for approximately 20 days of procurement lead time when planning its material requirements, based on this illustrative transaction.

Note: Sa actual business, the definition may include supplier processing, transit, receiving, and inspection, depending on the company's lead-time policy.

## 6.2 On-Time Delivery (OTD)

OTD measures the percentage of deliveries completed on or before the agreed delivery date, subject to the company's definition.

**OTD = (On-Time Deliveries / Total Deliveries) × 100**

Example:

ABC Electronics completed 100 deliveries.

Out of these, 92 were on time.

OTD = (92 / 100) × 100

**OTD = 92%**

Interpretation:

92% of the deliveries met their agreed delivery dates.

The remaining 8% were late.

Why is this important?

Because late deliveries can indicate problems in production planning, material availability, inventory, or logistics.

## 6.3 Inventory Availability

A simple inventory coverage calculation can help determine whether available inventory is sufficient for expected demand.

**Days of Supply = Available Inventory / Average Daily Demand**

Example:

- Available finished goods = 30,000 units.
- Average daily demand = 3,000 units.

DOS = 30,000 / 3,000

**DOS = 10 days**

Interpretation:

The company has approximately 10 days of inventory coverage, assuming demand remains at the stated average and the inventory is usable and available.

This does not automatically mean 10 days is sufficient. We still need to consider replenishment lead time, safety stock, and demand variability.

## 6.4 Supply Chain Cost Consideration

One useful principle is that supply chain costs must be evaluated collectively.

For example:

| Cost Component | Illustrative Monthly Cost |
|---|---:|
| Procurement and inbound costs | ₱500,000 |
| Manufacturing-related costs | ₱1,200,000 |
| Warehousing costs | ₱200,000 |
| Transportation and distribution | ₱300,000 |
| Inventory carrying costs | ₱150,000 |
| **Total** | **₱2,350,000** |

**Total SCM Cost = Sum of Supply Chain Cost Components**

These figures are illustrative, not industry benchmarks.

The important lesson is that reducing one cost component does not necessarily reduce the total supply chain cost.

For example, bulk purchasing may reduce material unit prices but increase inventory carrying costs.

# 7. Business Perspective and Decision-Making

Now let's connect SCM to your role as an S&OP professional.

## 7.1 What Does an S&OP Analyst Need to Understand About SCM?

An S&OP analyst does not necessarily manage every supply chain operation directly.

However, the analyst must understand how each activity affects the company's ability to satisfy demand.

Imagine that the sales forecast increases by 20%.

As an S&OP analyst, you should not simply report that demand increased.

You need to understand its implications.

| Area | Questions an S&OP Analyst Should Ask |
|---|---|
| Demand | Is the increase supported by customer requirements? |
| Procurement | Can suppliers provide additional materials? |
| Inventory | Do we have enough raw materials and finished goods? |
| Manufacturing | Can production support the increased demand? |
| Capacity | Are machines, labor, and production hours sufficient? |
| Logistics | Can the additional volume be delivered on time? |
| Finance | What are the additional costs and revenue implications? |

This is where SCM knowledge becomes essential.

## 7.2 What Is the Relationship Between SCM and S&OP?

Let's establish this clearly.

**Supply Chain Management is the broader management discipline. S&OP is a cross-functional business planning and decision-making process that supports the coordination of the supply chain.**

Think of SCM as the overall system and S&OP as one of the important mechanisms used to align that system.

### Relationship between SCM and S&OP

**SUPPLY CHAIN MANAGEMENT**

Overall management of the supply chain:

- Demand management
- Procurement
- Manufacturing
- Inventory management
- Logistics
- Distribution
- Returns management
- Supplier management

↓

*Planning and cross-functional coordination*

**SALES & OPERATIONS PLANNING (S&OP)**

Aligns demand, supply, operations, and financial objectives:

1. Demand Plan
2. Supply Plan
3. Reconciled Business Plan
4. Management Decisions and Execution Alignment

Note: Simplified conceptual illustration ito. S&OP interacts with the wider supply chain and does not replace its operational planning and execution processes.

## 7.3 How Does S&OP Support SCM?

Let's use a practical example.

ABC Electronics expects the following demand:

| Month | Forecast Demand |
|---|---:|
| January | 100,000 |
| February | 120,000 |
| March | 150,000 |

The demand is increasing every month.

The S&OP process helps the company evaluate whether its supply chain can support this increase.

### Step 1: Demand Review

Sales and demand planning evaluate the expected demand.

Questions:
- Is the forecast realistic?
- Are customer orders increasing?
- Are there new customers?
- Is the increase temporary or sustained?

### Step 2: Supply Review

Supply planning evaluates the company's ability to satisfy demand.

Questions:
- Are raw materials sufficient?
- Can suppliers support the increase?
- Is manufacturing capacity sufficient?
- Can inventory cover the requirements?

### Step 3: Reconciliation

The company compares the demand plan against the supply plan.

Example:

| Month | Demand | Available Supply | Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| January | 100,000 | 110,000 | +10,000 |
| February | 120,000 | 110,000 | -10,000 |
| March | 150,000 | 120,000 | -30,000 |

For simplicity:

**Supply Gap = Available Supply − Demand**

A negative result indicates a potential shortage.

A positive result indicates supply exceeds the stated demand.

### Step 4: Evaluate Alternatives

For February and March, the company has potential supply shortages.

Possible alternatives:

- Increase production capacity.
- Add production shifts.
- Negotiate additional supplier capacity.
- Purchase materials earlier.
- Use existing finished goods inventory.
- Evaluate demand prioritization.
- Negotiate delivery schedules with customers.

### Step 5: Management Decision

The management team evaluates the alternatives based on:

- Customer impact.
- Revenue.
- Production costs.
- Inventory investment.
- Supplier risks.
- Operational feasibility.

The result is an agreed business plan.

**This is how S&OP supports SCM: it helps the organization identify imbalances, evaluate alternatives, and align cross-functional decisions before execution.**

## 7.4 SCM vs. S&OP: Important Distinctions

| Area | Supply Chain Management | Sales & Operations Planning |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Overall supply chain | Cross-functional planning and alignment |
| Primary purpose | Manage end-to-end supply chain performance | Balance demand and supply with business objectives |
| Activities | Planning, sourcing, production, logistics, returns | Demand review, supply review, reconciliation, management decisions |
| Timeframe | Strategic, tactical, and operational | Primarily medium-term, depending on company design |
| Participants | Multiple supply chain functions and external partners | Sales, operations, supply chain, finance, and management |
| Output | Products and services delivered to customers | Aligned and approved business plan |

A simple way to remember:

- SCM manages the broader supply chain.
- S&OP aligns the business plans that guide the supply chain.

They are not competing processes. They complement each other.

## 7.5 Why Is SCM Knowledge Important for Your S&OP Role?

As an S&OP analyst, you will encounter situations where numbers may appear correct individually but do not make sense collectively.

Example:

- Sales forecast: 150,000 units.
- Procurement plan: Materials for 100,000 units.
- Production capacity: 120,000 units.
- Finished goods inventory: 10,000 units.

An inexperienced analyst might simply consolidate these numbers.

A knowledgeable S&OP analyst asks:

1. Can the available materials support the production plan?
2. Can production capacity meet the demand?
3. Can existing inventory cover the supply gap?
4. What is the actual supply constraint?
5. What options are available?
6. What is the financial impact of each option?

That is the transition from being a reporting analyst to becoming a business planning professional.

# 8. Key Takeaways

Before moving to the knowledge check, let's consolidate the most important concepts.

| Concept | What You Should Remember |
|---|---|
| Supply Chain | The network involved in moving a product from raw materials to the end customer |
| Supply Chain Management | Coordinating and managing the activities and flows within that network |
| SCM Objectives | Satisfy customer requirements while managing costs and operational performance |
| Planning | Determine what is needed, how much, and when |
| Procurement | Obtain the materials and services required |
| Manufacturing | Convert materials into finished products |
| Logistics | Store, transport, and deliver products |
| Returns Management | Manage products moving back through the supply chain |
| Supply Chain Participants | Suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, retailers, customers, and service providers |
| Material Flow | Movement of physical goods |
| Information Flow | Exchange of forecasts, orders, schedules, and other data |
| Financial Flow | Payments, costs, revenue, and financial transactions |
| Trade-offs | Improving one performance area may negatively affect another |
| End-to-End Optimization | Improve the overall supply chain rather than individual departments only |
| S&OP | A planning and coordination process that aligns demand, supply, and business objectives within the wider SCM environment |

### Five fundamental principles to remember

1. **A supply chain is a network, not just a transportation process.**
2. **SCM involves the coordination of materials, information, and financial flows.**
3. **Every supply chain activity affects other activities.**
4. **The goal is not simply to minimize costs or maximize inventory availability, but to balance overall business performance.**
5. **S&OP supports SCM by aligning demand, supply, operations, and financial decisions.**
