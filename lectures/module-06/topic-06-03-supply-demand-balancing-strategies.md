# Module 6 – Topic 6.3: Supply-Demand Balancing Strategies

**Main learning objective:** Matutunan kung paano mag-develop, mag-compare, at mag-recommend ng business strategies kapag hindi nagtutugma ang customer demand at available supply.

- **Topic 6.2 – Identifying Supply Gaps:** Paano natutukoy, nasusukat, at ini-investigate ang shortages at capacity constraints.

Ngayon, papasok tayo sa next logical step:

**Topic 6.3 – Supply-Demand Balancing Strategies**

Kung nalaman mong kulang ang production capacity para matugunan ang customer demand, ano ang gagawin mo?

May several possible solutions:

1. Mag-overtime ang production.
2. Magdagdag ng production shift.
3. Mag-subcontract sa ibang manufacturer.
4. Gumamit ng alternative production line.
5. Mag-expedite ng raw materials.
6. Makipag-negotiate sa customers para baguhin ang delivery schedule.

Pero hindi lahat ng solutions ay magandang business decisions.

Halimbawa, puwedeng ma-meet ang customer demand through overtime, pero kung sobrang taas naman ng labor cost, baka mas malaki pa ang additional expense kaysa sa benefit.

Ang objective ng S&OP ay hindi lamang magkaroon ng enough supply.

Ang objective ay makabuo ng **feasible, cost-effective, and commercially acceptable supply-demand plan.**

**Illustrative business situation**

- Possible delayed customer orders.
- Possible revenue loss.
- Possible customer dissatisfaction.
- Possible backlog sa following planning periods.

Pero kung mag-overtime ka, mag-expedite ng materials, o mag-adjust ng delivery schedules, maaari mong mabawasan o tuluyang ma-resolve ang shortage.

Dito pumapasok ang supply-demand balancing.

## 2. Layman's Explanation

### 2.1 Understanding supply-demand balancing through a simple example

Imagine na may maliit kang bakery.

Every day, may customers na bumibili ng bread.

- Customer demand: 1,000 pieces.
- Normal production capacity: 800 pieces.
- Available inventory: 100 pieces.

Question: Kaya mo bang matugunan ang 1,000 pieces na demand?

Let's calculate:

\[
800+100=900
\]

Mayroon kang 900 pieces available.

\[
1,000-900=100
\]

May shortage kang 100 pieces.

Now, ano ang possible solutions?

| Possible solution | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Overtime | Magpagawa ng additional 100 pieces. |
| Additional shift | Magdagdag ng production time para makagawa ng additional bread. |
| Outsourcing | Bumili ng 100 pieces mula sa ibang bakery. |
| Inventory utilization | Gamitin ang available inventory na 100 pieces. |
| Demand adjustment | Makipag-usap sa customers kung puwedeng ilipat ang ibang orders sa susunod na araw. |

Notice something important?

Hindi iisa ang solution sa supply shortage.

Maraming possible approaches, at bawat approach ay may corresponding cost, limitation, at business impact.

### 2.2 The difference between identifying and resolving a supply gap

Ito ang isang importanteng distinction.

**Identifying a supply gap** means nalaman mo na may problema.

**Resolving a supply gap** means gumawa ka ng action para ma-address ang problema.

| Activity | Example |
|---|---|
| Identify | Demand is 10,000 units, but supply is only 8,000. |
| Investigate | Production capacity is limited due to machine downtime. |
| Develop options | Overtime, subcontracting, inventory allocation. |
| Evaluate | Compare cost, feasibility, and customer impact. |
| Decide | Management approves overtime and inventory reallocation. |
| Execute | Production and supply chain implement the approved plan. |
| Monitor | Check whether the shortage was resolved. |

**Important:** Hindi sapat na sabihin ng S&OP analyst na may shortage na 2,000 units.

Kailangan din niyang maipakita kung ano ang possible solutions at kung ano ang consequences ng bawat decision.

## 3. Technical Explanation

### 3.1 Formal definition

**Supply-Demand Balancing** is the process of evaluating and adjusting demand requirements, supply capabilities, inventory positions, and operational resources to develop a feasible plan that meets business objectives.

Sa mas simpleng technical interpretation:

It is the process of determining how a company can align its available and planned supply with expected demand through operational, inventory, sourcing, and commercial adjustments.

May dalawang primary approaches:

1. **Supply-side adjustments:** Dagdagan o i-optimize ang available supply.
2. **Demand-side adjustments:** I-adjust ang timing, quantity, configuration, or prioritization ng demand.

### 3.2 The relationship between demand, supply, inventory, capacity, and customer service

Hindi independent ang mga ito. Magkakaugnay sila.

| Component | Role in balancing |
|---|---|
| Demand | Tells us how much customers require and when they need it. |
| Supply | Represents how much product can be made, sourced, or delivered. |
| Inventory | Provides a buffer between demand and production or replenishment. |
| Capacity | Determines how much production can realistically be completed. |
| Customer service | Measures how effectively the company fulfills customer requirements. |

Halimbawa:

Kung tumaas ang demand, pero fixed ang production capacity, maaaring magkaroon ng shortage.

Kung may available inventory, puwedeng gamitin iyon para ma-cover ang shortage.

Pero kung masyadong maraming inventory ang gagamitin, baka magkaroon naman ng shortage sa future planning periods.

Kaya hindi lang current month ang dapat tingnan.

**Dapat pinag-aaralan din ang epekto ng balancing decision sa succeeding periods.**

### 3.3 Important technical terminology

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Capacity Flexibility | Ability ng operation na mag-adjust ng production output. |
| Overtime | Additional working hours beyond normal schedule. |
| Subcontracting | Pagpapagawa ng products sa external manufacturer. |
| Expediting | Pagpapabilis ng material delivery or procurement. |
| Inventory Reallocation | Paglipat ng available stock sa ibang location, customer, or requirement. |
| Backlog | Unfulfilled customer orders na naghihintay ng fulfillment. |
| Build-ahead | Producing inventory earlier than the period when demand occurs. |
| Inventory Drawdown | Using existing inventory to satisfy demand, reducing stock levels. |
| Demand Shaping | Influencing demand timing, quantity, or product mix through commercial actions. |
| Capacity Constraint | Limitation that prevents production from reaching the required output. |
| Trade-off | A decision where improving one business objective may negatively affect another. |

## 4. Detailed Discussion: Supply-Side Strategies

Supply-side strategies focus on increasing, recovering, or optimizing the company's ability to provide products.

Ang central question:

**"How can we increase available supply to meet customer demand?"**

### 4.1 Increasing production through overtime

**Concept:** Magdagdag ng working hours beyond the normal production schedule para makagawa ng additional units.

Example:

- Normal production: 500 units/day
- Normal working hours: 8 hours
- Additional overtime: 2 hours

Assuming constant production rate:

\[
500 \times \frac{10}{8}=625
\]

Additional output:

\[
625-500=125\text{ units}
\]

**Advantages**
- Relatively mabilis i-implement.
- Hindi na kailangan mag-hire ng permanent employees.
- Puwedeng gamitin para sa temporary demand spikes.
- Existing production equipment and processes can be utilized.

**Limitations**
- Additional labor and utility costs.
- Employee fatigue and possible quality issues.
- Machine maintenance requirements.
- Hindi sustainable kung long-term ang capacity shortage.
- May legal and company-policy limitations sa working hours.

**S&OP consideration:** Overtime is useful when the shortage is temporary and the additional production requirement is achievable within available machine, labor, and material constraints.

### 4.2 Adding additional production shifts

**Concept:** Magdagdag ng production shift para mas maraming oras na operational ang manufacturing facility.

Example:

| Production schedule | Operating hours |
|---|---:|
| One shift | 8 hours |
| Two shifts | 16 hours |
| Three shifts | 24 hours |

Kung ang production rate ay 100 units/hour:

| Shifts | Theoretical output |
|---|---:|
| 1 shift | 800 units |
| 2 shifts | 1,600 units |
| 3 shifts | 2,400 units |

Note: Theoretical output ito. Actual output may be lower due to downtime, changeovers, breaks, maintenance, and efficiency losses.

**Advantages:**
- Higher production capacity.
- More sustainable than continuously extending one employee's working hours.
- Better utilization of existing equipment.

**Limitations:**
- Additional operators and supervisors.
- Additional utilities and operating expenses.
- Training and qualification requirements.
- Maintenance scheduling becomes more complicated.

**Important distinction:**

Overtime extends the working hours of an existing shift or workforce.

Additional shifts extend the operating schedule by adding another production team or operating period.

### 4.3 Subcontracting and outsourcing

**Concept:** Magpagawa ng products or components sa external manufacturer para madagdagan ang available supply.

Example:

Your factory can produce 10,000 units, but demand is 12,000 units.

Instead of expanding your factory immediately, you subcontract 2,000 units.

**Advantages:**
- Additional supply without immediately investing in new machines.
- Useful for temporary demand increases.
- Allows the company to focus on its core manufacturing processes.

**Limitations:**
- Higher unit cost may occur.
- External supplier quality must be controlled.
- Intellectual property and confidentiality risks.
- Supplier capacity and delivery reliability.
- Additional inspection and qualification requirements.

**Manufacturing example:**

A company manufactures electronic components internally but outsources selected mechanical components during periods of high demand.

Hindi ibig sabihin na lahat ng products ay puwedeng i-outsource. Some products require specialized equipment, validated processes, or customer approval.

### 4.4 Using alternative production lines or facilities

**Concept:** Ilipat ang production requirement sa ibang available production line or manufacturing facility.

Example:

| Production line | Current utilization | Available capacity |
|---|---:|---:|
| Line A | 100% | 0 units |
| Line B | 70% | 3,000 units |
| Line C | 90% | 1,000 units |

Kung may shortage sa Line A, puwedeng ilipat ang qualified products sa Line B.

**Advantages:**
- Utilizes idle capacity.
- May avoid the need for overtime.
- May shorten production lead time.

**Limitations:**
- Not all lines can manufacture all products.
- Different machine capabilities and process parameters.
- Qualification and validation requirements.
- Possible transportation costs between facilities.

**Important term: Production Flexibility**

Ito ang ability ng manufacturing system na mag-produce ng different products, configurations, or volumes using available production resources.

### 4.5 Expediting raw materials and supplier deliveries

**Concept:** Pabilisin ang delivery ng raw materials para hindi ma-delay ang production.

Example:

- Required raw material: 5,000 kg
- Available material: 3,500 kg
- Material shortage: 1,500 kg
- Normal supplier lead time: 20 days
- Customer delivery requirement: 12 days

Possible actions:

- Request partial delivery.
- Use air freight instead of sea freight.
- Purchase from an alternative qualified supplier.
- Negotiate supplier priority.
- Expedite existing purchase orders.

**Advantages:**
- Can unlock production capacity that is currently idle due to material shortages.
- Helps protect customer delivery commitments.
- May avoid production shutdowns.

**Limitations:**
- Premium freight and supplier charges.
- Alternative suppliers may require qualification.
- Expediting does not guarantee material availability.
- Can create inventory imbalances in future periods.

**Important concept:**

Sometimes, the factory has available production capacity but cannot manufacture because raw materials are unavailable.

Therefore:

**Available capacity does not automatically mean available supply.**

### 4.6 Reallocating available inventory

**Concept:** Ilipat ang available finished goods inventory from one location, customer allocation, or requirement to another.

Example:

| Warehouse | Available inventory |
|---|---:|
| Warehouse A | 2,000 units |
| Warehouse B | 500 units |
| Warehouse C | 1,500 units |

Warehouse A has an urgent shortage of 1,000 units for customer orders, but Warehouse B and C have excess stocks.

Possible action: transfer 1,000 units from Warehouse C to Warehouse A.

**Advantages:**
- Faster than manufacturing new products.
- Uses existing inventory.
- May prevent stockouts without increasing production.

**Limitations:**
- Transportation and handling costs.
- Inventory may be committed to other customers.
- Product compatibility and shelf-life considerations.
- May transfer the shortage from one location to another.

### 4.7 Building inventory ahead of future demand

**Concept:** Produce products earlier than their actual demand period to establish sufficient inventory before demand increases.

Example:

| Month | Forecast demand |
|---|---:|
| January | 5,000 |
| February | 5,000 |
| March | 9,000 |

Kung expected na tataas ang demand in March, puwedeng mag-produce ng additional units during January or February, assuming may available capacity.

**Advantages:**
- Provides inventory buffer during peak demand.
- Reduces pressure on production during high-demand periods.
- Helps protect customer service.

**Limitations:**
- Higher inventory holding costs.
- Risk of obsolete or slow-moving inventory.
- Requires storage space.
- Ties up working capital.
- Risky kapag inaccurate ang forecast.

**Important:** Hindi automatic na magandang decision ang pag-build ng inventory.

Kailangan may reasonable demand visibility and sufficient storage and financial capacity.

### 4.8 Summary of supply-side strategies

| Strategy | Best used when | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Overtime | Temporary production shortage | Labor fatigue and cost |
| Additional shifts | Sustained short-term capacity need | Workforce and operating cost |
| Subcontracting | Internal capacity is insufficient | External quality and cost |
| Alternative lines | Other qualified lines have idle capacity | Equipment compatibility |
| Expedite materials | Materials are the production constraint | Premium procurement cost |
| Reallocate inventory | Stock exists elsewhere | May create shortages elsewhere |
| Build-ahead | Future demand increase is predictable | Holding cost and obsolescence |

## 5. Detailed Discussion: Demand-Side Strategies

Supply-side strategies increase available supply.

Demand-side strategies, on the other hand, manage the amount, timing, or configuration ng demand that the company needs to fulfill.

Ang central question:

**"How can we manage demand so that available supply can meet the most important customer requirements?"**

### 5.1 Demand prioritization and allocation

**Concept:** Decide kung aling customer orders or products ang dapat unahin kapag insufficient ang supply.

Example:

Available supply: 5,000 units.

| Customer | Requested quantity |
|---|---:|
| Customer A | 3,000 |
| Customer B | 2,000 |
| Customer C | 2,000 |
| Total | 7,000 |

Shortage: 2,000 units.

Hindi sapat ang supply para sa lahat ng requested orders.

Possible prioritization considerations:

- Customer contractual commitments.
- Order due dates.
- Strategic customer importance.
- Revenue contribution.
- Profitability.
- Penalty for late delivery.
- Available alternative products.

Hindi dapat basta unahin ang pinakamalaking customer without considering the broader business impact.

We'll discuss this in greater depth in Topic 6.4.

### 5.2 Order rescheduling and delivery negotiation

**Concept:** Makipag-coordinate sa customers para baguhin ang original requested delivery dates.

Example:

| Customer order | Original delivery | Proposed delivery |
|---|---|---|
| Order A | March 5 | March 5 |
| Order B | March 7 | March 12 |
| Order C | March 8 | March 15 |

Sa halip na piliting i-produce lahat immediately, puwedeng i-reschedule ang selected orders.

**Advantages:**
- Reduces immediate production pressure.
- Allows the company to prioritize urgent orders.
- May reduce overtime and expediting costs.

**Limitations:**
- Customer approval is necessary.
- Potential contractual penalties.
- Customer satisfaction may decline.
- Delayed orders remain in the backlog.

Important: Rescheduling does not necessarily eliminate demand.

It changes when demand needs to be fulfilled.

### 5.3 Managing promotions and sales activities

**Concept:** I-adjust ang commercial activities para maiwasan ang additional demand during periods of constrained supply.

Example:

A company plans to launch a promotional campaign in March.

Expected additional demand:

- Normal forecast: 10,000 units
- Promotional demand: 3,000 units
- Total expected demand: 13,000 units

Available supply is only 11,000 units.

Possible action:

- Move the promotion to April.
- Reduce the promotional volume.
- Limit promotion to selected products.
- Adjust the campaign duration.

**Advantages:**
- Reduces demand spikes.
- Protects existing customer commitments.
- Allows supply planning to prepare for future demand.

**Limitations:**
- Potential lost sales opportunities.
- Marketing campaign changes.
- Competitive risks.
- Possible customer disappointment.

### 5.4 Product substitution and alternative configurations

**Concept:** Offer an alternative product or configuration that can satisfy the customer's requirements using available supply.

Example:

Customer requests Product A, but Product A is unavailable.

However, Product B has similar functionality and is available.

Possible solution: Offer Product B as an alternative.

But product substitution must consider:

- Technical compatibility.
- Customer specifications.
- Regulatory requirements.
- Quality approval.
- Commercial agreement.
- Product interchangeability.

**Manufacturing example:**

If a particular component specification is constrained, an approved alternative component may be used to continue production.

However, hindi puwedeng basta palitan ang raw material dahil lamang available ang alternative.

The substitute must satisfy engineering, quality, and customer requirements.

### 5.5 Customer prioritization

**Concept:** Determine which customers receive available supply first.

This may involve evaluating:

- Strategic account importance.
- Contractual obligations.
- Revenue and margin contribution.
- Customer service requirements.
- Long-term business relationships.

Example:

| Customer | Revenue contribution | Delivery urgency |
|---|---:|---|
| A | ₱5 million | High |
| B | ₱2 million | High |
| C | ₱8 million | Low |

Hindi automatic na Customer C ang priority dahil pinakamalaki ang revenue.

Kung may contractual delivery obligation si Customer A, mas malaking business risk ang hindi pagtugon sa commitment.

Customer prioritization requires cross-functional agreement, not an arbitrary decision by the production team alone.

### 5.6 Summary of demand-side strategies

| Strategy | Main objective | Potential disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| Demand allocation | Distribute limited supply | Some customers receive less |
| Order rescheduling | Move demand to later periods | Backlog and customer impact |
| Promotion management | Avoid demand spikes | Lost promotional opportunity |
| Product substitution | Use available alternatives | Approval and compatibility issues |
| Customer prioritization | Protect critical commitments | Commercial trade-offs |

## 6. Inventory and Production Strategies

Ngayon, pagsasamahin natin ang inventory and production planning concepts.

May three important approaches na kailangan mong maintindihan.

### 6.1 Level production strategy

**Concept:** Maintain relatively stable production output regardless of short-term fluctuations in demand.

Example:

| Month | Demand | Production |
|---|---:|---:|
| January | 5,000 | 7,000 |
| February | 9,000 | 7,000 |
| March | 8,000 | 7,000 |
| April | 4,000 | 7,000 |

Kapag mas mataas ang production kaysa demand, nadadagdagan ang inventory.

Kapag mas mataas ang demand kaysa production, ginagamit ang accumulated inventory.

**Advantages:**
- Stable workforce.
- More predictable production schedule.
- Less frequent changes in production volume.
- May improve equipment utilization.

**Disadvantages:**
- Inventory increases during low-demand periods.
- Storage and holding costs.
- Risk of obsolete inventory.
- May require working capital.

**Best suited for:** Manufacturing operations where production stability is valuable and inventory can be stored economically.

### 6.2 Chase production strategy

**Concept:** Adjust production output to closely follow demand fluctuations.

Example:

| Month | Demand | Production |
|---|---:|---:|
| January | 5,000 | 5,000 |
| February | 9,000 | 9,000 |
| March | 8,000 | 8,000 |
| April | 4,000 | 4,000 |

Production increases when demand increases and decreases when demand decreases.

**Advantages:**
- Lower finished goods inventory.
- Less inventory holding cost.
- Production is aligned with current demand.

**Disadvantages:**
- Frequent schedule changes.
- More difficult workforce planning.
- Potential overtime and idle time.
- Increased pressure on suppliers.
- Possible machine setup and changeover losses.

**Best suited for:** Operations where inventory holding is expensive and production capacity can adjust relatively easily.

### 6.3 Level versus chase production

| Criteria | Level production | Chase production |
|---|---|---|
| Production output | Relatively stable | Follows demand |
| Inventory | Usually higher | Usually lower |
| Workforce | More stable | May fluctuate |
| Overtime | May be lower | May be higher |
| Schedule stability | Higher | Lower |
| Inventory risk | Higher | Lower |
| Capacity flexibility | Less dependent | More dependent |

**Important:** Hindi ibig sabihin na level production is always better or chase production is always more efficient.

The best strategy depends on demand variability, capacity flexibility, inventory costs, labor availability, and customer requirements.

### 6.4 Inventory build-ahead and inventory drawdown

These two concepts are particularly important in balancing.

**Inventory build-ahead**

Produce more than current-period demand to prepare for future demand.

Example:

- Current demand: 5,000
- Production: 7,000
- Opening inventory: 1,000

Ending inventory:

\[
1,000+7,000-5,000=3,000
\]

**Inventory drawdown**

Use existing inventory to satisfy demand without producing the full quantity during the current period.

Example:

- Opening inventory: 3,000
- Current demand: 5,000
- Current production: 4,000

Ending inventory:

\[
3,000+4,000-5,000=2,000
\]

### 6.5 Managing backlog and excess supply

**Backlog** is the quantity of customer orders that remain unfulfilled.

Formula:

\[
Ending\ Backlog =
Opening\ Backlog + New\ Orders - Fulfilled\ Orders
\]

Example:

- Opening backlog: 500 units
- New orders: 2,000 units
- Fulfilled orders: 1,800 units

\[
500+2,000-1,800=700
\]

Ending backlog = 700 units.

Meanwhile, excess supply occurs when available supply exceeds demand.

Example:

- Available supply: 10,000
- Demand: 8,000

\[
10,000-8,000=2,000
\]

Excess supply = 2,000 units, assuming all supply is usable and not otherwise committed.

Possible actions:

- Reduce future production.
- Cancel or defer purchase orders where feasible.
- Reallocate stock.
- Build strategic inventory if justified.
- Adjust procurement plans.
- Review forecast accuracy.

**Important distinction:** Backlog and excess inventory can exist simultaneously across different products.

For example, Product A may be short by 1,000 units while Product B has 2,000 units of excess stock.

That is why S&OP must evaluate product-level imbalances, not only total company supply.

## 7. Evaluating Alternative Balancing Strategies

Ito ang isa sa pinakamahalagang skills ng S&OP analyst.

Hindi sapat na makapagbigay ka ng solution. Kailangan mong malaman kung alin ang may pinakamagandang overall business outcome.

### 7.1 Operational feasibility

First question:

**Can the operation realistically execute the proposed strategy?**

Consider:

- Machine capacity.
- Available labor.
- Raw material availability.
- Supplier capability.
- Production lead time.
- Quality and engineering approvals.
- Transportation availability.

Example:

Kung kailangan mong gumawa ng additional 5,000 units pero available machine capacity is only 1,000 units, hindi feasible ang plan unless may ibang production source.

### 7.2 Additional production and procurement costs

Evaluate the additional costs created by the balancing strategy.

Examples:

- Overtime premium.
- Additional shift expenses.
- Subcontracting premium.
- Premium freight.
- Additional raw material procurement.
- Additional inspection costs.

### 7.3 Inventory holding costs

Kapag nag-build ka ng additional inventory, may associated expenses.

Examples:

- Warehouse storage.
- Insurance.
- Material handling.
- Capital financing.
- Obsolescence risk.

Hindi free ang inventory kahit nasa warehouse lang ito.

### 7.4 Customer service and delivery impact

Evaluate whether the strategy protects customer delivery commitments.

Possible measures:

- On-time delivery.
- Order fulfillment rate.
- Backlog.
- Customer service level.
- Past-due orders.

### 7.5 Revenue and profitability implications

A strategy may improve production output but reduce profitability.

Example:

Additional production through outsourcing may generate sales revenue, but subcontracting costs may reduce gross margin.

Therefore, hindi dapat production volume lamang ang basis ng decision.

### 7.6 Short-term versus long-term solutions

| Short-term solution | Potential long-term improvement |
|---|---|
| Overtime | Improve production capacity |
| Expedited delivery | Improve supplier reliability |
| Subcontracting | Strategic sourcing agreement |
| Inventory reallocation | Improve inventory deployment |
| Demand rescheduling | Improve demand and capacity planning |
| Temporary additional shift | Invest in permanent capacity |

**Professional insight:**

A short-term balancing strategy addresses the immediate problem.

A long-term improvement addresses the underlying reason why the problem repeatedly occurs.

For example, kung every month ay nag-e-expedite ka ng raw materials, hindi sapat na patuloy kang magbayad ng premium freight.

You should investigate whether the root cause involves poor supplier performance, inaccurate material planning, insufficient safety stock, or procurement lead-time assumptions.

## 8. Formulas and Calculations

Dito natin iko-connect ang concepts sa actual numerical analysis na ginagamit ng S&OP analysts.

For consistency, gagamit tayo ng units per month unless otherwise stated.

### 8.1 Supply-demand gap before and after adjustments

**Formula:**

\[
Supply\ Gap = Available\ Supply - Demand
\]

Interpretation:

- Positive result = excess supply.
- Negative result = supply shortage.
- Zero = balanced.

Example:

| Item | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Demand | 10,000 units |
| Opening inventory | 1,000 units |
| Planned production | 7,000 units |
| Confirmed receipts | 500 units |
| Total available supply | 8,500 units |

Available supply:

\[
1,000+7,000+500=8,500
\]

Supply-demand gap:

\[
8,500-10,000=-1,500
\]

**Shortage: 1,500 units**

Now, suppose you implement:
- Additional production: 1,000 units
- Additional confirmed material receipts: 500 units

Adjusted supply:

\[
8,500+1,000+500=10,000
\]

Adjusted gap:

\[
10,000-10,000=0
\]

Balanced na ang supply and demand.

**Limitation:** This is an aggregate calculation. Dapat compatible ang additional production and inventory sa actual product requirements. Hindi puwedeng gamitin ang excess Product A inventory para i-cover ang Product B shortage unless an approved substitution exists.

### 8.2 Additional production requirements

**Formula:**

\[
Additional\ Production =
Demand - Available\ Supply
\]

Assuming no other adjustments are available.

Example:

- Demand: 15,000 units
- Opening inventory: 2,000 units
- Confirmed receipts: 1,000 units
- Planned production: 10,000 units

Available supply:

\[
2,000+1,000+10,000=13,000
\]

Additional production required:

\[
15,000-13,000=2,000
\]

You need another 2,000 units of production to cover the demand.

### 8.3 Additional capacity requirements

Capacity tells us how much production output can be achieved within a given period.

**Formula:**

\[
Additional\ Capacity =
Required\ Production - Available\ Capacity
\]

Example:

- Required production: 12,000 units
- Available production capacity: 10,000 units

\[
12,000-10,000=2,000
\]

Additional capacity required = 2,000 units.

Now, let's calculate the additional operating hours.

Assume:

- Production rate = 100 units/hour.
- Additional output required = 2,000 units.

\[
Additional\ Hours =
\frac{2,000}{100}
\]

\[
=20\ hours
\]

Kailangan mo ng 20 additional productive machine-hours.

**Important limitation:** This assumes constant production rate and no additional downtime, changeover losses, quality losses, or material constraints.

### 8.4 Inventory impact of alternative strategies

**Formula:**

\[
Ending\ Inventory =
Beginning\ Inventory + Receipts - Demand
\]

Where:

- Beginning Inventory = opening usable inventory.
- Receipts = production output plus incoming inventory.
- Demand = fulfilled customer requirements.

Example:

| Item | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Beginning inventory | 2,000 |
| Production | 8,000 |
| Incoming receipts | 1,000 |
| Demand fulfilled | 9,000 |

\[
EI=2,000+8,000+1,000-9,000
\]

\[
EI=2,000
\]

Ending inventory = 2,000 units.

This assumes there are no additional losses, scrap, quality holds, or other inventory adjustments.

### 8.5 Remaining backlog after supply allocation

**Formula:**

\[
Remaining\ Backlog =
Total\ Required\ Demand - Fulfilled\ Demand
\]

Example:

- Total demand: 10,000 units.
- Available supply allocated: 8,500 units.

\[
10,000-8,500=1,500
\]

Remaining backlog = 1,500 units.

For a multi-period analysis:

\[
Ending\ Backlog =
Opening\ Backlog + New\ Orders - Fulfilled\ Orders
\]

Remember na ang backlog ay demand na hindi pa fulfilled, not necessarily lost sales.

### 8.6 Incremental cost of balancing alternatives

**Formula:**

\[
Total\ Incremental\ Cost =
Production\ Premium +
Procurement\ Premium +
Freight +
Other\ Additional\ Costs
\]

Example:

Suppose you choose overtime and material expediting.

| Cost component | Additional cost |
|---|---:|
| Overtime premium | ₱40,000 |
| Material expediting | ₱15,000 |
| Additional utilities | ₱5,000 |
| Total | ₱60,000 |

\[
40,000+15,000+5,000=60,000
\]

Additional balancing cost = ₱60,000.

**Important distinction:** Incremental cost means additional cost caused by the balancing decision, not necessarily the full cost of manufacturing the products.

### 8.7 Demand fulfillment percentage

**Formula:**

\[
Demand\ Fulfillment\ \% =
\frac{Fulfilled\ Demand}{Total\ Demand}\times100
\]

Example:

- Total demand: 10,000 units.
- Fulfilled demand: 8,500 units.

\[
\frac{8,500}{10,000}\times100
=85\%
\]

Demand fulfillment = 85%.

Unfulfilled demand percentage:

\[
100\%-85\%=15\%
\]

**Important:** Demand fulfillment percentage is not automatically the same as On-Time Delivery (OTD).

You can fulfill 100% of orders eventually but still have poor on-time delivery if many orders are late.

### 8.8 Additional formula: Inventory holding cost

Useful ito kapag nag-e-evaluate ka ng inventory build-ahead strategy.

\[
Inventory\ Holding\ Cost =
Average\ Inventory \times Holding\ Cost\ per\ Unit
\]

For a monthly holding cost rate:

\[
Holding\ Cost =
Average\ Inventory \times Unit\ Cost \times Monthly\ Holding\ Rate
\]

Example:

- Average inventory: 2,000 units.
- Unit cost: ₱100.
- Monthly holding rate: 2%.

\[
2,000\times100\times0.02
\]

\[
=₱4,000
\]

Monthly inventory holding cost = ₱4,000.

This is an illustrative calculation. Actual company holding-cost methodology may include different cost components and accounting assumptions.

### 8.9 Formula summary

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|
| Supply-demand gap | Available Supply − Demand |
| Additional production | Demand − Available Supply |
| Additional capacity | Required Production − Available Capacity |
| Ending inventory | Beginning Inventory + Receipts − Demand |
| Remaining backlog | Opening Backlog + New Orders − Fulfilled Orders |
| Incremental balancing cost | Sum of additional costs |
| Demand fulfillment % | Fulfilled Demand ÷ Total Demand × 100 |
| Inventory holding cost | Average Inventory × Unit Cost × Holding Rate |

## 9. Practical Manufacturing Application

Now, let's put everything together.

Gagawa tayo ng realistic manufacturing case involving multiple products, demand fluctuations, limited production capacity, raw material shortages, and customer commitments.

This will be our main case study for Topic 6.3.

### 9.1 Business scenario

Imagine na nagtatrabaho ka as an S&OP Analyst sa isang electronic components manufacturing company.

The company manufactures three products:

- Product A
- Product B
- Product C

For the upcoming month, the sales team submitted the following demand forecast.

**Demand forecast**

| Product | Customer demand |
|---|---:|
| A | 6,000 units |
| B | 4,000 units |
| C | 3,000 units |
| **Total** | **13,000 units** |

However, the factory has limited production resources.

**Opening finished goods inventory**

| Product | Inventory |
|---|---:|
| A | 500 |
| B | 300 |
| C | 200 |
| **Total** | **1,000** |

**Available production capacity**

The factory has a normal monthly capacity of 10,000 units.

However, a raw material shortage limits Product B production.

| Product | Planned production |
|---|---:|
| A | 5,000 |
| B | 2,500 |
| C | 2,000 |
| **Total** | **9,500** |

Although the factory has 10,000 units of normal capacity, it can only produce 9,500 units because of the raw material constraint.

**Initial supply-demand position**

| Measure | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Total demand | 13,000 units |
| Available supply | 10,500 units |
| Supply shortage | 2,500 units |
| Initial fulfillment | 80.77% |

### 9.2 Product-level supply-demand analysis

Let's calculate the shortage per product.

| Product | Demand | Inventory | Production | Supply | Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 6,000 | 500 | 5,000 | 5,500 | -500 |
| B | 4,000 | 300 | 2,500 | 2,800 | -1,200 |
| C | 3,000 | 200 | 2,000 | 2,200 | -800 |
| **Total** | **13,000** | **1,000** | **9,500** | **10,500** | **-2,500** |

Observe:

- Product A has a shortage of 500 units.
- Product B has a shortage of 1,200 units.
- Product C has a shortage of 800 units.

Product B has the largest shortage because of its raw material constraint.

This is important because the balancing strategy must consider the individual product requirements.

Hindi sapat na sabihin na kulang ang company ng 2,500 units. Kailangan nating malaman kung anong products ang kulang.

### 9.3 Alternative Strategy 1: Overtime + Raw Material Expediting

**Proposed strategy:**

1. Expedite raw materials for Product B.
2. Recover 500 units of production that were previously restricted by material availability.
3. Implement overtime to produce another 1,000 units.

Additional production:

| Product | Existing production | Additional production | Adjusted production |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 5,000 | 500 | 5,500 |
| B | 2,500 | 500 | 3,000 |
| C | 2,000 | 500 | 2,500 |
| **Total** | **9,500** | **1,500** | **11,000** |

The additional 1,500 units consist of:

- 500 units from recovered normal capacity after material expediting.
- 1,000 units from overtime.

Adjusted available supply:

\[
1,000+11,000=12,000
\]

New supply-demand gap:

\[
12,000-13,000=-1,000
\]

**Remaining shortage: 1,000 units**

**Product-level results**

| Product | Demand | Adjusted supply | Remaining shortage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 6,000 | 6,000 | 0 |
| B | 4,000 | 3,300 | 700 |
| C | 3,000 | 2,700 | 300 |
| **Total** | **13,000** | **12,000** | **1,000** |

**Incremental cost assumptions**

| Cost component | Cost |
|---|---:|
| Overtime premium | ₱80,000 |
| Material expediting | ₱40,000 |
| Additional utilities | ₱10,000 |
| **Total** | **₱130,000** |

Demand fulfillment:

\[
\frac{12,000}{13,000}\times100
=92.31\%
\]

**Analysis:**

Advantages:
- Increases internal production.
- Improves fulfillment from 80.77% to 92.31%.
- Protects more customer commitments.
- Recovers capacity that would otherwise remain unused.

Disadvantages:
- Requires additional labor and procurement costs.
- Still leaves 1,000 units unfulfilled.
- Depends on supplier ability to deliver the raw material on time.
- Additional overtime may affect productivity and quality.

### 9.4 Alternative Strategy 2: Subcontracting

**Proposed strategy:**

Instead of increasing internal production further, the company will subcontract 1,500 units to a qualified external manufacturer.

Subcontracting allocation:

| Product | Internal supply | Subcontracted supply | Total available |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 5,500 | 500 | 6,000 |
| B | 2,800 | 700 | 3,500 |
| C | 2,200 | 300 | 2,500 |
| **Total** | **10,500** | **1,500** | **12,000** |

Remaining shortage:

\[
13,000-12,000=1,000
\]

Demand fulfillment:

\[
\frac{12,000}{13,000}\times100=92.31\%
\]

**Incremental cost assumptions**

Assume subcontracting creates an additional premium of ₱130 per unit compared with the normal internal manufacturing cost.

\[
1,500\times130=₱195,000
\]

Additional inspection and coordination:

\[
₱15,000
\]

Total incremental cost:

\[
195,000+15,000=₱210,000
\]

**Analysis:**

Advantages:
- Less pressure on internal production capacity.
- Can be useful when demand exceeds internal manufacturing capability.
- May avoid excessive overtime.

Disadvantages:
- Higher incremental cost.
- External production quality must be controlled.
- Supplier lead time and qualification are critical.
- Still leaves 1,000 units unfulfilled.

Notice that the fulfillment percentage is identical to Alternative 1, but the cost is different.

This illustrates why S&OP should evaluate both operational performance and financial impact.

### 9.5 Alternative Strategy 3: Demand Rescheduling

**Proposed strategy:**

Instead of trying to produce the entire 13,000-unit requirement, negotiate with selected customers to move 2,500 units of demand to the next month.

The existing supply remains:

\[
10,500\ units
\]

Revised current-month demand:

\[
13,000-2,500=10,500
\]

Adjusted supply-demand gap:

\[
10,500-10,500=0
\]

**Revised demand fully covered**

**Demand rescheduling allocation**

| Product | Original demand | Deferred demand | Revised demand |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 6,000 | 500 | 5,500 |
| B | 4,000 | 1,200 | 2,800 |
| C | 3,000 | 800 | 2,200 |
| **Total** | **13,000** | **2,500** | **10,500** |

Now, all current-month demand can be covered by available supply.

But be careful with the interpretation.

The original due-date fulfillment is still:

\[
\frac{10,500}{13,000}\times100=80.77\%
\]

The revised schedule fulfillment is:

\[
\frac{10,500}{10,500}\times100=100\%
\]

These are different measurements.

We achieved 100% fulfillment against the revised schedule, but 2,500 units were not delivered on their original requested dates.

**Incremental cost assumptions**

Assume:
- Customer coordination and rescheduling cost: ₱10,000.
- Additional next-month delivery and handling premium: ₱20 per deferred unit.

\[
2,500\times20=₱50,000
\]

Total:

\[
10,000+50,000=₱60,000
\]

**Analysis:**

Advantages:
- Lowest incremental cost among the three alternatives.
- No additional internal production requirement.
- No subcontracting qualification requirement.
- Avoids additional inventory buildup.

Disadvantages:
- Customer commitments are changed.
- Delayed revenue recognition or shipment may occur, depending on the contract and accounting rules.
- Requires customer acceptance.
- May increase next month's production pressure.
- Backlog or rescheduled demand must be monitored.

Important: This strategy does not eliminate demand. It transfers 2,500 units of demand into the next planning period.

### 9.6 Comparing the three strategies

**Illustrative comparison**

| Evaluation | Alternative 1 | Alternative 2 | Alternative 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Strategy | Overtime + expedite | Subcontract | Reschedule |
| Current supply | 12,000 | 12,000 | 10,500 |
| Original demand | 13,000 | 13,000 | 13,000 |
| Original-date fulfillment | 92.31% | 92.31% | 80.77% |
| Remaining shortage | 1,000 | 1,000 | 2,500 deferred |
| Incremental cost | ₱130,000 | ₱210,000 | ₱60,000 |
| Internal capacity pressure | Higher | Lower | None additional |
| Customer schedule impact | Partial shortage | Partial shortage | 2,500 units rescheduled |

**Incremental cost comparison**

| Strategy | Incremental cost |
|---|---:|
| Overtime + expedite | ₱130,000 |
| Subcontracting | ₱210,000 |
| Rescheduling | ₱60,000 |

### 9.7 Which strategy should we recommend?

Hindi automatic na Alternative 3 ang best choice dahil pinakamura ito.

Imagine na ang 2,500 deferred units ay committed sa strategic customers na may strict delivery requirements.

Then, delaying those orders could cause substantial commercial damage.

Similarly, Alternative 1 may cost ₱130,000, but it protects more original delivery commitments.

A professional S&OP analyst should evaluate the following:

1. Which customers are affected?
2. Are there contractual delivery penalties?
3. Can suppliers deliver the required materials on time?
4. Can production execute the overtime schedule?
5. Is the additional cost justified by the protected revenue and customer commitments?
6. What will happen to next month's demand and capacity?

**My preliminary recommendation: A hybrid balancing strategy.**

Instead of using only one alternative:

- Use overtime and material expediting to recover feasible internal supply.
- Negotiate rescheduling for the remaining shortage, provided customers approve.
- Protect the most critical customer commitments.
- Monitor next month's capacity to ensure that deferred demand does not create another shortage.

This combines supply-side and demand-side adjustments.

However, the final recommendation depends on customer priorities, actual margin contribution, penalties, and supplier confirmation.

### 9.8 Important lesson from the case study

The cheapest strategy is not always the best.

The strategy with the highest production volume is not always the most profitable.

And the strategy with 100% revised-schedule fulfillment does not necessarily mean 100% on-time delivery against original commitments.

**The best balancing strategy is the one that achieves an acceptable combination of operational feasibility, financial performance, inventory health, and customer service.**

## 10. Supply-Demand Balancing in the S&OP Process

Ngayon, paano ginagamit ng actual S&OP Analyst ang lahat ng pinag-aralan natin?

Hindi naman ikaw ang direktang magde-decide kung mag-overtime, mag-subcontract, o mag-reschedule ng customer orders.

Ang responsibility mo ay mag-provide ng reliable data, evaluate alternatives, quantify business impacts, at tulungan ang cross-functional teams na makagawa ng informed decisions.

### 10.1 Role of the S&OP Analyst

Typical responsibilities:

| Activity | Analyst responsibility |
|---|---|
| Demand analysis | Identify demand requirements and changes. |
| Supply analysis | Review production, inventory, capacity, and procurement availability. |
| Gap analysis | Quantify shortages and excess supply. |
| Scenario planning | Develop alternative balancing strategies. |
| Cost analysis | Calculate incremental costs of each alternative. |
| Customer impact | Identify affected orders and commitments. |
| Recommendation | Present the most feasible and beneficial option. |
| Monitoring | Track whether the approved plan is executed successfully. |

### 10.2 Supply Review

During Supply Review, the supply planning team evaluates whether the existing supply plan can support the demand forecast.

Typical discussion points:

- Available production capacity.
- Production constraints.
- Raw material availability.
- Supplier delivery performance.
- Inventory levels.
- Production schedule.
- Backlog.
- Potential supply gaps.
- Alternative balancing strategies.

Example presentation:

**Supply Review Finding**

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Demand | 13,000 units |
| Available supply | 10,500 units |
| Supply gap | 2,500 units |
| Normal capacity | 10,000 units |
| Planned production | 9,500 units |
| Material constraint | Product B |

**Proposed actions:**

1. Expedite raw materials for Product B.
2. Evaluate additional overtime.
3. Identify subcontracting opportunities.
4. Coordinate with Sales regarding possible delivery adjustments.

The objective is to establish technically feasible supply options.

### 10.3 Pre-S&OP

Sa Pre-S&OP, mas malawak na ang discussion.

Hindi lang supply team ang involved.

Usually, participating functions include:

- Sales
- Demand Planning
- Supply Planning
- Manufacturing
- Procurement
- Finance
- Logistics
- Customer Service

Each function evaluates the proposed balancing alternatives from its own perspective.

| Department | Main concern |
|---|---|
| Sales | Customer commitments and revenue |
| Manufacturing | Production capacity and execution |
| Procurement | Material availability and supplier capability |
| Finance | Additional costs and profitability |
| Logistics | Shipment and delivery feasibility |
| Customer Service | Order fulfillment and customer satisfaction |

Example:

Manufacturing recommends overtime.

Finance questions the additional labor cost.

Sales recommends rescheduling some customer orders.

Customer Service warns that certain customers have strict delivery commitments.

Procurement confirms that expedited materials can arrive before the production schedule.

The purpose of Pre-S&OP is to reconcile these perspectives and develop a cross-functionally aligned recommendation.

### 10.4 Management decision-making

Some decisions require management approval because they involve significant financial or commercial consequences.

Examples:

- Approving additional production expenses.
- Authorizing subcontracting.
- Prioritizing strategic customers.
- Accepting customer delivery delays.
- Approving inventory build-ahead.
- Adjusting revenue or production expectations.

The management team evaluates the alternatives and approves the recommended business plan or requests further analysis.

### 10.5 How to present balancing strategies professionally

As an S&OP Analyst, avoid presenting only this:

"May shortage tayo ng 2,500 units. Kailangan mag-overtime."

Instead, present something like this:

**Sample S&OP balancing recommendation**

**Business situation**

Current demand exceeds available supply by 2,500 units, primarily due to limited raw material availability and production constraints.

**Alternative solutions**

| Option | Cost | Fulfillment |
|---|---:|---:|
| Overtime + expedite | ₱130,000 | 92.31% |
| Subcontracting | ₱210,000 | 92.31% |
| Demand rescheduling | ₱60,000 | 80.77% original-date |

**Recommended approach**

Implement additional internal production where feasible and negotiate delivery rescheduling for the remaining uncovered demand.

**Key considerations**

- Protect critical customer commitments.
- Confirm raw material delivery.
- Validate overtime production capacity.
- Assess next month's impact of deferred orders.
- Monitor incremental costs and actual fulfillment.

**Management decision required**

Approve the proposed overtime and material expediting budget, subject to supplier confirmation and customer delivery alignment.

This is the difference between merely reporting operational data and providing business decision support.

## 11. Business Perspective and Decision-Making

At the professional level, you need to understand that every balancing decision creates trade-offs.

### 11.1 How balancing decisions affect business performance

| Business area | Potential impact |
|---|---|
| Demand | Orders may be fulfilled, rescheduled, or deprioritized. |
| Supply | Production and sourcing requirements may increase. |
| Inventory | Stock may increase or decrease depending on the strategy. |
| Capacity | Overtime, shifts, or alternative production resources may be required. |
| Procurement | Additional purchases and expedited deliveries may occur. |
| Customer service | Delivery performance may improve or deteriorate. |
| Cost | Additional operating and inventory costs may arise. |
| Revenue | Protected sales or delayed shipments may affect financial results. |
| Profitability | Higher fulfillment may come at the expense of additional costs. |

### 11.2 The importance of evaluating multiple planning periods

One common mistake is solving the current month's shortage without considering the following month.

Example:

| Metric | March | April |
|---|---:|---:|
| Original demand | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| Deferred demand from previous month | 0 | 2,000 |
| Total requirement | 10,000 | 12,000 |
| Available supply | 10,000 | 10,500 |
| Supply gap | 0 | -1,500 |

March is balanced.

But April now has a 1,500-unit shortage because of the deferred demand.

This is called a **planning horizon impact**.

A good S&OP analyst evaluates the consequences of decisions across multiple periods rather than optimizing only the current month.

### 11.3 Short-term fix versus structural improvement

Consider this situation:

For three consecutive months, the company has been expediting raw materials to prevent production shortages.

A short-term response is to continue expediting.

But a professional analyst should ask:

- Why is the supplier consistently late?
- Is the procurement lead time accurate?
- Is the safety stock sufficient?
- Is the demand forecast increasing unexpectedly?
- Is there an alternative qualified supplier?
- Is the procurement planning process working correctly?

The long-term objective is to prevent recurring supply gaps rather than continuously paying for emergency solutions.

## 12. Key Takeaways

Ito ang important concepts na dapat mong maalala before proceeding.

| Concept | What you should remember |
|---|---|
| Supply-Demand Balancing | Aligning supply and demand through operational and commercial adjustments. |
| Supply-side strategies | Increase or optimize available supply. |
| Demand-side strategies | Adjust demand timing, quantity, or configuration. |
| Overtime | Increase production through additional working hours. |
| Subcontracting | Use external manufacturing resources. |
| Expediting | Accelerate material deliveries. |
| Inventory reallocation | Move available inventory to where it is needed. |
| Build-ahead | Produce earlier to prepare for future demand. |
| Level production | Maintain relatively stable output. |
| Chase production | Adjust production according to demand. |
| Backlog | Unfulfilled customer orders. |
| Demand fulfillment | Measure how much demand is fulfilled. |
| Trade-off analysis | Evaluate cost, feasibility, inventory, and customer impact. |

**Five important professional lessons:**

1. Identifying a supply gap is different from resolving it.
2. A supply shortage can be addressed through supply-side or demand-side adjustments.
3. Inventory and production decisions must consider future planning periods.
4. The cheapest solution is not automatically the best business solution.
5. S&OP analysts must quantify alternatives and communicate their trade-offs to management.
