# Module 8 – Topic 8.3: Demand Review Meeting

**Main objective:** Matutunan mong mag-prepare, mag-analyze, mag-facilitate, at mag-document ng Demand Review Meeting bilang isang S&OP Analyst.

Sa previous topic, **8.2 – Data Gathering and Preparation**, pinag-aralan natin kung paano kinokolekta, chine-check, at kino-consolidate ang data na ginagamit sa S&OP.

Ngayon, gagamitin natin ang prepared data para sagutin ang isang importanteng business question:

> **"Based on what we know today, how much demand should the business realistically expect in the coming months?"**

Hindi lang ito simpleng meeting kung saan sasabihin ng Sales kung ilan ang posibleng ibenta. Isa itong structured business discussion kung saan kailangang pagsamahin ang historical data, statistical forecasts, customer information, market intelligence, at commercial judgment para makabuo ng isang realistic at agreed demand plan.

# 1. Topic Overview

## 1.1 What is a Demand Review Meeting?

Ang **Demand Review Meeting** ay isang formal meeting within the monthly S&OP cycle kung saan nire-review ng iba't ibang departments ang expected customer demand at pinagkakasunduan kung ano ang magiging demand plan ng company.

Simple example:

May manufacturing company na gumagawa ng tatlong products:

- Product A – Industrial Capacitors
- Product B – Automotive Components
- Product C – Electronic Modules

Ang statistical forecast system ay nagsasabi na:

| Product | Next Month Forecast |
|---|---:|
| Product A | 10,000 units |
| Product B | 8,000 units |
| Product C | 5,000 units |
| **Total** | **23,000 units** |

Pero may bagong information ang Sales:

- Product A: May additional customer order na 2,000 units.
- Product B: May customer na nag-postpone ng order na 1,000 units.
- Product C: May bagong commercial opportunity na 3,000 units, pero hindi pa confirmed.

Question: Dapat bang basta palitan ang forecast?

Hindi agad.

Kailangan munang malaman:

1. Confirmed ba ang customer order?
2. Talaga bang postponed ang order?
3. Gaano kalaki ang probability na makuha ang bagong opportunity?
4. Kailan kailangan ng customer ang products?
5. One-time demand lang ba ito o magiging recurring?
6. May historical evidence ba na sumusuporta sa projected demand?

Ito ang mga klase ng discussion na nangyayari sa Demand Review Meeting.

## 1.2 Why is it important?

Ang demand plan ang magiging starting point ng maraming operational decisions.

| Department | Bakit kailangan nila ang demand plan? |
|---|---|
| Production | Para malaman kung gaano karaming products ang kailangang gawin. |
| Procurement | Para malaman kung gaano karaming raw materials ang kailangang bilhin. |
| Inventory | Para maiwasan ang shortage at excess inventory. |
| Finance | Para makagawa ng revenue at financial projections. |
| Sales | Para ma-align ang commercial commitments sa actual business expectations. |
| Management | Para malaman kung achievable ang business targets. |

**Important principle:**

An inaccurate demand plan can create problems throughout the supply chain.

Kapag overestimated ang demand, possible na magkaroon ng excess inventory, unnecessary production, at additional inventory holding costs.

Kapag underestimated naman, possible na magkaroon ng stockouts, missed sales, delayed deliveries, at dissatisfied customers.

## 1.3 Position within the monthly S&OP cycle

Balikan natin ang overall S&OP process from Topic 8.1.

### Monthly S&OP Cycle

1. **Data Gathering and Preparation**  
   Collect, validate, and consolidate historical and current business data.
2. **Demand Review Meeting (Current Topic 8.3)**  
   Review demand, challenge assumptions, and establish the consensus demand plan.
3. **Supply Review Meeting (Next Topic 8.4)**  
   Evaluate whether available supply and capacity can support the demand plan.
4. **Pre-S&OP Meeting**  
   Resolve cross-functional gaps and prepare recommendations for management.
5. **Executive S&OP Meeting**  
   Make strategic decisions and approve the integrated business plan.

Notice the relationship:

**Demand Review determines what the business expects to sell. Supply Review determines how the business can fulfill that demand.**

Hindi pa natin kailangang malaman kung kaya ba ng production ang demand during the Demand Review. Ang priority muna ay magkaroon ng credible demand plan. Pero dapat documented ang risks na maaaring makaapekto sa supply.

# 2. Layman's Explanation

## 2.1 Imagine that you own a bakery

Imagine na may bakery ka na gumagawa ng 1,000 loaves of bread every day.

Last month, ang actual sales mo ay:

| Week | Actual Sales |
|---|---:|
| Week 1 | 6,000 |
| Week 2 | 6,500 |
| Week 3 | 7,000 |
| Week 4 | 6,500 |
| **Total** | **26,000** |

Based on historical sales, your forecasting system estimates that next month you will sell 28,000 loaves.

Pero may bagong information:

- Marketing will launch a promotional campaign.
- Sales expects a new supermarket customer.
- One existing customer will temporarily reduce its orders.
- There is an upcoming holiday.

Now, magkakaroon ng meeting.

Sales will explain what customers are planning to purchase.

Marketing will explain the expected effect of the promotion.

Demand Planning will explain what the historical data and forecasting model indicate.

Finance will evaluate the financial implications.

Together, they will decide the most reasonable demand expectation.

That is essentially a Demand Review Meeting.

## 2.2 Three different forecasts you need to understand

May tatlong importanteng concepts na hindi dapat pinagpapalit.

### 1. Statistical Forecast

**Data-driven baseline**

Ito ang forecast generated using historical demand patterns and statistical forecasting methods.

Example: Based on historical data, the system predicts 10,000 units.

### 2. Sales Forecast

**Commercial judgment**

Ito ang projection ng Sales based on customer conversations, pipeline opportunities, market intelligence, and expected orders.

Example: Sales expects 13,000 units because of a potential new customer contract.

### 3. Consensus Demand Plan

**Cross-functional agreement**

Ito ang agreed demand projection after evaluating the statistical forecast and valid commercial information.

Example: The company agrees to plan for 11,500 units, with another 1,500 units recorded as an upside opportunity.

**Important distinction:** Hindi automatic na ang Sales Forecast ang magiging final demand plan. Hindi rin automatic na statistical forecast ang tama.

Ang goal ay magkaroon ng demand plan na supported by evidence and business assumptions.

# 3. Technical Explanation

## 3.1 Formal definition

A Demand Review Meeting is a cross-functional planning forum within the S&OP process where stakeholders evaluate historical demand, review the current forecast, incorporate validated commercial intelligence, assess demand risks and opportunities, and establish a consensus demand plan.

May ilang importanteng terms dito.

| Technical term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Baseline Forecast | Forecast generated from historical demand patterns before additional business adjustments. |
| Statistical Forecast | Forecast generated using quantitative forecasting models. |
| Sales Forecast | Expected demand based on commercial expectations and customer intelligence. |
| Demand Adjustment | A change made to the baseline forecast based on valid additional information. |
| Consensus Demand Plan | Agreed demand projection across relevant functions. |
| Forecast Override | Manual adjustment to a system-generated forecast. |
| Demand Assumption | An expectation or condition used to justify a forecast. |
| Demand Risk | An event that could reduce expected demand. |
| Demand Opportunity | An event that could increase expected demand. |
| Forecast Horizon | The future period covered by the forecast. |
| Demand Reconciliation | The process of resolving differences between demand projections. |

## 3.2 Statistical forecast versus sales forecast versus consensus demand plan

Let's use one manufacturing example.

The company manufactures industrial components.

| Source | Forecast |
|---|---:|
| Statistical forecast | 10,000 units |
| Sales forecast | 13,000 units |
| Marketing forecast adjustment | +1,000 units |
| Confirmed customer cancellation | -500 units |

A simple reconciliation would be:

\[
10,000+1,000-500=10,500
\]

However, we cannot automatically add the entire Sales forecast of 13,000 and the Marketing adjustment of 1,000.

Why?

Because the Sales forecast might already include the marketing promotion.

This is called **double counting**.

The team must identify which demand events are already reflected in the baseline forecast and which ones are genuinely incremental.

The agreed demand plan might therefore be 10,500 units, subject to the validity of the assumptions.

## 3.3 Why demand assumptions must be challenged

Imagine Sales says:

"We expect demand to increase by 40% next month."

As an S&OP Analyst, hindi sapat na tanggapin mo lang ang statement.

You need to ask:

- What is driving the 40% increase?
- Which customers will generate the additional demand?
- Are there confirmed purchase orders?
- Is there a signed contract?
- Is this based on a historical trend?
- Is the increase temporary or permanent?
- Is the expected demand already included in the baseline forecast?
- What happens if the expected demand does not materialize?

The objective is not to question someone's competence.

The objective is to determine whether the forecast has sufficient evidence to support it.

**Professional principle: Challenge the assumption, not the person.**

# 4. Detailed Discussion

## 4.1 Meeting Participants and Responsibilities

Hindi lahat ng departments ay may parehong responsibility.

Each department contributes a different perspective.

| Department / Role | Main responsibility |
|---|---|
| Sales | Provides customer intelligence, expected orders, opportunities, and changes in customer demand. |
| Marketing | Provides promotions, campaigns, market trends, and product positioning information. |
| Demand Planning | Develops and maintains the demand forecast and evaluates forecast changes. |
| Product Management | Provides product lifecycle, launches, discontinuations, and product strategy information. |
| Finance | Evaluates revenue implications, financial assumptions, and alignment with business targets. |
| S&OP Analyst | Consolidates analysis, identifies gaps, prepares reports, tracks decisions, and supports alignment. |
| Meeting Facilitator | Manages the agenda, discussion, time, and decision process. |
| Decision-maker | Resolves disagreements and approves decisions within their authority. |

### Demand Planner versus S&OP Analyst

Important ito dahil magkaiba ang focus nila, although in some companies, overlapping ang responsibilities.

**Demand Planner**

Primarily responsible for the demand forecast.

Typical activities:
- Generate statistical forecasts.
- Analyze historical demand.
- Evaluate forecast accuracy and bias.
- Investigate forecast exceptions.
- Apply justified forecast adjustments.
- Maintain the demand planning system.

**S&OP Analyst**

Primarily responsible for supporting the integrated planning process.

Typical activities:
- Consolidate information from different departments.
- Prepare demand and supply analysis.
- Identify significant changes and business gaps.
- Prepare meeting presentations.
- Document assumptions, risks, and decisions.
- Track action items and unresolved issues.
- Support communication between departments.

Think of it this way:

The Demand Planner focuses on developing the demand forecast.

The S&OP Analyst helps ensure that the forecast becomes part of a coordinated business planning process.

## 4.2 Responsibilities Before, During, and After the Meeting

| Department | Before | During | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | Update customer expectations | Explain demand changes | Confirm commercial assumptions |
| Marketing | Submit campaign details | Explain promotional impact | Track campaign results |
| Demand Planning | Generate forecasts | Explain statistical results | Update agreed forecast |
| Product Management | Submit lifecycle updates | Discuss new/discontinued products | Update product assumptions |
| Finance | Prepare revenue analysis | Review financial impact | Update financial projections |
| S&OP Analyst | Prepare reports and agenda | Present analysis and record decisions | Distribute minutes and track actions |

### The meeting facilitator

The facilitator ensures that the meeting remains focused.

For example:

Sales: "We expect Product A to increase by 30%."

Demand Planning: "Our historical trend does not support that increase."

The facilitator should guide the discussion toward evidence:

"What specific customer or market information supports the additional 30%?"

If there is no agreement, the issue should be documented and escalated according to the company's governance process.

## 4.3 Demand Review Inputs

Bago magsimula ang meeting, kailangan prepared na ang relevant data.

Ito ang major inputs.

| Input | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Historical demand | Understand previous demand behavior. |
| Statistical forecast | Establish the baseline projection. |
| Forecast accuracy | Evaluate forecast reliability. |
| Forecast bias | Identify consistent overforecasting or underforecasting. |
| Customer orders | Identify actual customer commitments. |
| Backlog | Review outstanding customer orders. |
| Sales pipeline | Identify potential future commercial demand. |
| Promotions | Estimate demand impact from marketing activities. |
| Seasonality | Understand recurring demand patterns. |
| Market trends | Identify changes in the external business environment. |
| New product introductions | Include expected demand from newly launched products. |
| Product discontinuations | Remove or transition demand from discontinued products. |
| Previous assumptions | Compare previous expectations against actual results. |
| Actual performance | Determine whether previous plans materialized. |

### Important distinction: Orders, backlog, and forecast

These three concepts are related but different.

**Customer Orders**

Actual orders submitted by customers.

**Backlog**

Orders that have been received but have not yet been fulfilled.

**Forecast**

Expected future demand that may not yet have become an actual customer order.

Example:

- Customer orders: 5,000 units
- Backlog: 2,000 units
- Additional expected future demand: 8,000 units

Hindi ibig sabihin na 15,000 units agad ang demand.

Kailangan mong malaman kung kasama na ba ang backlog sa customer orders, at kung paano kino-compute ng company ang forecast demand.

Otherwise, you might double count the demand.

### Gross demand versus net demand

Another important concept:

- **Gross Demand:** Total expected customer demand before considering supply constraints.
- **Net Demand:** Demand remaining after applying relevant deductions or adjustments, depending on the company's planning methodology.

In S&OP, the demand review generally establishes the unconstrained demand plan first.

Supply constraints are evaluated during Supply Review.

## 4.4 Demand Review Activities

Ito ang actual workflow ng meeting.

### Demand Review Meeting Workflow

1. **Review historical demand**  
   Check actual sales, demand patterns, and unusual changes.
2. **Evaluate forecast performance**  
   Compare previous forecasts against actual demand.
3. **Review the statistical forecast**  
   Examine the baseline projection and identify exceptions.
4. **Incorporate commercial intelligence**  
   Evaluate orders, customer updates, promotions, and pipeline opportunities.
5. **Challenge assumptions**  
   Validate the reasons behind forecast increases and decreases.
6. **Evaluate risks and opportunities**  
   Identify possible upside and downside demand scenarios.
7. **Reconcile differences**  
   Resolve disagreements between Sales, Demand Planning, and other stakeholders.
8. **Establish consensus demand**  
   Agree on the demand plan and document remaining uncertainties.

### Upside and downside scenarios

Hindi lahat ng uncertainty ay kailangang piliting gawing one-number forecast.

For example:

| Scenario | Product A Demand |
|---|---:|
| Downside | 9,000 |
| Consensus | 10,000 |
| Upside | 12,000 |

Interpretation:

- Downside: Possible reduction in customer demand.
- Consensus: Most reasonable current expectation.
- Upside: Additional demand if a commercial opportunity materializes.

This allows the business to understand the possible range of future demand without treating uncertain opportunities as guaranteed sales.

# 5. Forecast Analysis and Calculations

Ito ang isa sa pinakamahalagang technical sections ng Demand Review Meeting.

Hindi sapat na sabihin na "tumaas ang demand" o "mababa ang forecast accuracy."

As an S&OP Analyst, kailangan mong maipakita kung gaano kalaki ang pagbabago, saan nanggaling, at ano ang business implication nito.

Let's study six important calculations.

## 5.1 Forecast Variance

**Purpose:** Sukatin ang difference between forecast demand and actual demand.

Formula:

\[
\text{Forecast Variance} = F-A
\]

Where:

- \(F\) = Forecast demand (units)
- \(A\) = Actual demand (units)

Example:

- Forecast = 10,000 units
- Actual = 9,000 units

\[
10,000-9,000=1,000
\]

**+1,000 units: Overforecast**

Ibig sabihin, nag-forecast tayo ng 1,000 units more than actual demand.

Percentage variance:

\[
\text{Variance \%}=\frac{F-A}{A}\times100
\]

\[
=\frac{10,000-9,000}{9,000}\times100
\]

\[
=11.11\%
\]

Interpretation: The forecast was 11.11% higher than actual demand.

**Important:** Positive variance means overforecasting under this formula. Negative variance means underforecasting.

Note that some companies use the opposite sign convention, so always verify the reporting definition.

## 5.2 Forecast Accuracy

**Purpose:** Determine how close the forecast is to actual demand.

One commonly used approach is absolute percentage error:

\[
APE=\frac{|F-A|}{A}\times100
\]

Then:

\[
\text{Forecast Accuracy}=100\%-APE
\]

Using our previous example:

\[
APE=\frac{|10,000-9,000|}{9,000}\times100
\]

\[
APE=11.11\%
\]

Therefore:

\[
Accuracy=100\%-11.11\%
\]

**88.89% Accuracy**

### Important limitation

The formula above is appropriate for an individual period with nonzero actual demand, but it can produce negative accuracy when forecast error exceeds actual demand.

For multiple products or periods, companies commonly use WAPE.

**Weighted Absolute Percentage Error (WAPE):**

\[
WAPE=\frac{\sum|F-A|}{\sum A}\times100
\]

\[
\text{WAPE-based Accuracy}=100\%-WAPE
\]

Example:

| Product | Forecast | Actual | Absolute Error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 10,000 | 9,000 | 1,000 |
| B | 8,000 | 8,500 | 500 |
| C | 5,000 | 4,500 | 500 |
| **Total** | **23,000** | **22,000** | **2,000** |

\[
WAPE=\frac{2,000}{22,000}\times100
\]

\[
WAPE=9.09\%
\]

\[
Accuracy=100\%-9.09\%
\]

**90.91% WAPE-based Accuracy**

Why use WAPE?

Because it evaluates the combined forecasting error relative to total actual demand. Larger-volume products have greater influence on the result.

**Limitation:** WAPE can hide poor performance on low-volume products. If total actual demand is zero, WAPE is undefined.

Also, WAPE-based accuracy is a reporting convention, not the only accepted definition of forecast accuracy.

## 5.3 Forecast Bias

**Purpose:** Identify whether the company consistently overforecasts or underforecasts.

Formula:

\[
\text{Forecast Bias}=
\frac{\sum(F-A)}{\sum A}\times100
\]

Using the previous example:

| Product | Forecast | Actual | Forecast - Actual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 10,000 | 9,000 | +1,000 |
| B | 8,000 | 8,500 | -500 |
| C | 5,000 | 4,500 | +500 |
| **Total** | **23,000** | **22,000** | **+1,000** |

\[
Bias=\frac{1,000}{22,000}\times100
\]

\[
Bias=+4.55\%
\]

**Positive Bias: Overforecasting**

Interpretation:

The company overestimated demand by 4.55% relative to actual demand.

If the result were negative, it would indicate underforecasting.

### Forecast Accuracy versus Forecast Bias

Important na maintindihan ang difference.

| Metric | What does it tell us? |
|---|---|
| Forecast Accuracy | How large is the forecasting error? |
| Forecast Bias | Is the forecast consistently higher or lower than actual demand? |

Example:

Imagine two products:

- Product A: Forecast is sometimes too high and sometimes too low.
- Product B: Forecast is consistently higher than actual demand.

They could have similar overall error magnitudes, but Product B has a systematic overforecasting problem.

That is why both metrics are needed.

**Professional insight:** A company can have acceptable forecast accuracy but still have a persistent bias in certain products, customers, or periods.

## 5.4 Percentage Change in Demand

**Purpose:** Measure how much demand increased or decreased compared with a previous period.

Formula:

\[
\% Change=
\frac{D_{new}-D_{old}}{D_{old}}\times100
\]

Example:

Previous month demand = 10,000 units

Current month forecast = 12,000 units

\[
\frac{12,000-10,000}{10,000}\times100
\]

\[
=20\%
\]

**20% Demand Increase**

Interpretation:

Expected demand increased by 20% compared with the previous month.

**Important:** A 20% increase does not automatically mean that the forecast is correct.

You still need to understand the reason behind the increase.

## 5.5 Demand Growth Rate

Percentage change and demand growth rate can use the same basic formula, but they answer slightly different questions depending on the time horizon.

For a year-over-year demand growth rate:

\[
Growth\ Rate=
\frac{D_{current}-D_{previous}}{D_{previous}}\times100
\]

Example:

| Year | Demand |
|---|---:|
| 2025 | 100,000 units |
| 2026 | 115,000 units |

\[
Growth=\frac{115,000-100,000}{100,000}\times100
\]

\[
=15\%
\]

The business experienced 15% year-over-year demand growth.

For a multi-year period, we can also calculate Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR):

\[
CAGR=\left(\frac{Ending}{Beginning}\right)^{1/n}-1
\]

Where \(n\) is the number of years.

For example, demand increased from 100,000 to 133,100 units over three years.

\[
CAGR=(133,100/100,000)^{1/3}-1
\]

\[
=10\%
\]

This means demand grew at an equivalent compounded annual rate of 10%.

**Limitation:** Historical growth does not guarantee future growth. A temporary demand spike should not automatically be treated as a long-term trend.

## 5.6 Impact of Promotions and Special Demand Events

**Purpose:** Estimate the additional demand generated by a promotion or unusual commercial event.

Basic formula:

\[
Incremental\ Demand=
Promotion\ Demand-Baseline\ Demand
\]

Example:

A manufacturing company supplies electronic components to an appliance manufacturer.

Expected baseline demand without promotion = 10,000 units.

Expected demand during the promotion = 13,000 units.

\[
Incremental=13,000-10,000
\]

\[
=3,000\ units
\]

The promotion is expected to generate an additional 3,000 units.

Percentage uplift:

\[
Promotion\ Uplift=
\frac{Incremental\ Demand}{Baseline\ Demand}\times100
\]

\[
=\frac{3,000}{10,000}\times100
\]

\[
=30\%
\]

**30% Promotion Uplift**

### A more realistic promotion analysis

Hindi lahat ng increase during a promotion ay truly incremental.

Consider these factors:

1. **Demand pull-forward:** Customers may purchase earlier than usual, reducing demand in the following month.
2. **Cannibalization:** A promotion for one product may reduce sales of another product.
3. **Seasonality:** Demand may already be increasing because of seasonal factors.
4. **Double counting:** The baseline forecast might already include the promotion.
5. **Post-promotion decline:** Demand may return to normal or fall below normal after the event.

For example:

| Period | Baseline | Promotional Forecast |
|---|---:|---:|
| Month 1 | 10,000 | 13,000 |
| Month 2 | 10,000 | 8,000 |
| **Total** | **20,000** | **21,000** |

Although Month 1 increased by 3,000 units, Month 2 decreased by 2,000 units.

Net incremental demand:

\[
21,000-20,000=1,000
\]

The promotion generated only 1,000 additional units across the two months.

This is a more meaningful business evaluation than simply reporting a 30% promotional uplift.

## 5.7 Summary of Important Formulas

| Metric | Formula | Main purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Forecast Variance | \(F-A\) | Measure forecast difference |
| Forecast Variance % | \((F-A)/A \times 100\) | Measure relative over/underforecast |
| Absolute Percentage Error | \(|F-A|/A \times 100\) | Measure individual period error |
| WAPE | \(\sum|F-A|/\sum A \times 100\) | Measure aggregate forecast error |
| Bias | \(\sum(F-A)/\sum A \times 100\) | Identify systematic forecast direction |
| Percentage Change | \((New-Old)/Old \times 100\) | Measure demand movement |
| CAGR | \((Ending/Beginning)^{1/n}-1\) | Measure compounded annual growth |
| Promotion Uplift | \((Promotion-Baseline)/Baseline \times 100\) | Measure promotional increase |

### How to use these in the Demand Review

Hindi kailangang ipakita lahat ng metrics sa bawat meeting.

Choose metrics based on the business question:

- Is the forecast reliable? Use accuracy or WAPE.
- Are we consistently overforecasting? Use bias.
- Why did demand suddenly increase? Use percentage change.
- Is the business growing? Use demand growth rate.
- Did the promotion create additional demand? Use incremental demand and promotion uplift.

Ang goal ay gamitin ang calculation para suportahan ang business decision, hindi para punuin ang presentation ng numbers.

# 6. Practical Manufacturing Application

Now let's simulate an actual Demand Review Meeting.

Imagine that you are an S&OP Analyst working for an electronics manufacturing company.

The company manufactures three products used by industrial and automotive customers.

## 6.1 Business Scenario

*Illustrative business data*

The company is preparing its November demand plan for the upcoming December planning period.

Three products are included:

- Product A: Industrial Capacitor
- Product B: Automotive Component
- Product C: Electronic Module

### Step 1: Review Historical Demand

| Product | September Actual | October Actual | November Actual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 9,000 | 9,500 | 10,000 |
| B | 7,000 | 7,500 | 8,000 |
| C | 4,500 | 5,000 | 5,500 |
| **Total** | **20,500** | **22,000** | **23,500** |

Observation:

All three products show increasing demand.

As an analyst, you should investigate whether the increase is caused by:

- Genuine customer growth
- Seasonal demand
- New customer orders
- Promotions
- Temporary demand fluctuations

Do not automatically assume that the same growth rate will continue.

### Step 2: Review the Statistical Forecast

The system generates the following December baseline forecast:

| Product | November Actual | December Statistical Forecast |
|---|---:|---:|
| A | 10,000 | 10,500 |
| B | 8,000 | 8,400 |
| C | 5,500 | 5,800 |
| **Total** | **23,500** | **24,700** |

The initial statistical forecast is 24,700 units.

### Step 3: Review Sales Projections

During the pre-meeting data submission, Sales provides updated projections.

| Product | Statistical Forecast | Sales Forecast | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 10,500 | 12,500 | +2,000 |
| B | 8,400 | 7,400 | -1,000 |
| C | 5,800 | 7,800 | +2,000 |
| **Total** | **24,700** | **27,700** | **+3,000** |

Now there are significant differences.

Product A increased by 2,000 units.

Product B decreased by 1,000 units.

Product C increased by 2,000 units.

This is where the Demand Review Meeting becomes important.

### Step 4: Investigate Significant Changes

Let's examine each product.

**Product A: Industrial Capacitor**

*Commercial increase supported*

Sales explains:

- Existing customer confirmed an additional order of 1,500 units.
- Another 500 units are expected from a new customer opportunity.

Analyst assessment:

- 1,500 units have confirmed customer demand.
- 500 units remain uncertain because the opportunity is not yet confirmed.

**Recommended treatment:** Incorporate the validated 1,500 units into the consensus forecast. Record the remaining 500 units as an upside opportunity, subject to the company's demand planning policy.

**Product B: Automotive Component**

*Demand reduction supported*

Sales explains:

- A customer postponed an order of 1,000 units to January.
- The order is not cancelled.

Analyst assessment:

- The December demand must be reduced by 1,000 units.
- The demand should be reviewed in January.
- It must not be permanently removed from the forecast.

**Recommended treatment:** Reduce December demand by 1,000 units and record the timing shift.

**Product C: Electronic Module**

*Assumption requires validation*

Sales explains:

- A new customer may purchase an additional 2,000 units.
- No confirmed purchase order exists.
- The customer is still evaluating the product.

Analyst assessment:

- There is a potential commercial opportunity.
- However, there is insufficient evidence to treat all 2,000 units as committed demand.

**Recommended treatment:** Retain the statistical forecast as the base consensus demand and record the 2,000 units as upside potential until stronger evidence becomes available.

### Step 5: Develop the Consensus Demand Plan

Let's consolidate the validated adjustments.

| Product | Statistical Forecast | Validated Adjustment | Consensus Demand |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 10,500 | +1,500 | 12,000 |
| B | 8,400 | -1,000 | 7,400 |
| C | 5,800 | 0 | 5,800 |
| **Total** | **24,700** | **+500** | **25,200** |

The agreed December demand plan is:

### December Consensus Demand: 25,200 units

- Initial statistical forecast: 24,700 units
- Validated net adjustment: +500 units
- Additional upside opportunity: 2,500 units

The upside comprises 500 units for Product A and 2,000 units for Product C. These are not included in the consensus baseline.

Notice that the Sales forecast was 27,700 units, but the consensus demand plan became 25,200 units.

Why?

Because the company accepted validated information rather than automatically adopting every commercial projection.

### Step 6: Document the Risks and Opportunities

The Demand Review should not end with just one agreed number.

It should also record what could change that number.

| Product | Type | Quantity | Reason | Required Action |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| A | Upside | +500 | New customer opportunity | Sales to confirm order |
| B | Timing risk | 1,000 | Order moved to January | Confirm revised delivery date |
| C | Upside | +2,000 | New customer evaluation | Sales to validate opportunity |

### Step 7: Interpret the Results

As the S&OP Analyst, you should communicate the following:

1. December demand is expected to reach 25,200 units.
2. The consensus plan is 500 units higher than the statistical baseline.
3. Product A has a validated customer-driven increase.
4. Product B has a timing shift rather than permanent demand loss.
5. Product C has an additional 2,000-unit commercial opportunity that remains unconfirmed.
6. Total upside potential is 2,500 units.

**Professional insight:** A good demand plan separates what the business reasonably expects from what the business hopes to achieve.

This distinction prevents uncertain opportunities from being treated as guaranteed demand.

## 6.2 Visualizing the Demand Changes

The following comparison illustrates how the three forecasts differ.

| Product | Statistical Forecast | Sales Forecast | Consensus Demand |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Product A | 10,500 | 12,500 | 12,000 |
| Product B | 8,400 | 7,400 | 7,400 |
| Product C | 5,800 | 7,800 | 5,800 |

Observe:

- Sales forecast is higher for Product A and Product C.
- The consensus plan accepts Product A's validated adjustment.
- Product C's unconfirmed opportunity remains outside the base demand plan.
- Product B's reduction is reflected in the consensus plan.

This is the type of analysis that makes your presentation useful for decision-makers.

## 7.3 Handoff to Supply Review

Ito ang pinaka-importanteng connection to Topic 8.4.

The Supply Review team needs the agreed demand plan to evaluate:

- Production capacity
- Available inventory
- Raw material availability
- Supplier capacity and lead times
- Labor requirements
- Manufacturing constraints
- Potential supply shortages

For our example:

### Demand Review Output

**25,200 units**

Agreed December demand.

### Supply Review Question

Can the company manufacture and deliver 25,200 units within December using available inventory, materials, production capacity, and supplier commitments?

For example, if the available production capacity is only 23,000 units, the company has a potential 2,200-unit supply gap.

But that is a Supply Review problem, not something that should be hidden by reducing the demand forecast.

**Remember: Demand Review establishes expected demand. Supply Review evaluates how to fulfill it.**

# 8. Demand Review in Professional S&OP Practice

Now let's move from understanding the process to performing the actual job.

Imagine ikaw ang S&OP Analyst na responsible for preparing the monthly Demand Review presentation.

What exactly should you do?

## 8.1 Before the Meeting: Preparation

Your job starts before the meeting itself.

### A. Collect the necessary information

Request the latest information from:

- Demand Planning
- Sales
- Marketing
- Product Management
- Finance

Make sure that everyone is using the same planning horizon, product hierarchy, units of measurement, and data cutoff.

### B. Prepare the analysis

Your presentation should answer these questions:

| Analysis | Business Question |
|---|---|
| Historical demand trend | What happened to demand? |
| Forecast accuracy | How reliable was our previous forecast? |
| Forecast bias | Are we consistently overestimating or underestimating? |
| Forecast versus actual | Where are the biggest gaps? |
| Forecast versus previous cycle | What changed since last month? |
| Sales versus statistical forecast | Where do commercial expectations differ? |
| Upside/downside analysis | What could change the expected demand? |
| Demand by product/customer | Where are the important changes occurring? |

### C. Identify exceptions

You do not need to discuss every product in equal detail.

Focus on significant changes.

For example:

- Product demand increased by 25%.
- Forecast accuracy declined significantly.
- A major customer postponed an order.
- A new product has no historical demand.
- A large sales opportunity remains unconfirmed.

This is called **exception-based management**.

Instead of spending the meeting reviewing hundreds of unchanged product lines, focus on items that require explanation or decisions.

## 8.2 During the Meeting: Presenting Demand Analysis

Your presentation should be structured around business questions.

Example:

**Slide 1: Executive Demand Summary**

- Total demand forecast
- Change from previous month
- Major increases and decreases
- Key demand risks

**Slide 2: Historical Demand Trend**

- Actual demand by month
- Demand growth or decline
- Significant unusual patterns

**Slide 3: Forecast Performance**

- Forecast accuracy
- Forecast bias
- Major forecast errors
- Products requiring corrective action

**Slide 4: Demand Changes**

- Statistical forecast versus Sales forecast
- Previous forecast versus current forecast
- Major commercial adjustments

**Slide 5: Risks and Opportunities**

- Customer order changes
- Promotions
- New business opportunities
- Product launches and discontinuations

**Slide 6: Consensus Demand Plan**

- Agreed demand by product and month
- Adjustments and supporting assumptions
- Remaining uncertainties

**Slide 7: Decisions and Action Items**

- Agreed decisions
- Open issues
- Responsible persons
- Target completion dates

This is a practical presentation structure. Actual company templates may have different formats.

## 8.3 How to Challenge Assumptions Professionally

This is an important skill for an S&OP Analyst.

You will encounter situations where Sales, Marketing, and Demand Planning have different expectations.

Your role is to facilitate an evidence-based discussion.

Consider these examples:

| Situation | Professional Question |
|---|---|
| Sales expects a 30% increase | "What customer or market information supports the projected increase?" |
| Marketing expects promotional growth | "Do we have historical promotional performance to estimate the incremental demand?" |
| Demand Planning rejects a sales adjustment | "Can we identify which assumptions are causing the difference?" |
| Customer delays an order | "Is the order cancelled or simply moved to another planning period?" |
| New product has no history | "What comparable products or customer commitments can support the initial forecast?" |

Avoid statements such as:

- "Your forecast is wrong."
- "The system is always correct."
- "Sales doesn't understand the data."

Instead, use:

- "Can we validate the assumptions behind this adjustment?"
- "What evidence supports the proposed demand increase?"
- "Can we separate confirmed demand from potential opportunities?"
- "What would be the impact if this opportunity does not materialize?"

## 8.4 Managing Department Disagreements

Not every discussion will result in immediate agreement.

For example:

- Sales believes demand will increase by 20%.
- Demand Planning estimates only 5%.
- Finance expects growth of 10%.

What should you do?

Do not simply average the three numbers.

First, understand the assumptions behind each estimate.

| Department | Projection | Supporting Information |
|---|---:|---|
| Demand Planning | 10,500 | Historical trend |
| Sales | 12,000 | Customer pipeline |
| Finance | 11,000 | Revenue target |

The team must investigate:

1. Is the sales pipeline sufficiently qualified?
2. Is the historical trend still representative?
3. Is the financial target a business objective or a realistic demand expectation?

If disagreement remains, document the competing assumptions and escalate the decision to the appropriate authority.

**Important:** A financial target is not automatically a demand forecast. The business should not manipulate expected demand simply to match a revenue target.

## 8.5 After the Meeting: Documentation and Follow-up

Your responsibility does not end when the meeting finishes.

You must ensure that:

- The agreed demand plan is recorded.
- Forecast adjustments are documented.
- Assumptions are traceable.
- Risks and opportunities have owners.
- Open issues have deadlines.
- The latest agreed plan is provided to Supply Review.
- Any required forecast updates are completed in the planning system.

A useful principle:

**If a decision is not documented, it becomes difficult to track, validate, and defend during the next S&OP cycle.**

# 9. Key Takeaways

Let's consolidate what you should remember from this topic.

## 9.1 Core Concepts

1. Demand Review is a cross-functional meeting that establishes the company's consensus demand plan.
2. Statistical forecast is data-driven, Sales forecast is commercially informed, and consensus demand is the result of evaluating and reconciling available information.
3. Forecast assumptions must be challenged using evidence.
4. Customer orders, backlog, and forecasts must be properly distinguished to prevent double counting.
5. Forecast accuracy measures error magnitude, while forecast bias identifies systematic overforecasting or underforecasting.
6. Commercial opportunities should not automatically be treated as confirmed demand.
7. The agreed demand plan becomes an important input for Supply Review.
8. Unresolved risks and opportunities must be documented, assigned, and monitored.

## 9.2 The Most Important Mental Model

### The Demand Review Logic

**Historical Demand + Statistical Forecast**

+

**Customer Intelligence + Commercial Information**

+

**Business Assumptions + Risk Assessment**

↓

**Consensus Demand Plan**

*Agreed, evidence-based expected demand*

↓

**Input to Supply Review**

The central idea:

**A good demand plan is not necessarily the highest forecast, the lowest forecast, or the statistical forecast. It is the most reasonable demand expectation supported by available evidence and agreed business assumptions.**
