# Module 8 – S&OP Meetings and Governance
## Topic 8.6: Executive S&OP Meeting

**Main focus: Executive Governance, Strategic Decision-Making, and Business Plan Approval**

Sa topic na ito, aakyat tayo mula sa operational discussions papunta sa management-level decision-making.

Sa previous topic, **Pre-S&OP Meeting (Topic 8.5)**, pinag-usapan natin kung paano nirereconcile ng different departments ang demand, supply, at financial plans, at kung paano naghahanda ng recommendations para sa senior management.

Ngayon, ang focus naman natin ay:

**Kapag hindi kayang resolbahin ng operational teams ang isang business problem, paano magdedesisyon ang senior management?**

Hindi na lang ito tungkol sa kung ilang units ang kayang gawin ng production. Ang tanong na ngayon ay kung anong business decision ang pinakamakabubuti sa buong organization.

---

# 1. Topic Overview

## 1.1 What is an Executive S&OP Meeting?

Ang **Executive S&OP Meeting** ay isang formal monthly management meeting kung saan sinusuri at pinagdedesisyunan ng senior leadership team ang overall business plan ng organization.

Dito tinitingnan kung aligned ang:

- Customer demand
- Production and supply capabilities
- Inventory requirements
- Financial targets
- Business strategies
- Organizational resources

Ang pinakaimportanteng function nito ay hindi simpleng pag-review ng reports.

Ito ang venue kung saan ang senior management ay:

1. Evaluates overall business performance.
2. Reviews major business gaps and risks.
3. Makes strategic decisions.
4. Resolves issues beyond operational authority.
5. Allocates resources.
6. Approves the integrated S&OP plan.

**Simple definition:**

Executive S&OP is where senior management makes decisions to ensure that the company's demand, supply, and financial plans support its overall business objectives.

## 1.2 Why is it important?

Imagine na ang Sales Department ay nangangakong tataas ang customer deliveries by 20%.

Pero:

- Production capacity is already at 95%.
- One critical supplier has a delivery delay.
- Inventory is insufficient.
- Overtime costs are increasing.
- Finance is concerned about declining profit margins.

Puwedeng mag-agree ang Sales na kailangan ng additional supply. Puwede ring mag-agree ang Operations na kailangan ng additional capacity.

Pero sino ang mag-aapprove kung kailangan ng:

- Additional production equipment?
- New supplier?
- Higher inventory investment?
- Additional workforce?
- Capital expenditure?

These decisions may require authority beyond the functional departments.

Dito pumapasok ang Executive S&OP.

**The purpose is to make the best decision for the entire business, not just for one department.**

## 1.3 Position in the Monthly S&OP Cycle

Balikan natin ang overall S&OP cycle from Topic 8.1.

### Monthly S&OP Cycle

1. **Data Gathering and Preparation**

   Collect actual sales, inventory, production, and financial data.

2. **Demand Review**

   Evaluate historical demand, forecasts, customer requirements, and opportunities.

3. **Supply Review**

   Evaluate production capacity, materials, suppliers, inventory, and constraints.

4. **Pre-S&OP Meeting**

   Reconcile demand, supply, and financial plans. Develop scenarios and recommendations.

5. **Executive S&OP Meeting**

   Senior management evaluates escalated issues, makes strategic decisions, and approves the integrated plan.

After approval: communicate decisions, execute the plan, and monitor performance throughout the next planning cycle.

The important distinction:

- Earlier stages prepare and reconcile the plans.
- Pre-S&OP prepares recommendations and escalates unresolved issues.
- Executive S&OP provides management decisions, direction, and approval.

Hindi ibig sabihin na sa Executive S&OP pa lang pinag-uusapan ang buong demand at supply plan. Dapat prepared na ang analysis bago pumasok sa executive meeting.

## 1.4 Difference Between Pre-S&OP and Executive S&OP

Ito ang isa sa pinakamahalagang distinctions sa Module 8.

| Area | Pre-S&OP | Executive S&OP |
|---|---|---|
| Main purpose | Reconcile plans | Make business decisions |
| Participants | Functional managers and planners | Senior executives |
| Focus | Operational and financial alignment | Strategic business alignment |
| Issues | Analyze and develop solutions | Decide on escalated issues |
| Scenarios | Develop and evaluate | Select or approve |
| Authority | Usually recommends | Has decision-making authority |
| Output | Recommendations | Approved integrated plan |

### Example

**Situation:** Expected demand is 120,000 units, but production capacity is only 100,000 units.

**During Pre-S&OP:**

The team evaluates:
- Overtime
- Additional shifts
- Outsourcing
- Inventory utilization
- Customer prioritization

The team determines the cost and operational impact of each option.

**During Executive S&OP:**

Senior management decides:
- Which option to implement?
- How much additional budget to approve?
- Which customers receive priority?
- Is the company willing to accept additional inventory or cost?
- What is the financial impact of the decision?

Remember:

**Pre-S&OP recommends. Executive S&OP decides and approves.**

# 2. Participants and Responsibilities

## 2.1 Who attends the Executive S&OP Meeting?

Hindi lahat ng employees kailangan umattend. Usually, ang participants ay senior leaders na may authority over business performance, resources, and strategic priorities.

| Participant | Primary Responsibility |
|---|---|
| CEO / General Manager | Overall business direction and final strategic alignment |
| Sales Director | Customer demand, revenue opportunities, and customer commitments |
| Operations Director | Production capabilities, capacity, and operational performance |
| Supply Chain Director | Material availability, inventory, suppliers, and logistics |
| Finance Director / CFO | Revenue, costs, margins, cash flow, and financial feasibility |
| Procurement Head | Supplier risks, sourcing strategies, and purchasing commitments |
| Product / Engineering Head | Product changes, technical constraints, and new product requirements |
| S&OP Leader | Facilitates the process and ensures an integrated business plan |

Depending on company size and organizational structure, may iba pang participants such as HR, Commercial, Marketing, or Business Unit Heads.

## 2.2 Understanding their different perspectives

Importanteng maintindihan na magkakaiba ang priorities ng bawat executive.

**Sales Director**

Ang pangunahing concern niya:

"Can we fulfill customer demand and achieve our revenue targets?"

**Operations Director**

"Can we produce the required volume with our available capacity and resources?"

**Supply Chain Director**

"Can we secure the materials and deliver products on time?"

**Finance Director**

"Can we achieve the business targets without exceeding the approved financial limits?"

**CEO / General Manager**

"Are we making decisions that maximize the overall business performance?"

Notice that each department has a different perspective.

The purpose of Executive S&OP is to make sure that these perspectives are aligned.

## 2.3 Executive Sponsor vs. Decision-Makers

Magkaiba ang responsibilities nila.

**Executive Sponsor**

The executive sponsor is the senior leader who supports the S&OP process, reinforces organizational participation, and ensures that the process has management authority.

**Decision-Makers**

These are executives authorized to approve decisions within their respective areas or through collective management agreement.

For example:

- Operations Director may approve certain production adjustments within delegated authority.
- CFO may approve financial commitments within financial authority.
- CEO or General Manager may approve major strategic investments.

Hindi automatic na lahat ng decisions ay exclusively sa CEO. Depende ito sa company's governance structure and delegated authority.

## 2.4 Accountability

One important principle:

**Decision authority and execution accountability are different.**

Example:

The executive team approves a second production shift.

- Decision owner: Executive team / authorized executive.
- Implementation owner: Operations Director.
- Supporting teams: HR, Maintenance, Supply Chain.
- Monitoring owner: S&OP team or designated performance owner.

Hindi sapat na may decision. Kailangan may responsible person na magpapatupad at magmo-monitor nito.

# 3. Meeting Inputs and Preparation

Bago magsimula ang Executive S&OP Meeting, dapat may consolidated information na ang management team.

Hindi ideal na sa meeting pa lang malalaman ng executives na may production shortage, supplier issue, o financial gap.

The executive meeting should focus on **decisions, not basic data gathering.**

## 3.1 Required Meeting Inputs

May six major categories ng information na karaniwang inihahanda.

| Meeting Input | Information Required | Management Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Demand Plan | Forecast, customer orders, demand changes | Understand revenue opportunities and demand risks |
| Supply Plan | Production capacity, materials, inventory, supplier constraints | Determine supply feasibility |
| Financial Plan | Revenue, COGS, operating expenses, profit margins | Evaluate financial performance |
| Business Performance | Actual vs. target KPIs | Identify major deviations |
| Risks and Opportunities | Supplier delays, capacity shortages, new customers | Identify potential business impacts |
| Escalated Issues | Unresolved Pre-S&OP decisions | Obtain executive direction and approval |

Let's understand each one.

### A. Reconciled Demand and Supply Plans

Dapat may clear understanding ang management kung kaya bang suportahan ng supply organization ang expected demand.

Example:

| Metric | Monthly Plan |
|---|---:|
| Forecast Demand | 120,000 units |
| Available Production Capacity | 100,000 units |
| Available Inventory | 8,000 units |
| Total Available Supply | 108,000 units |
| Supply Gap | 12,000 units |

May 12,000-unit gap.

Ang executive team needs to understand:
- What caused the gap?
- Is the shortage temporary or recurring?
- Which customers will be affected?
- What options are available?
- What is the financial impact?

Hindi sapat na ipakita lang ang shortage. Kailangan ipakita kung anong business decision ang kailangan.

### B. Financial Projections

Dito pumapasok ang Finance.

The executive team should understand how the demand and supply plans translate into financial results.

Common financial measures:

| Financial Metric | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Revenue | Expected sales value |
| COGS | Cost of Goods Sold |
| Gross Profit | Revenue minus COGS |
| Operating Expenses | Costs of running the business |
| Operating Profit | Profit after operating expenses |
| Working Capital | Funds invested in operating assets and liabilities |
| Cash Flow | Movement of cash into and out of the business |

Example:

The company may increase production by 20,000 units to support customer demand.

However, additional production could require:
- Higher material purchases
- Additional labor
- Overtime
- Higher utilities
- Additional inventory investment

Possible na tataas ang revenue pero bababa naman ang profit margin.

Kaya hindi puwedeng volume alone ang basis ng decision.

### C. Business Performance and KPIs

Management needs to compare actual performance against targets.

Common executive-level KPIs:

| KPI | What It Measures |
|---|---|
| Forecast Accuracy | How closely forecasts match actual demand |
| Forecast Bias | Whether forecasts consistently overestimate or underestimate demand |
| OTIF | On-Time In-Full customer delivery performance |
| Service Level | Ability to fulfill customer demand |
| Inventory Turns | How efficiently inventory is utilized |
| Days of Supply (DOS) | How long inventory can support demand |
| Capacity Utilization | How much available production capacity is being used |
| Revenue Attainment | Actual revenue versus revenue target |
| Gross Margin | Profitability after COGS |
| Schedule Attainment | Ability to meet production schedules |

**Important:** Hindi lahat ng KPIs kailangang ipakita sa executive dashboard. Piliin ang indicators na may material impact sa business decisions.

### D. Risks and Opportunities

Executives should also understand what could happen in the future.

Examples:

| Risk / Opportunity | Potential Business Impact |
|---|---|
| Critical supplier delay | Production interruption |
| New major customer | Additional revenue opportunity |
| Machine breakdown | Reduced production capacity |
| Higher raw material prices | Increased COGS |
| Demand decline | Excess inventory |
| New production technology | Improved productivity |

### E. Unresolved Pre-S&OP Issues

Ito ang isa sa pinakamahalagang meeting inputs.

Only issues requiring executive direction or approval should be escalated.

Examples:

- Additional capital expenditure requires approval.
- Supplier risk requires strategic sourcing decisions.
- Production capacity cannot support all customer requirements.
- Sales and Operations disagree on customer prioritization.
- Proposed inventory investment exceeds financial limits.

## 3.2 Executive Decision Paper

A useful practice is preparing a concise decision paper for every major escalated issue.

It should contain:

| Section | Required Information |
|---|---|
| Business Issue | What is the problem? |
| Current Situation | What is happening now? |
| Business Impact | What happens if no action is taken? |
| Root Cause | Why is it happening? |
| Available Options | What alternatives exist? |
| Financial Impact | What are the costs and benefits? |
| Recommendation | Which option is preferred and why? |
| Decision Required | What exactly must management approve? |

**Professional tip:** Every major executive presentation should make it easy to answer three questions:

1. What is happening?
2. Why does it matter to the business?
3. What decision do we need from management?

# 4. Executive Meeting Activities

Now, let's discuss what actually happens during the meeting.

Hindi ito simpleng presentation ng bawat department. The meeting should be structured around business performance, exceptions, and decisions.

## 4.1 Review Business Performance

First, executives review the overall performance of the organization.

Example:

| KPI | Target | Actual | Status |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Revenue | ₱50M | ₱46M | Below target |
| OTIF | 98% | 93% | Below target |
| Inventory Turns | 8.0 | 7.2 | Below target |
| Gross Margin | 30% | 28% | Below target |
| Production Attainment | 95% | 92% | Below target |

The executive team asks:

- Why is revenue below target?
- Is poor delivery performance affecting sales?
- Is inventory increasing because of inaccurate forecasts?
- Are production inefficiencies increasing costs?
- What corrective actions are necessary?

The goal is not to discuss every small variance.

The goal is to understand significant deviations that require management attention.

## 4.2 Evaluate Demand, Supply, and Financial Alignment

Next, executives examine whether the three major plans support one another.

### Integrated Business Plan

**DEMAND PLAN**

120,000 units

Expected customer requirements

↕  

**SUPPLY PLAN**

108,000 units

Available supply before additional actions

↕

**FINANCIAL PLAN**

Business Gap

Revenue exposure, incremental costs, and profitability

Executive decision: How should the organization close the gap?

The objective is to develop one integrated plan.

For example, the company may have enough demand to generate ₱50 million in revenue, but insufficient capacity to manufacture the required quantity.

The executives must decide whether to:
- Increase production capacity.
- Outsource production.
- Use existing inventory.
- Prioritize profitable customers.
- Accept some delayed customer orders.

Each decision affects the business differently.

## 4.3 Assess Business Scenarios

Executives evaluate alternatives instead of relying on only one proposed solution.

Common scenarios include:

| Scenario | Description |
|---|---|
| Base Case | Current expected demand and available resources |
| Upside Case | Higher-than-expected demand |
| Downside Case | Lower-than-expected demand |
| Constrained Case | Supply or capacity limitations |
| Recovery Case | Actions designed to recover missed targets |

Example:

A manufacturer expects demand to increase by 15% next month.

Management may evaluate:

- What if demand increases by 25%?
- What if the supplier delivers materials two weeks late?
- What if the production line experiences a major breakdown?
- What if customer demand suddenly declines?

These scenarios help executives prepare decisions before the problems actually occur.

## 4.4 Resolve Escalated Issues

This is where executive authority becomes particularly important.

Example:

Sales wants to prioritize a major customer because of a high-value contract.

Operations wants to prioritize another customer because its products are easier to manufacture and have higher production efficiency.

Finance wants to prioritize products with higher contribution margins.

These priorities may conflict.

The executive team must evaluate the total business impact and establish the final priority.

## 4.5 Approve the Integrated Plan

After reviewing performance, alternatives, and risks, management approves the business plan.

Approval means the organization has an agreed direction regarding:

- Demand assumptions.
- Supply commitments.
- Inventory strategy.
- Financial expectations.
- Customer priorities.
- Resource requirements.
- Major business risks.

The approved plan becomes the management-level reference for the next planning period.

## 4.6 Assign Actions and Responsibilities

Every important decision must have an implementation owner.

Example:

| Action | Responsible Team | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Secure alternative supplier | Procurement | November 25 |
| Implement additional shift | Operations | November 22 |
| Revalidate customer priorities | Sales | November 20 |
| Update financial forecast | Finance | November 21 |
| Monitor delivery performance | Supply Chain | Weekly |

A decision without an owner and deadline is simply a discussion.

# 5. Executive Decision-Making

Ito ang pinakaimportanteng technical and analytical section ng topic natin.

Ang Executive S&OP ay hindi lamang reporting meeting. It is a management decision-making process.

## 5.1 Decision Rights and Escalation Process

Hindi lahat ng decisions ay dapat umabot sa Executive S&OP.

May decisions na kayang resolbahin ng functional teams.

For example:

| Decision | Appropriate Decision Level |
|---|---|
| Minor production schedule adjustment | Production Planning |
| Routine material replenishment | Procurement / Supply Planning |
| Demand forecast correction | Demand Planning |
| Cross-functional supply-demand conflict | Pre-S&OP |
| Major capacity investment | Executive Management |
| Strategic customer prioritization | Executive Management |
| Major budget allocation | Authorized Senior Management |

This is called **Decision Rights**.

Decision rights define who has the authority to make, approve, and implement specific business decisions.

### Escalation Process

**Operational Issue Identified**

Production, sales, procurement, or planning

↓

**Functional Teams Analyze**

Resolve within existing authority

↓

**Pre-S&OP**

Cross-functional reconciliation and recommendations

↓

**Executive S&OP**

Approve, reject, prioritize, or request further analysis

↓

**Implementation and Monitoring**

Assigned teams execute approved decisions

Important: Escalation does not mean transferring all responsibilities to executives. The functional teams remain accountable for analyzing and implementing the decisions.

## 5.2 Exception-Based Management

One of the most important concepts in executive governance is **Management by Exception**.

Simple explanation:

Hindi kailangang pag-usapan ng senior management ang lahat ng activities ng organization.

Instead, they focus on significant deviations from the approved plan, business risks, and decisions requiring management authority.

Example:

| KPI | Target | Actual | Deviation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| OTIF | 98% | 97.5% | -0.5 pp |
| Production Attainment | 95% | 94.5% | -0.5 pp |
| Inventory Turns | 8.0 | 6.0 | -2.0 |
| Gross Margin | 30% | 25% | -5 pp |

Which issue should receive the most attention?

Potentially, gross margin and inventory turns, because they show substantial business exposure.

However, importance is not determined by numerical deviation alone.

Consider:
- Financial impact
- Customer impact
- Urgency
- Strategic importance
- Probability of occurrence
- Ability to resolve the issue

For example, a 0.5-percentage-point decline in OTIF may be extremely serious if it affects a major customer contract.

### What makes an issue executive-level?

An issue should generally be escalated when:

1. It exceeds the authority of the functional team.
2. It requires significant financial resources.
3. It affects strategic business priorities.
4. It creates substantial customer or financial risk.
5. It requires trade-offs between departments.

## 5.3 Balancing Competing Business Priorities

Executives rarely make decisions where every department benefits equally.

Usually, there are trade-offs.

| Business Priority | What Management Wants | Possible Conflict |
|---|---|---|
| Customer Service | Deliver all customer orders on time | Requires additional capacity |
| Capacity | Maintain feasible production schedules | May limit available supply |
| Inventory | Maintain sufficient stock | Increases working capital |
| Revenue | Maximize sales | May require expensive production |
| Cost | Minimize operating expenses | May reduce flexibility |
| Profitability | Protect contribution margin | May require customer prioritization |

### Understanding Business Trade-offs

Consider three options:

**Option A: Increase Production Through Overtime**

- Higher production volume.
- Better customer service.
- Higher labor and utility costs.
- Possible employee fatigue.

**Option B: Outsource Production**

- Additional capacity without major capital investment.
- Potentially higher unit cost.
- Additional quality and supplier risks.

**Option C: Prioritize Customer Orders**

- Protects selected strategic customers.
- Reduces immediate capacity pressure.
- Some customers may experience delayed deliveries.
- Potential revenue and relationship consequences.

There is no universally correct answer.

The best decision depends on the organization's strategic priorities and the overall financial and operational impact.

## 5.4 How Executives Choose Between Competing Priorities

A useful decision-making framework is:

1. **Customer Impact:** Which option protects important customer commitments?
2. **Financial Impact:** Which option produces the best financial outcome?
3. **Operational Feasibility:** Can the organization realistically execute it?
4. **Risk Exposure:** What could go wrong?
5. **Strategic Alignment:** Does it support long-term business objectives?

Executives should avoid optimizing one department's performance at the expense of the entire organization.

For example:

Sales might want maximum volume, while Finance wants maximum margin.

The executive team must evaluate the profitability and strategic importance of the additional volume, rather than automatically choosing either side.

### Important concept: Enterprise-Wide Optimization

This means making decisions that improve the performance of the entire business rather than maximizing an individual department's KPI.

This is one of the central principles of effective S&OP.

# 6. Outputs of Executive S&OP

Pagkatapos ng meeting, dapat may documented outputs.

Hindi dapat matatapos ang meeting sa verbal agreement lamang.

## 6.1 Approved S&OP Plan

The main output is the management-approved integrated business plan.

It should establish:

- Expected demand.
- Approved supply plan.
- Production and capacity priorities.
- Inventory strategy.
- Financial expectations.
- Major business assumptions.

## 6.2 Management Decisions and Resource Allocation

Examples:

| Management Decision | Resource / Business Impact |
|---|---|
| Approve additional shift | Additional labor and operating costs |
| Approve new supplier | Additional sourcing arrangements |
| Increase safety stock | Additional working capital |
| Approve machine investment | Capital expenditure |
| Prioritize strategic customer | Allocation of constrained capacity |

## 6.3 Approved Business Priorities

Executives must establish what matters most.

Examples:

- Prioritize strategic customer deliveries.
- Protect high-margin products.
- Reduce excess and obsolete inventory.
- Secure critical raw materials.
- Maintain minimum service-level requirements.

## 6.4 Risks and Mitigation Actions

All major risks should have corresponding actions.

| Risk | Mitigation Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier delivery delay | Identify alternative supplier | Procurement |
| Capacity shortage | Implement additional shift | Operations |
| Excess inventory | Review production and purchasing plans | Supply Chain |
| Demand decline | Adjust production commitments | Sales and Operations |

## 6.5 Action Register

The action register is an important document used to monitor management decisions.

Example:

| ID | Executive Decision / Action | Owner | Due Date | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 001 | Approve overtime | Operations | Nov 22 | Open |
| 002 | Negotiate supplier allocation | Procurement | Nov 24 | Open |
| 003 | Reforecast revenue | Finance | Nov 21 | In Progress |
| 004 | Confirm customer priorities | Sales | Nov 20 | Completed |

The S&OP team monitors the status and escalates overdue or unresolved actions when necessary.

## 6.6 Communication Requirements

Approved decisions must be communicated to the appropriate departments.

For example:

If management approves a 10% increase in production capacity, the information must reach:

- Production Planning
- Manufacturing
- Procurement
- Supply Chain
- Finance
- Sales

Otherwise, different departments may continue operating using different assumptions.

**One approved plan should lead to one aligned organizational direction.**

# 7. Practical Manufacturing Case Study

Let's apply everything using a realistic manufacturing situation.

## 7.1 Business Situation

Imagine a manufacturing company producing electronic components for automotive and industrial customers.

The company receives an unexpected increase in customer demand.

At the same time, it faces production capacity constraints and supplier delivery risks.

### Manufacturing Business Case

*Illustrative assumptions for one monthly planning period*

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Forecast demand | 120,000 units |
| Normal production capacity | 100,000 units |
| Available inventory | 8,000 units |
| Supply gap | 12,000 units |

**Additional business conditions**

- One critical raw material supplier is experiencing a two-week delivery delay.
- Production is already operating at high utilization.
- Sales expects the demand increase to continue for three months.
- Finance has a strict profitability target.
- Some customers have contractual delivery commitments.

## 7.2 Initial Supply-Demand Gap

Let's calculate the shortage.

**Formula:**

\[
\text{Supply Gap}=\text{Demand}-\text{Available Supply}
\]

Where:

\[
\text{Available Supply}=\text{Production}+\text{Available Inventory}
\]

Substitute:

\[
=120,000-(100,000+8,000)
\]

\[
\boxed{12,000\text{ units}}
\]

The company cannot currently support all forecast demand.

This is the business issue that must be addressed.

## 7.3 Alternatives Evaluated

During Pre-S&OP, the cross-functional team develops three possible scenarios.

The assumptions below are illustrative.

| Option | Description | Additional Cost |
|---|---|---:|
| A | Overtime production of 12,000 units | ₱600,000 |
| B | Outsource 12,000 units | ₱960,000 |
| C | Produce 100,000 units and prioritize customer deliveries | No additional production cost |

Let's evaluate each.

### Option A: Overtime Production

The company increases production using overtime.

Assumption:
- Additional production: 12,000 units.
- Additional overtime cost: ₱600,000.

**Advantage**

- Full demand can potentially be supported.
- Existing production processes are retained.
- Customer commitments can be protected.

**Disadvantage**

- Higher labor and operating expenses.
- Risk of employee fatigue.
- Raw material availability may still prevent execution.

**Critical consideration:** Additional production capacity is useless if the required raw materials are unavailable.

Therefore, the supplier delay must also be addressed.

### Option B: Outsource Production

The company transfers some production requirements to an external manufacturer.

Assumption:
- Outsourced volume: 12,000 units.
- Additional cost: ₱960,000.

**Advantage**

- Adds capacity without immediately purchasing new equipment.
- Reduces pressure on internal production.
- Potentially supports all customer demand.

**Disadvantage**

- Higher production cost.
- External quality risks.
- Additional supplier qualification requirements.
- Possible logistics complications.

### Option C: Customer Prioritization

The company maintains normal production capacity and allocates available supply to priority customers.

Assumption:
- Available production: 100,000 units.
- Inventory: 8,000 units.
- Total supply: 108,000 units.
- Unfulfilled demand: 12,000 units.

**Advantage**

- No additional production expenditure.
- Maintains internal production stability.
- Avoids excessive operational costs.

**Disadvantage**

- Some customers experience delayed deliveries.
- Potential lost sales.
- Possible contractual penalties.
- Customer relationship risks.

## 7.4 Compare the Financial Implications

Assume the following additional financial data:

| Financial Variable | Assumption |
|---|---:|
| Selling price per unit | ₱100 |
| Normal production cost per unit | ₱65 |
| Overtime incremental cost | ₱50 per additional unit |
| Outsourcing cost | ₱80 per unit |

Calculate the contribution before other fixed expenses.

**Option A: Overtime**

Additional revenue:

\[
12,000 \times ₱100=₱1,200,000
\]

Additional production cost:

\[
12,000 \times ₱50=₱600,000
\]

Additional contribution:

\[
₱1,200,000-₱600,000
\]

\[
\boxed{₱600,000}
\]

**Option B: Outsourcing**

Additional revenue:

\[
12,000 \times ₱100=₱1,200,000
\]

Outsourcing cost:

\[
12,000 \times ₱80=₱960,000
\]

Additional contribution:

\[
₱1,200,000-₱960,000
\]

\[
\boxed{₱240,000}
\]

**Option C: Customer Prioritization**

The company does not produce the additional 12,000 units.

Potential sales revenue exposure:

\[
12,000 \times ₱100=₱1,200,000
\]

However, this is not automatically an actual revenue loss. Some orders may simply be delayed and recognized in a later period.

The company must evaluate:
- Delayed revenue.
- Permanently lost orders.
- Contractual penalties.
- Customer retention risks.

## 7.5 Executive Decision

Now, imagine that the Pre-S&OP team recommends Option A, overtime production.

However, management discovers that the critical raw material supplier cannot guarantee sufficient material delivery.

The executive team therefore considers a combined strategy:

1. Approve overtime production for the volume supported by available materials.
2. Authorize Procurement to secure additional raw materials from an alternative qualified supplier.
3. Prioritize strategic customers based on contractual commitments and profitability.
4. Use outsourcing only as a contingency if internal capacity and material recovery are insufficient.
5. Review the situation weekly until supply stabilizes.

Notice something important:

**The executive decision does not necessarily need to select only one scenario.**

Management may combine different alternatives to create the best overall solution.

## 7.6 Business Consequences of the Decision

| Business Area | Expected Impact |
|---|---|
| Customer Service | Priority customers receive protected supply |
| Production | Higher utilization through overtime |
| Procurement | Additional supplier coordination |
| Inventory | Potential increase in raw material inventory |
| Finance | Additional operating expenses |
| Revenue | Better opportunity to capture expected demand |
| Profitability | Additional costs must be monitored |
| Risk Management | Reduced dependence on the delayed supplier |

## 7.7 Final Executive Decision Record

A simplified decision record could look like this:

| Decision | Approved Action | Accountable Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity | Approve overtime within authorized budget | Operations Director |
| Material | Secure additional qualified supply | Procurement Head |
| Customers | Confirm delivery priority | Sales Director |
| Financials | Update projected costs and margins | Finance Director |
| Monitoring | Review weekly supply-demand position | S&OP Leader |

**Key lesson from the case:**

The executive team is not simply choosing the cheapest option.

It is choosing a business strategy that balances customer commitments, supply feasibility, financial performance, and long-term business objectives.

# 8. S&OP Analyst's Role in Executive S&OP

Now let's discuss your role as an S&OP Analyst.

Importanteng maintindihan ito because you will not necessarily be the person approving major business decisions.

However, you can significantly influence the quality of those decisions through accurate data, analysis, reporting, and recommendations.

Your responsibility is to make complex operational information understandable to senior management.

## 8.1 Preparing Executive Presentations

One of your responsibilities is preparing executive-level reports and presentations.

A typical Executive S&OP presentation may contain:

| Slide | Content | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Executive Summary | Overall business situation |
| 2 | Business Performance | Actual vs. target KPIs |
| 3 | Demand Outlook | Forecast and demand changes |
| 4 | Supply Outlook | Capacity, inventory, and material availability |
| 5 | Financial Outlook | Revenue, cost, and profitability |
| 6 | Key Risks and Opportunities | Major business exposures |
| 7 | Scenario Comparison | Available alternatives |
| 8 | Management Decisions Required | Recommendations and approvals |
| 9 | Previous Action Status | Progress of approved actions |

Hindi ibig sabihin na kailangan laging siyam ang slides. Depende ito sa company structure and meeting requirements.

The important thing is that the presentation tells a coherent business story.

## 8.2 Presenting Exceptions and Business Gaps

As an analyst, kailangan mong matukoy kung anong information ang importanteng makita ng executives.

Example:

Instead of presenting:

"Production output decreased from 100,000 units to 92,000 units."

Provide the business context:

"Production output declined by 8%, resulting in a potential supply shortage of 8,000 units. If the shortage continues, priority customer deliveries may be affected, potentially creating revenue exposure."

The second statement is more useful for executive decision-making.

Why?

Because it connects an operational deviation to its business consequence.

## 8.3 Translating Operational Issues into Financial Impact

Ito ang skill na gusto kong ma-develop mo as an S&OP professional.

Let's compare:

| Operational Information | Business Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Production shortage of 5,000 units | Potential lost or delayed revenue |
| Excess inventory of 20,000 units | Increased working capital |
| Supplier delay of 14 days | Potential production interruption |
| Low capacity utilization | Possible inefficient resource allocation |
| Higher overtime hours | Increased operating expenses |
| Poor forecast accuracy | Higher inventory and service-level risks |

An effective analyst understands both sides:

**Operational Metrics → Business Impact → Management Decision**

Hindi sapat na marunong ka lang mag-compute ng inventory, forecast, or production attainment.

You should also understand what those numbers mean financially and strategically.

## 8.4 Developing Recommendations

Your job is not just to identify problems.

You should also support management by evaluating possible solutions.

A good recommendation contains:

1. Problem statement.
2. Supporting data.
3. Root cause.
4. Available alternatives.
5. Financial and operational impact.
6. Recommended action.
7. Expected result.

For example:

"Based on the projected 12,000-unit supply shortage, we recommend implementing controlled overtime production while securing additional raw material supply. This option provides an opportunity to protect strategic customer commitments while avoiding the higher incremental cost of full outsourcing."

Notice that the recommendation includes the reason behind the proposed decision.

## 8.5 Recording Executive Decisions

After the meeting, the S&OP Analyst may help maintain the decision and action registers.

You should capture:

- What was approved?
- What was rejected?
- What assumptions were agreed upon?
- Who is responsible?
- When is the deadline?
- What is the expected result?
- When should the issue be reviewed again?

This is essential because the S&OP process does not end when the meeting ends.

The approved decisions must translate into actual business execution.

## 8.6 Monitoring Previous Decisions

At the next monthly S&OP cycle, the analyst should evaluate whether previous executive decisions were effective.

Example:

Management approved overtime to increase production by 12,000 units.

The following month, review:

| Metric | Expected | Actual |
|---|---:|---:|
| Additional Production | 12,000 | 10,000 |
| Additional Cost | ₱600,000 | ₱650,000 |
| Customer Delivery | 100% | 96% |

Questions to investigate:

- Why was production below the target?
- Why did additional costs exceed expectations?
- Were customer commitments protected?
- Was the decision effective?
- Does management need to adjust the strategy?

This creates a continuous improvement loop.

**Executive Decision → Implementation → Performance Monitoring → Management Review**

# 9. Key Takeaways

Let's consolidate the important concepts before proceeding to the knowledge check.

## 9.1 Concepts You Must Remember

| Concept | What You Should Understand |
|---|---|
| Executive S&OP | Senior management decision-making and approval meeting |
| Pre-S&OP | Cross-functional reconciliation and preparation of recommendations |
| Executive Sponsor | Senior leader who supports and reinforces S&OP governance |
| Decision Rights | Defines who has authority to make specific decisions |
| Escalation | Elevating issues beyond the authority of operational teams |
| Management by Exception | Focusing on significant deviations and business risks |
| Enterprise-Wide Optimization | Making decisions that benefit the entire organization |
| Integrated Business Plan | Aligned demand, supply, and financial plans |
| Action Register | Documents approved actions, owners, and deadlines |
| Executive Governance | Management oversight, accountability, and decision authority |

## 9.2 Connections to Previous Topics and Modules

Mahalagang makita mo kung paano connected ang topics natin.

| Previous Topic | Connection to Executive S&OP |
|---|---|
| Topic 8.1: S&OP Meeting Structure | Establishes the overall monthly S&OP governance and meeting sequence |
| Topic 8.5: Pre-S&OP Meeting | Provides reconciled plans, evaluated scenarios, and escalated recommendations |

Think of the relationship this way:

- Module 6 tells the company what customers are expected to need.
- Module 7 determines whether the company can supply that demand.
- Topic 8.5 reconciles the competing plans and prepares recommendations.
- Topic 8.6 enables senior management to make decisions and approve the integrated business direction.

## 9.3 The Most Important Principle

I want you to remember this statement:

**The purpose of Executive S&OP is not to make every department happy. It is to ensure that every department operates according to one approved business direction.**

Sales may want more volume.

Operations may want stable production.

Supply Chain may want sufficient inventory.

Finance may want lower costs.

But the company needs one integrated plan that balances all these objectives.

That is the essence of Executive S&OP.
