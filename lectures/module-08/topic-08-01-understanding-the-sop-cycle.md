# Module 8 – S&OP Process Execution and Governance
## Topic 8.1: Understanding the S&OP Cycle

**Main learning objective:** Understand how different departments work together through a structured, recurring process to create, review, reconcile, approve, and execute one integrated business plan.

---

# 1. Topic Overview

## 1.1 What are we going to learn?

Sa Modules 1–7, pinag-aralan natin ang iba't ibang components ng Sales & Operations Planning:

- Demand planning and forecasting.
- Supply planning and capacity management.
- Inventory management.
- Material and procurement planning.
- Demand-supply balancing.
- KPI monitoring and performance analysis.
- Financial reconciliation and scenario analysis.

Pero may isang importanteng tanong:

**Paano pinagsasama-sama ng isang company ang lahat ng ito para makabuo ng isang coordinated business plan?**

Dito pumapasok ang S&OP Cycle.

Imagine a manufacturing company:

- Sales expects customer demand to increase by 20%.
- Production says its current capacity cannot accommodate the increase.
- Procurement says critical raw materials have an eight-week lead time.
- Finance says the company must protect its gross margin.
- Management wants to increase sales without creating excessive inventory.

Lahat ng departments ay may sariling information, targets, at priorities.

Ang problema: hindi automatic na magkakatugma ang kanilang plans.

Kaya kailangan ng structured process kung saan:

1. Kinokolekta ang business information.
2. Gumagawa ng demand plan.
3. Gumagawa ng supply plan.
4. Nirereconcile ang differences.
5. Nagdedesisyon ang management.
6. Ipinapatupad ang approved plan.

Ang buong sequence na ito ang tinatawag na **S&OP Cycle**.

## 1.2 Why is this topic important?

Ang S&OP Cycle ang nagsisilbing operating framework ng entire S&OP process.

Hindi sapat na marunong kang mag-compute ng forecast, safety stock, production capacity, inventory requirements, or financial projections.

Kailangan mo ring maintindihan:

- Kailan ginagawa ang bawat analysis?
- Sino ang responsible sa bawat activity?
- Anong information ang kailangan?
- Kanino ipinapasa ang results?
- Sino ang nag-aapprove ng decisions?
- Paano mino-monitor kung nasusunod ang approved plan?

Bilang S&OP Analyst, hindi lang numbers ang trabaho mo. Kailangan mong maintindihan kung paano gumagalaw ang information at decisions across the organization.

## 1.3 Connection to the 10 learning modules

Important distinction:

**Ang five stages ng S&OP ay hindi five modules ng ating curriculum.**

Ang five stages ay ang actual business process na ginagamit ng companies. Ang 10 modules naman ay ang structured learning program natin para maintindihan ang iba't ibang concepts, tools, methodologies, and applications ng S&OP.

Ang Modules 1–7 ang nagbigay sa atin ng foundational knowledge. Module 8 naman ang magtuturo kung paano ginagamit ang knowledge na iyon sa actual recurring business process.

# 2. Layman's Explanation

## 2.1 Think of S&OP as preparing for a large family event

Imagine na ikaw ang organizer ng isang malaking family reunion.

May iba't ibang responsibilities ang bawat tao:

| Person | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Organizer | Overall coordination |
| Food team | Estimates food requirements |
| Purchasing team | Buys ingredients |
| Kitchen team | Determines cooking capacity |
| Finance | Controls the budget |
| Family representatives | Estimate the number of attendees |

Ngayon, may problema.

The family representatives expect 300 attendees, pero ang kitchen team kayang mag-prepare ng food for only 220 people.

Meanwhile, limited ang budget, at hindi pa available ang ibang ingredients.

Hindi puwedeng basta sabihin ng organizer na:

"Okay, 300 people ang plan natin."

Kailangan munang malaman:

- Ilan talaga ang expected attendees?
- Ilan ang kaya ng kitchen?
- May enough ingredients ba?
- Kaya ba ng budget?
- Ano ang gagawin kung hindi kayang i-accommodate ang lahat?

Magkakaroon ng discussions, adjustments, and decisions.

At pagkatapos, may isang agreed plan na susundin ng lahat.

**That is essentially how S&OP works in a manufacturing company.**

Palitan lang natin ang:

- Attendees → Customer demand.
- Food requirements → Raw materials.
- Kitchen capacity → Production capacity.
- Budget → Financial targets.
- Family organizer → S&OP leadership.

## 2.2 Why does S&OP happen every month?

Hindi permanent ang business conditions.

For example:

- This month, customer demand is 10,000 units.
- Next month, a major customer increases its orders.
- A supplier experiences a delivery delay.
- A machine requires unexpected maintenance.
- Finished goods inventory becomes insufficient.
- Exchange rates or material prices change.

Kung isang beses lang gagawa ng business plan ang company, mabilis itong magiging outdated.

Kaya recurring ang S&OP.

Every month, the company reviews its latest information and updates its plans.

Think of it as a continuous business planning cycle, not a one-time meeting.

## 2.3 Individual planning versus integrated planning

Ito ang isa sa pinakamahalagang concepts sa topic natin.

**Individual departmental planning**

Each department develops its own plan based on its responsibilities.

Example:

- Sales: We need to sell 12,000 units.
- Production: We can manufacture 10,000 units.
- Procurement: We can secure materials for 9,500 units.
- Finance: We need to achieve a specific revenue and margin target.

Lahat may sariling numbers.

Pero hindi sila aligned.

**Integrated business planning**

Instead of allowing each department to operate independently, pinagsasama-sama ang kanilang plans.

The organization evaluates whether the sales target, production capability, material availability, inventory position, and financial objectives can work together.

The goal:

**One agreed business plan, supported by all relevant departments.**

Hindi ibig sabihin na pare-pareho ang responsibilities nila. Ibig sabihin, pare-pareho silang sumusunod sa isang coordinated set of business assumptions and decisions.

# 3. Technical Explanation

## 3.1 Formal definition of the S&OP Cycle

The Sales and Operations Planning (S&OP) Cycle is a structured, recurring, cross-functional business planning process through which an organization reviews demand, evaluates supply capabilities, reconciles operational and financial plans, and obtains management decisions to establish an integrated business plan.

Let's break down the technical terms.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Structured | May defined activities, responsibilities, and sequence. |
| Recurring | Regularly repeated, typically monthly. |
| Cross-functional | Involving multiple departments. |
| Demand review | Evaluation of expected customer requirements. |
| Supply review | Evaluation of the company's ability to meet demand. |
| Reconciliation | Resolving differences between demand, supply, and financial plans. |
| Management decisions | Approvals and decisions regarding business priorities and trade-offs. |
| Integrated business plan | Coordinated plan reflecting the organization's agreed direction. |

## 3.2 Why a structured monthly cycle?

May five primary reasons.

**1. Consistency**

Every department follows an established planning sequence.

Hindi magkakanya-kanya ng timing ang Sales, Operations, and Finance.

**2. Visibility**

Management gains visibility into future demand, capacity, inventory, and financial risks.

**3. Accountability**

May assigned owners for data, analysis, recommendations, and decisions.

**4. Early problem identification**

Nadidetect ang possible shortages, capacity constraints, and financial gaps before they become actual operational problems.

**5. Decision alignment**

Instead of individual departments making conflicting decisions, management establishes common priorities.

## 3.3 S&OP is a rolling planning process

One important characteristic is the *rolling planning horizon*.

For example, a company may maintain a 12-month planning horizon.

Every month:

- The previous month becomes actual historical data.
- The current month is monitored.
- The next months are updated using the latest assumptions.
- A new future month is added to maintain the 12-month horizon.

**Illustration: Rolling 12-month planning horizon**

| Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 3 | Month 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Actual | M2 | M3 | M4 |
| M5 | M6 | M7 | M8 |
| M9 | M10 | M11 | M12 |

The horizon moves forward every monthly cycle, replacing the oldest month with actual results and adding a new future month.

This allows the company to plan beyond immediate production requirements.

For example, kung may supplier lead time na eight weeks, hindi sapat na next week's production requirements lang ang titingnan.

Kailangan makita ng company ang future material requirements.

# 4. The Five Main Stages of S&OP

Ito ang core ng ating lesson.

The traditional S&OP process commonly follows five major stages:

1. **Data Gathering:** Collect and validate business information.
2. **Demand Planning:** Determine what customers are expected to need.
3. **Supply Planning:** Determine how the company can fulfill expected demand.
4. **Pre-S&OP Meeting:** Evaluate gaps, scenarios, and proposed solutions.
5. **Executive S&OP Meeting:** Make final management decisions and approve the integrated plan.

Let's examine each stage in detail.

## 4.1 Stage 1: Data Gathering

**Primary purpose:** Collect, consolidate, validate, and prepare the information required for the current S&OP cycle.

Simple explanation:

Bago magplano ang company, kailangan munang malaman ang current business situation.

Hindi puwedeng gumawa ng demand forecast or production plan gamit ang outdated or incorrect information.

### Key activities

1. Collect historical sales and shipment data.
2. Review existing customer orders and backlog.
3. Retrieve current inventory levels.
4. Collect production performance and capacity information.
5. Review supplier availability and procurement lead times.
6. Gather financial actuals and business targets.
7. Validate data accuracy and completeness.
8. Identify unusual changes or data discrepancies.

### Participants and responsibilities

| Department | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Sales | Customer orders, shipments, sales history |
| Marketing | Market trends, promotions, product launches |
| Operations | Production output, downtime, capacity |
| Supply Chain | Inventory, backlog, logistics information |
| Procurement | Supplier availability, lead times |
| Finance | Revenue, costs, margins, financial actuals |
| S&OP Analyst | Consolidation, validation, reporting |

### Inputs

- Historical sales data.
- Customer orders.
- Sales backlog.
- Inventory reports.
- Production reports.
- Capacity data.
- Supplier delivery information.
- Financial performance reports.

### Outputs

- Validated historical data.
- Updated inventory position.
- Current backlog report.
- Updated capacity information.
- Data exception reports.
- Planning assumptions for the current cycle.

**Important concept: Data integrity**

Data integrity means the information used in planning is accurate, complete, consistent, and reliable.

For example:

If the inventory system reports 5,000 units but the actual physical inventory is only 4,200 units, the supply plan may incorrectly assume that the company has enough inventory.

That can lead to shortages, missed deliveries, and incorrect production decisions.

## 4.2 Stage 2: Demand Planning

**Primary purpose:** Develop an agreed view of expected future customer demand.

Simple explanation:

Dito sinasagot ang question:

**"Ilang products ang kailangan nating maibenta o ma-deliver sa future?"**

Hindi pa natin tinatanong kung kaya ba itong gawin ng production.

Ang focus muna ay customer requirements and market demand.

### Key activities

1. Review historical demand.
2. Analyze statistical forecasts.
3. Review customer forecasts.
4. Evaluate confirmed orders and backlog.
5. Assess market trends.
6. Incorporate promotions and commercial activities.
7. Evaluate new product introductions.
8. Identify demand risks and opportunities.
9. Develop an unconstrained demand plan.

### What is an unconstrained demand plan?

Ito ang forecast ng expected customer demand bago limitahan ng actual supply constraints.

Example:

Sales expects 12,000 units of demand.

However, production capacity is only 10,000 units.

The initial demand plan should still reflect the 12,000-unit demand.

Bakit?

Because reducing the forecast simply because production cannot accommodate it would hide the actual business problem.

We need to see the real demand first before evaluating supply limitations.

### Participants and responsibilities

| Department | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Sales | Customer demand, account forecasts |
| Marketing | Market trends, promotions |
| Product Management | Product lifecycle, launches |
| Demand Planning | Statistical forecast and demand consolidation |
| Finance | Revenue and pricing assumptions |
| S&OP Analyst | Forecast analysis, variance reporting, consolidation |

### Inputs

- Validated historical demand.
- Statistical forecasts.
- Customer forecasts.
- Sales orders.
- Backlog.
- Market intelligence.
- Promotions and product launch plans.

### Outputs

- Consensus demand forecast.
- Unconstrained demand plan.
- Demand assumptions.
- Demand risks and opportunities.
- Demand forecast accuracy analysis.

### Example

| Product | Previous Forecast | Updated Demand |
|---|---:|---:|
| Product A | 4,000 | 5,000 |
| Product B | 3,000 | 3,500 |
| Product C | 2,000 | 3,500 |
| **Total** | **9,000** | **12,000** |

The company now expects 12,000 units of demand.

That becomes an important input for Supply Planning.

## 4.3 Stage 3: Supply Planning

**Primary purpose:** Determine whether the company can meet the expected demand using its available production capacity, materials, inventory, workforce, and supply network.

Simple explanation:

Kung ang Demand Planning ay nagtatanong ng:

"How much do customers need?"

Ang Supply Planning naman ay nagtatanong ng:

"Can we actually provide what customers need?"

### Key activities

1. Evaluate available production capacity.
2. Review production schedules and constraints.
3. Calculate material requirements.
4. Assess supplier capacity and lead times.
5. Evaluate inventory availability.
6. Identify potential shortages.
7. Review workforce and equipment requirements.
8. Develop alternative supply scenarios.
9. Identify demand-supply gaps.

### Participants and responsibilities

| Department | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Production | Production capacity and schedules |
| Operations | Equipment, labor, operational constraints |
| Supply Chain | Inventory and distribution |
| Procurement | Raw material availability and suppliers |
| Engineering | Technical constraints and process changes |
| Planning | Production and material requirements |
| Finance | Cost implications of supply alternatives |
| S&OP Analyst | Supply-demand comparison and gap analysis |

### Inputs

- Consensus demand plan.
- Current inventory.
- Production capacity.
- Production schedules.
- Material availability.
- Supplier lead times.
- Workforce availability.
- Maintenance schedules.

### Outputs

- Constrained supply plan.
- Production requirements.
- Material requirements.
- Capacity utilization.
- Inventory projections.
- Supply constraints and shortages.
- Alternative supply scenarios.

### Example

Using our previous demand plan:

| Product | Demand | Available Supply | Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Product A | 5,000 | 4,500 | -500 |
| Product B | 3,500 | 3,500 | 0 |
| Product C | 3,500 | 2,000 | -1,500 |
| **Total** | **12,000** | **10,000** | **-2,000** |

The company has a 2,000-unit supply shortage.

This is an important distinction:

The demand plan remains 12,000 units, but the currently feasible supply plan is only 10,000 units.

The gap must be evaluated and resolved.

Possible solutions:

- Increase production capacity.
- Authorize overtime.
- Outsource production.
- Expedite raw materials.
- Reallocate inventory.
- Prioritize important customers.
- Negotiate delivery schedules.

Each alternative has different cost, service, and operational implications.

## 4.4 Stage 4: Pre-S&OP Meeting

**Primary purpose:** Reconcile demand, supply, and financial plans, evaluate scenarios, and prepare recommendations for executive decision-making.

Simple explanation:

Sa first three stages, bawat department ay nag-develop na ng kanilang information and plans.

Ngayon, kailangan nilang pag-usapan kung paano pagsasamahin ang mga ito.

Ito ang stage kung saan nagiging business discussion ang operational analysis.

### Key activities

1. Review the consensus demand plan.
2. Review the proposed supply plan.
3. Identify unresolved demand-supply gaps.
4. Evaluate inventory and capacity implications.
5. Review financial impact.
6. Develop alternative scenarios.
7. Evaluate risks and opportunities.
8. Agree on proposed solutions.
9. Escalate unresolved decisions to executive management.

### Participants

Usually includes:

- Sales representatives.
- Demand Planning.
- Supply Planning.
- Operations.
- Procurement.
- Supply Chain.
- Finance.
- Product Management.
- S&OP Process Owner.

The exact participants depend on the organization's structure.

### Inputs

- Demand plan.
- Supply plan.
- Inventory projections.
- Capacity constraints.
- Material availability.
- Financial projections.
- Risk and opportunity analysis.
- Scenario evaluations.

### Outputs

- Reconciled business plan recommendation.
- Proposed supply-demand solutions.
- Financial impact analysis.
- Risk and opportunity register.
- Decision log.
- Escalation items for Executive S&OP.

### Example: Evaluating alternative solutions

Our company has a 2,000-unit shortage.

The team evaluates three scenarios.

| Scenario | Additional Supply | Additional Cost | Remaining Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A: Overtime | 1,000 | ₱100,000 | 1,000 |
| B: Outsourcing | 2,000 | ₱350,000 | 0 |
| C: Expedite materials | 1,500 | ₱200,000 | 500 |

*Illustrative assumptions only.*

The team cannot simply select the cheapest option.

It must consider:

- Customer delivery commitments.
- Additional manufacturing costs.
- Product quality.
- Supplier reliability.
- Delivery timing.
- Profitability.

The team may recommend a combination of overtime and expedited materials.

However, if the additional costs exceed the department's approval authority, management must make the decision.

That is why the issue is escalated to Executive S&OP.

## 4.5 Stage 5: Executive S&OP Meeting

**Primary purpose:** Obtain executive decisions and approval for the integrated business plan.

Simple explanation:

Dito pumapasok ang senior management.

The previous stages have already prepared the data, identified the problems, and evaluated possible solutions.

Now management must decide:

- What should the company prioritize?
- Which risks should it accept?
- Which investments should it authorize?
- Should it increase production?
- Should it adjust customer commitments?
- Can the financial targets still be achieved?

### Key activities

1. Review overall business performance.
2. Evaluate the proposed demand plan.
3. Review supply-demand gaps.
4. Assess financial implications.
5. Review major risks and opportunities.
6. Evaluate alternative scenarios.
7. Resolve escalated issues.
8. Approve the integrated business plan.
9. Assign decision owners and follow-up actions.

### Participants

Typically:

- General Manager or Business Unit Head.
- Sales Director.
- Operations Director.
- Supply Chain Director.
- Finance Director.
- Procurement leadership.
- Other relevant senior management.

The Executive S&OP meeting should focus on decisions, not detailed operational data reconciliation.

### Inputs

- Pre-S&OP recommendations.
- Demand and supply plans.
- Financial projections.
- Major risks and opportunities.
- Scenario analysis.
- Escalated decisions.
- Previous cycle's action items.

### Outputs

- Approved integrated business plan.
- Approved demand and supply assumptions.
- Management decisions.
- Approved investments or additional resources.
- Customer and production priorities.
- Assigned action items.
- Updated business risks and opportunities.

**Important:** Executive approval does not mean every problem has disappeared.

It means management has selected the direction the organization will follow, including the risks and trade-offs it is willing to accept.

# 5. S&OP Process Flow

Now that we understand the five stages individually, let's connect them.

Ang importanteng maintindihan dito ay hindi isolated meetings ang five stages. May information flow between them.

## 5.1 How information moves across the process

**End-to-end information flow**

1. **DATA GATHERING:** Historical data, orders, inventory, capacity, financials.
2. **DEMAND PLANNING:** Consensus demand and unconstrained forecast.
3. **SUPPLY PLANNING:** Capacity, materials, inventory, constrained supply.
4. **PRE-S&OP:** Reconciliation, scenario analysis, recommendations.
5. **EXECUTIVE S&OP:** Management decisions and plan approval.
6. **EXECUTION AND PERFORMANCE MONITORING:** Production, procurement, delivery, financial results, KPI monitoring.

Feedback from execution feeds the next monthly cycle.

The process is iterative, meaning the company continuously updates its understanding of the business as new information becomes available.

## 5.2 How demand and supply are reconciled

Let's revisit our example.

| Item | Units |
|---|---:|
| Unconstrained demand | 12,000 |
| Available supply | 10,000 |
| Demand-supply gap | 2,000 |

The company must determine how to address the shortage.

There are three broad possibilities:

**Option 1: Increase supply**

The company increases production, adds overtime, or obtains additional materials.

**Option 2: Manage demand**

The company negotiates delivery dates, reallocates available products, or prioritizes selected customers.

**Option 3: Accept the gap**

Management may decide that some demand cannot be fulfilled within the planning period.

This may be acceptable under certain circumstances, but it must be a conscious business decision.

The important principle is:

**Demand should represent customer requirements, while supply should represent what the organization can realistically provide.**

The reconciliation process determines how the organization will respond to the difference.

## 5.3 How financial information is incorporated

Ito ang connection natin sa Module 7.

Hindi enough na malaman ng company kung kaya nitong gumawa ng 12,000 units.

Kailangan ding malaman kung financially beneficial ba ang proposed plan.

For example:

| Financial Indicator | Current Plan | Proposed Plan |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales volume | 10,000 | 12,000 |
| Revenue | ₱5,000,000 | ₱6,000,000 |
| Production cost | ₱3,000,000 | ₱3,720,000 |
| Gross profit | ₱2,000,000 | ₱2,280,000 |
| Additional supply cost | ₱0 | ₱200,000 |

*Illustrative figures, assuming a selling price of ₱500 per unit and the stated cost assumptions.*

Under the proposed plan:

\[
\text{Gross Profit} = 6,000,000-3,720,000
\]

\[
= ₱2,280,000
\]

But we also need to deduct the additional supply cost if it is not already included in production cost:

\[
\text{Adjusted Profit}=2,280,000-200,000
\]

\[
= ₱2,080,000
\]

The proposed plan generates ₱80,000 more gross profit than the current plan, under these simplified assumptions.

Management can now evaluate whether the additional production and supply requirements justify the additional profit.

This is why Finance must participate in S&OP.

Operationally feasible does not automatically mean financially attractive.

## 5.4 How unresolved issues are escalated

Not every problem can be resolved by operational teams.

Consider this example:

- Sales wants to fulfill 12,000 units.
- Production can only provide 10,000 units.
- Procurement can expedite materials for another 1,000 units.
- The remaining 1,000 units require outsourcing.
- Outsourcing would cost an additional ₱250,000.
- Management approval is required.

The issue moves through the process:

| Stage | Action |
|---|---|
| Supply Planning | Identifies the 2,000-unit shortage. |
| Pre-S&OP | Evaluates overtime, outsourcing, and customer prioritization. |
| Executive S&OP | Decides whether to approve the additional cost. |
| Execution | Responsible teams implement the approved solution. |

This is called **management escalation**.

Escalation means transferring an unresolved issue to the appropriate decision-making authority.

It does not mean that the lower-level team failed.

It means the decision requires authority, resources, or risk acceptance beyond its responsibility.

## 5.5 What happens after approval?

The approved plan must be translated into departmental execution.

For example:

| Department | Post-S&OP Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Sales | Communicate customer commitments and priorities. |
| Production | Update production schedules. |
| Procurement | Release purchase orders and expedite materials. |
| Supply Chain | Coordinate inventory allocation and delivery. |
| Finance | Update financial projections and monitor performance. |
| Management | Monitor execution and resolve major deviations. |

One critical distinction:

**S&OP approves the integrated business direction. It does not replace detailed operational scheduling.**

For example, S&OP may approve 12,000 units of monthly production, but the production planning team still determines the daily machine schedules, sequencing, and shift assignments.

S&OP operates at an aggregated planning level, while detailed scheduling operates at a more granular level.

# 6. S&OP Calendar and Planning Timeline

## 6.1 Why does S&OP need a calendar?

Imagine that:

- Sales submits its forecast on the 10th.
- Procurement submits supplier information on the 15th.
- Production submits capacity information on the 20th.
- Finance submits financial projections on the 25th.

The Pre-S&OP meeting is scheduled on the 18th.

Problem: incomplete ang information.

Kaya kailangan ng defined planning calendar.

Every department must know:

- When to submit information.
- When data becomes final for the cycle.
- When analysis must be completed.
- When meetings will happen.
- When decisions will be approved.

## 6.2 Typical monthly S&OP calendar

The following is an illustrative monthly schedule. Actual company calendars vary.

**Example: November S&OP cycle**

Planning month: December and the forward planning horizon.

1. **Days 1–3: Data Gathering**
   - Close previous period's actual data.
   - Collect sales, inventory, and production information.
   - Validate data and resolve discrepancies.

2. **Days 4–7: Demand Planning**
   - Review statistical forecasts.
   - Obtain Sales input.
   - Evaluate customer changes.
   - Finalize consensus demand.

3. **Days 8–12: Supply Planning**
   - Evaluate capacity.
   - Review material availability.
   - Calculate inventory projections.
   - Identify shortages and constraints.

4. **Days 13–17: Pre-S&OP**
   - Reconcile demand and supply.
   - Review financial implications.
   - Compare alternative scenarios.
   - Prepare recommendations.

5. **Days 18–20: Executive S&OP**
   - Review major business issues.
   - Resolve escalations.
   - Approve the integrated business plan.

6. **Remaining days: Execution and Preparation**
   - Communicate approved decisions.
   - Implement action items.
   - Monitor business performance.
   - Prepare for the next planning cycle.

Day numbers are illustrative working-day allocations, not universal industry standards.

## 6.3 Why do data cutoffs matter?

A data cutoff is the agreed date and time after which the information for a particular planning cycle is considered frozen or ready for analysis.

For example:

The company establishes October 31 as the inventory data cutoff.

All inventory information must reflect the agreed snapshot as of that date.

Why?

Because if different departments use different inventory dates:

- Sales may assume 5,000 units are available.
- Production may use a report showing 4,700 units.
- Finance may use a different inventory valuation.

The company ends up discussing three different versions of reality.

Data cutoffs establish a common reference point.

However, data cutoff does not mean the company ignores important events afterward.

A major supplier disruption or significant customer order change must still be escalated when necessary.

## 6.4 Why are deadlines important?

The stages are dependent on each other.

If Demand Planning is delayed, Supply Planning may also be delayed.

If Supply Planning is incomplete, Pre-S&OP cannot properly evaluate scenarios.

If Pre-S&OP is delayed, Executive S&OP may not have enough time to make informed decisions.

This is called **process dependency**.

One delayed activity can affect the entire monthly cycle.

## 6.5 Preparing for the next cycle

After Executive S&OP:

1. Record all management decisions.
2. Assign responsible persons.
3. Establish completion dates.
4. Communicate approved plans.
5. Monitor actual execution.
6. Collect performance results.
7. Identify deviations.
8. Include relevant new information in the next cycle.

This is how S&OP becomes a continuous management process.

# 7. Cross-Functional Collaboration

## 7.1 Why must different departments participate?

Each department sees a different part of the business.

For example:

Sales understands customer requirements.

Operations understands production limitations.

Procurement understands supplier constraints.

Finance understands profitability and financial exposure.

Management understands business priorities and strategic direction.

No single department normally has all the information required to create a complete business plan.

That is why S&OP is cross-functional.

## 7.2 Roles of the major departments

| Department | Primary S&OP Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Sales | Customer requirements, sales forecast, commitments |
| Marketing | Market trends, promotions, product launches |
| Operations | Production capability, equipment, workforce |
| Supply Chain | Inventory, logistics, distribution |
| Procurement | Supplier capacity, material availability, lead times |
| Finance | Revenue, costs, margins, budgets |
| Management | Priorities, trade-offs, approvals |

Let's examine their relationships.

**Sales and Marketing**

They provide market intelligence and customer expectations.

Their objective is to ensure that the company understands expected demand.

**Operations and Production**

They evaluate whether the manufacturing system can support the demand plan.

Their concerns include machine capacity, labor, downtime, yield, and production efficiency.

**Procurement**

They ensure that raw materials and components can be obtained when required.

Their concerns include supplier reliability, lead times, purchasing costs, and material shortages.

**Supply Chain**

They connect inventory, production, logistics, and customer fulfillment.

Their objective is to ensure that products are available at the right location and time.

**Finance**

They translate operational plans into financial consequences.

Their focus includes revenue, cost, profitability, working capital, and financial targets.

**Management**

They make decisions when departments cannot independently resolve competing priorities.

## 7.3 Common departmental conflicts

Let's look at realistic examples.

| Conflict | Departmental Perspective | S&OP Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Demand exceeds capacity | Sales wants more production; Operations cannot accommodate it. | Evaluate overtime, outsourcing, and customer prioritization. |
| Excess inventory | Sales wants high availability; Finance wants lower working capital. | Evaluate inventory targets against service requirements. |
| Material shortage | Production needs materials; Procurement cannot obtain them immediately. | Evaluate alternative suppliers, expediting, and production allocation. |
| Low profitability | Sales wants volume growth; Finance wants margin protection. | Evaluate product mix, pricing, and profitability. |
| Limited resources | Multiple product lines compete for capacity. | Prioritize products based on business objectives. |

Notice something important:

S&OP does not eliminate departmental disagreements.

Instead, it provides a structured environment to resolve them.

## 7.4 The importance of One Set of Numbers

One of the most important S&OP principles is having one agreed set of planning numbers.

Imagine:

| Department | Monthly Demand Plan |
|---|---:|
| Sales | 12,000 units |
| Operations | 10,000 units |
| Finance | 11,000 units |
| Supply Chain | 9,500 units |

Which number should management use?

Kung walang reconciliation, magkakaiba ang assumptions ng bawat department.

The objective is to establish an agreed demand plan and a corresponding feasible supply plan, with all material differences and assumptions clearly documented.

For example:

| Planning Measure | Agreed Value |
|---|---:|
| Consensus demand | 12,000 |
| Feasible supply | 10,000 |
| Unresolved gap | 2,000 |
| Management-approved action | Additional 1,500 units |
| Remaining planned shortfall | 500 |

One set of numbers does not mean that demand and supply must always be equal.

It means everyone is working from the same agreed assumptions, numbers, and decisions.

## 7.5 Accountability and ownership

Every important activity must have a responsible owner.

For example:

| Activity | Responsible Owner |
|---|---|
| Historical demand consolidation | Demand Planning |
| Forecast assumptions | Sales / Demand Planning |
| Capacity analysis | Operations |
| Material availability | Procurement |
| Financial impact | Finance |
| Scenario consolidation | S&OP Analyst |
| Final approval | Executive Management |

A useful professional concept here is **RACI**:

- Responsible: Person who performs the task.
- Accountable: Person ultimately answerable for the result.
- Consulted: Person whose input is required.
- Informed: Person who needs to know the result.

RACI is a responsibility-assignment framework that can help companies clarify ownership within the S&OP process.

# 8. Practical Manufacturing Application

Let's now simulate an entire S&OP cycle.

This is where all the previous discussions come together.

## 8.1 Company background

Imagine a fictional manufacturing company called **ABC Electronics Manufacturing**.

The company manufactures electronic components and supplies them to industrial customers.

Its planning team is preparing the December 2026 production and business plan during November.

### ABC Electronics Manufacturing

| Business Information | Value |
|---|---:|
| Monthly production capacity | 10,000 units |
| Current finished goods inventory | 1,500 units |
| Available raw materials | 8,500 units worth of production |
| Standard production cost | ₱300/unit |
| Average selling price | ₱500/unit |
| Target demand | To be established |
| Planning horizon | 12 months |

The company has the following business situation:

- A major customer is increasing its order volume.
- Another customer has requested earlier deliveries.
- One critical raw material has an eight-week supplier lead time.
- Production is already operating near its normal capacity.
- Finance wants to protect profitability and working capital.

Now let's go through one complete monthly S&OP cycle.

## Stage 1: Data Gathering

**Activity: Establish the current business position.**

The S&OP Analyst collects information from different departments.

| Information | Source | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| Previous month demand | Sales | 9,000 units |
| Current customer backlog | Sales | 2,000 units |
| Finished goods inventory | Supply Chain | 1,500 units |
| Available raw materials | Procurement | 8,500 units |
| Monthly production capacity | Operations | 10,000 units |
| Standard production cost | Finance | ₱300/unit |

The analyst validates whether the reports use the same planning period and whether the inventory and order data are reliable.

**Output:** Validated planning database and current business position.

## Stage 2: Demand Planning

**Activity: Develop the expected customer demand.**

Sales and Demand Planning review the latest customer forecasts.

| Customer | Previous Forecast | Updated Forecast |
|---|---:|---:|
| Customer A | 4,000 | 5,500 |
| Customer B | 3,000 | 3,500 |
| Customer C | 2,000 | 3,000 |
| **Total** | **9,000** | **12,000** |

The consensus demand forecast is now 12,000 units.

Sales confirms that the increase is driven by higher customer requirements and expected new orders.

**Output:** 12,000-unit unconstrained demand plan.

## Stage 3: Supply Planning

**Activity: Determine how much the company can actually supply.**

Operations and Procurement evaluate the production and material requirements.

The company has:

- 1,500 units of finished goods inventory.
- Raw materials sufficient for 8,500 units of additional production.
- Production capacity of 10,000 units.

Therefore, its potential available supply is:

\[
1,500+8,500=10,000\text{ units}
\]

Assuming all 1,500 units of inventory are usable and available for the planned demand, the company has a supply gap of:

\[
12,000-10,000=2,000\text{ units}
\]

**Supply-demand position**

- Demand requirement: 12,000 units.
- Potential available supply: 10,000 units.
- Supply gap: 2,000 units.

Procurement identifies that additional materials may be available through expedited supplier deliveries.

Operations confirms that additional shifts may be possible, subject to labor and machine availability.

**Output:** Supply plan showing 10,000 units of potential supply and a 2,000-unit gap.

## Stage 4: Pre-S&OP Meeting

**Activity: Evaluate possible solutions.**

The departments discuss three alternatives.

| Alternative | Additional Supply | Additional Cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| Additional production shifts | 1,000 units | ₱100,000 |
| Outsourced production | 2,000 units | ₱350,000 |
| Expedited raw materials | 1,500 units | ₱180,000 |

These are illustrative scenario assumptions.

The team identifies the following considerations:

- Additional shifts are cheaper but may not close the entire gap.
- Outsourcing can close the gap but increases costs.
- Expedited materials may provide additional supply but depend on supplier confirmation and available production capacity.

The proposed solution is to combine additional shifts and expedited materials.

However, the team must validate that the additional materials can be processed within available production capacity.

The team prepares a recommendation and identifies the remaining risk.

**Output:** Recommended supply scenario, financial analysis, and management escalation.

## Stage 5: Executive S&OP Meeting

**Activity: Make the final business decisions.**

The executive team reviews:

1. The 12,000-unit demand forecast.
2. The available 10,000-unit supply.
3. The 2,000-unit gap.
4. Alternative supply scenarios.
5. Additional production and procurement costs.
6. Customer service and financial implications.

Management decides to:

- Approve additional production shifts.
- Authorize expedited raw material procurement.
- Prioritize Customer A's delivery requirements.
- Review the remaining supply gap before confirming final customer commitments.
- Assign Procurement and Operations to monitor the additional supply.

**Output:** Approved integrated business plan and assigned management actions.

## 8.2 Complete cycle summary

| Stage | Main Activity | Key Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Data Gathering | Collect and validate data | Validated business information |
| 2. Demand Planning | Establish expected demand | 12,000-unit demand plan |
| 3. Supply Planning | Evaluate available supply | 10,000-unit supply plan and gap |
| 4. Pre-S&OP | Evaluate alternatives | Recommended business solution |
| 5. Executive S&OP | Approve decisions | Approved integrated business plan |

The biggest lesson from this scenario:

**S&OP is not simply about producing a forecast or production schedule. It is about connecting customer demand, operational capabilities, resource requirements, financial consequences, and management decisions into one coordinated business plan.**

# 9. S&OP Governance and Business Decisions

Now let's move from understanding the process to understanding how companies control and manage it.

This is where we introduce **S&OP Governance**.

## 9.1 What is S&OP Governance?

S&OP Governance refers to the management structure, responsibilities, decision-making authority, policies, and performance controls that ensure the S&OP process operates effectively.

Simple explanation:

Kung ang S&OP Cycle ang sequence ng activities, ang Governance naman ang rules and management structure na nagpapatakbo sa sequence na iyon.

For example:

- Who owns the demand forecast?
- Who approves production capacity changes?
- Who can authorize additional spending?
- Who resolves conflicts between departments?
- Who is accountable for executing management decisions?

Without governance, puwedeng magkaroon ng meetings pero walang clear decisions or accountability.

## 9.2 Three levels of S&OP decision-making

Companies may organize decisions into three broad levels.

| Level | Main Responsibility | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Operational | Execute approved plans | Adjust daily production schedules |
| Tactical | Coordinate monthly business plans | Resolve supply-demand imbalances |
| Strategic | Establish business direction | Approve major capacity investments |

S&OP primarily connects tactical and strategic decision-making, while providing direction to operational execution.

### Example: Production capacity problem

ABC Electronics needs 12,000 units, but its current capacity is only 10,000.

Different decisions require different levels of authority.

**Operational decision:**

Can Production rearrange its daily machine schedules to improve output?

**Tactical decision:**

Can Operations add overtime or redistribute production across facilities?

**Strategic decision:**

Should management invest ₱20 million in another production line?

The S&OP process helps identify which decisions can be handled at each level and which require escalation.

## 9.3 Management review of risks and opportunities

During Pre-S&OP and Executive S&OP, management should evaluate both risks and opportunities.

| Category | Example | Potential Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Demand risk | Major customer cancels orders | Excess inventory |
| Supply risk | Supplier delivery delay | Production shortage |
| Capacity risk | Machine breakdown | Reduced output |
| Financial risk | Higher material prices | Lower margins |
| Demand opportunity | New customer order | Additional revenue |
| Supply opportunity | Improved production efficiency | Higher available capacity |
| Procurement opportunity | Better supplier pricing | Lower material costs |

A professional S&OP Analyst should not merely report that a problem exists.

The analyst should help explain:

1. What happened?
2. Why did it happen?
3. What is the potential impact?
4. What alternatives are available?
5. What decision is required?
6. Who should be accountable?

## 9.4 Understanding trade-offs

A trade-off happens when improving one business objective may negatively affect another.

For example:

| Decision | Benefit | Potential Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| Increase inventory | Better product availability | Higher working capital |
| Increase overtime | Higher production output | Higher labor costs |
| Outsource production | Additional capacity | Higher production costs |
| Expedite materials | Faster material availability | Additional logistics expenses |
| Reduce production | Lower operating expenses | Potential customer shortages |

There is rarely one perfect solution.

The goal is to identify the most appropriate balance based on the company's business priorities.

This is why Executive S&OP is important.

Management must evaluate the entire business, rather than optimize only one department's performance.

## 9.5 What happens after the Executive S&OP meeting?

The process does not end after approval.

There are five important post-meeting activities.

**1. Decision communication**

The approved business plan is communicated to all affected departments.

**2. Action assignment**

Every approved action must have an owner and target completion date.

**3. Operational execution**

Production, procurement, inventory, and customer fulfillment plans are updated.

**4. Performance monitoring**

Actual results are compared with the approved plan.

**5. Feedback and continuous improvement**

Deviations are analyzed and incorporated into the next planning cycle.

## 9.6 Actual performance versus approved plan

Let's assume that the company approved the following monthly plan:

| KPI | Approved Plan | Actual Result |
|---|---:|---:|
| Customer demand | 12,000 | 12,000 |
| Available supply | 12,000 | 11,200 |
| Actual shipments | 12,000 | 11,000 |
| Production cost per unit | ₱300 | ₱315 |

The company failed to deliver 1,000 units against its expected demand.

Production cost also exceeded the planned amount.

The next S&OP cycle should investigate:

- Why did production achieve only 11,200 units?
- Why were shipments lower than available supply?
- Were there machine breakdowns?
- Were raw materials delivered late?
- Did product quality issues reduce available output?
- Why did unit production costs increase?

This is called the **feedback loop**.

The company uses actual performance to improve future assumptions and decisions.

| Concept | What You Should Remember |
|---|---|
| S&OP Cycle | A recurring process for developing an integrated business plan. |
| Monthly Cycle | Ensures plans reflect updated business conditions. |
| Data Gathering | Establishes reliable information for planning. |
| Demand Planning | Determines expected customer requirements. |
| Supply Planning | Evaluates the company's ability to meet demand. |

1. Demand Planning and Supply Planning are different. Demand represents what customers need; supply represents what the company can provide.
2. Pre-S&OP prepares recommendations, while Executive S&OP makes management decisions.
3. S&OP is a recurring monthly process, not a one-time planning exercise.
4. Financial information must be considered alongside operational feasibility.
5. The cycle continues after executive approval through execution, monitoring, and feedback.
