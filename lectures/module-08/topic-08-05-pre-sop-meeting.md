# Module 8 – S&OP Meeting Process
## Topic 8.5: Pre-S&OP Meeting

**Main learning objective:** Understand how a company brings together Demand, Supply, and Finance to develop one aligned business plan, evaluate alternative scenarios, and prepare recommendations for executive decision-making.

Sa topic na ito, papasok tayo sa isang importanteng stage ng S&OP process: **Pre-S&OP Meeting**.

Dito nagsisimulang pagsamahin ang mga findings ng iba't ibang departments. Hindi na lang natin tinitingnan kung ano ang demand o kung kaya ba ng production na gumawa. Tinitingnan na natin kung ano ang pinakamagandang business decision considering operational capabilities, financial targets, costs, and customer requirements.

---

# 1. Topic Overview

## 1.1 What is a Pre-S&OP Meeting?

Sa simpleng explanation:

Ang Pre-S&OP Meeting ay isang cross-functional meeting kung saan pinag-uusapan ng Sales, Operations, Supply Chain, Finance, at iba pang departments kung paano pagsasamahin ang demand plan at supply plan para makabuo ng isang realistic at financially acceptable business plan.

Imagine natin:

- Sales says: "We need to sell 12,000 units next month."
- Operations says: "Our current capacity can only produce 10,000 units."
- Procurement says: "We have insufficient raw materials for the required production."
- Finance says: "We need to achieve our monthly revenue and profit targets."

May conflict.

Hindi puwedeng basta sundin ang Sales dahil hindi kaya ng production. Hindi rin puwedeng basta limitahan ang production dahil baka mawalan tayo ng revenue at customers.

**Ang Pre-S&OP ang venue kung saan susuriin ang mga options at bubuo ng recommendation kung paano haharapin ang conflict.**

## 1.2 Why is it important?

May apat na major reasons:

1. **Alignment:** Pinag-iisa ang understanding ng different departments tungkol sa business situation.
2. **Reconciliation:** Inaayos ang differences between demand, supply, and financial plans.
3. **Scenario Evaluation:** Sinusuri ang possible alternatives bago gumawa ng major decisions.
4. **Decision Preparation:** Inihahanda ang recommendations na kailangan ng approval ng senior management.

Importante ang Pre-S&OP dahil hindi dapat sa Executive S&OP pa lang malalaman ng management na may major capacity shortage, material shortage, o financial gap.

By that time, dapat analyzed na ang problem at may recommended solutions na.

## 1.3 Position within the monthly S&OP cycle

Ito ang simplified monthly sequence:

1. **Data Gathering and Preparation**

   Kinokolekta ang historical sales, inventory, orders, production, and financial data.

2. **Demand Review – Topic 8.3**

   Ano ang expected customer demand? Ano ang consensus demand plan?

3. **Supply Review – Topic 8.4**

   Kaya ba nating i-supply ang demand? Ano ang capacity, material, and inventory constraints?

4. **Pre-S&OP – Topic 8.5**

   Pagsamahin ang demand, supply, and financial plans. Evaluate scenarios and prepare recommendations.

5. **Executive S&OP – Topic 8.6**

   Senior management reviews recommendations and makes decisions on major business issues.

Tandaan: This is a typical sequence. Actual company calendars and meeting arrangements may vary.

## 1.4 Difference between the four S&OP meetings

Ito ang isang distinction na kailangan mong maintindihan bilang future S&OP Analyst.

| Meeting | Main question | Primary focus |
|---|---|---|
| Demand Review | How much will customers buy? | Demand |
| Supply Review | Can we fulfill the demand? | Supply |
| Pre-S&OP | What should the business do? | Reconciliation and scenarios |
| Executive S&OP | What decisions should leadership approve? | Strategic decisions |

Let's make the distinction more concrete.

**Demand Review:** Sales forecasts 12,000 units.

**Supply Review:** Operations identifies that only 10,000 units can be produced.

**Pre-S&OP:** The team evaluates overtime, additional shifts, subcontracting, inventory utilization, and demand prioritization.

**Executive S&OP:** Leadership approves the selected option, considering its financial impact, risks, and strategic priorities.

### Why not skip Pre-S&OP?

Imagine presenting directly to the President or General Manager:

"We have a shortage of 2,000 units. What should we do?"

The management team would need to ask:

- Why is there a shortage?
- Have we checked additional capacity?
- Can Procurement expedite materials?
- How much will overtime cost?
- What happens if we subcontract?
- Which option has the highest profit?
- What are the risks?

These are analyses that should already have been completed before Executive S&OP.

**Pre-S&OP converts raw business problems into decision-ready business recommendations.**

# 2. Meeting Participants and Responsibilities

Pre-S&OP is not an Operations-only or Supply Chain-only meeting.

It requires participation from departments that influence demand, supply, cost, and business performance.

## 2.1 Cross-functional participants

| Department | Main responsibility |
|---|---|
| Sales | Customer requirements, orders, market opportunities |
| Marketing | Market trends, promotions, product launches |
| Operations | Production capacity, output, manufacturing constraints |
| Supply Chain | Inventory, distribution, supply-demand alignment |
| Procurement | Raw materials, supplier availability, lead times |
| Finance | Revenue, costs, margins, budgets, profitability |
| Engineering | Technical limitations, process improvements, equipment capability |
| Quality | Quality restrictions, inspection capacity, product compliance |
| HR | Workforce availability, staffing, overtime requirements |

Not every company requires all these departments in every meeting. Participants depend on the business model and issues being discussed.

## 2.2 Three important S&OP roles

### A. S&OP Manager

The S&OP Manager oversees the overall S&OP process.

Responsibilities:

- Ensures that departments submit required inputs.
- Maintains the S&OP calendar.
- Coordinates cross-functional alignment.
- Ensures that assumptions are consistent.
- Escalates major unresolved issues.
- Ensures that recommendations are ready for Executive S&OP.

Think of this role as the overall owner of the S&OP process.

### B. S&OP Analyst

Ito ang role na particularly important sa ating learning program.

The S&OP Analyst supports the process through data consolidation, analysis, reporting, and scenario evaluation.

Typical responsibilities:

1. Collect Demand Review and Supply Review outputs.
2. Consolidate demand and supply plans.
3. Calculate demand-supply gaps.
4. Analyze projected inventory.
5. Identify capacity and material shortages.
6. Evaluate financial implications.
7. Prepare scenario comparisons.
8. Create presentation materials.
9. Document issues, decisions, and action items.

**Important distinction:** The analyst provides the evidence and recommendations. Hindi ibig sabihin na siya ang may final authority to approve additional spending, overtime, or major changes to the business plan.

### C. Meeting Facilitator

The facilitator manages the discussion itself.

Responsibilities:

- Follows the meeting agenda.
- Keeps discussions focused.
- Ensures that relevant departments participate.
- Clarifies conflicting assumptions.
- Records agreements and disagreements.
- Ensures that action items have owners and deadlines.

In some companies, the S&OP Manager also serves as the facilitator.

## 2.3 Decision-makers versus participants

Hindi lahat ng attendees ay may authority to approve decisions.

| Role | Typical authority |
|---|---|
| S&OP Analyst | Analyze and recommend |
| Department representative | Validate departmental inputs |
| S&OP Manager | Coordinate and recommend escalation |
| Functional manager | Commit departmental resources within authority |
| Senior management | Approve major business trade-offs |

For example:

Operations may recommend an additional production shift, but the Plant Manager or authorized leadership may need to approve it.

Finance may validate the cost, but the authorized budget owner approves the spending.

This distinction is important because **agreement during a meeting is not automatically the same as formal approval.**

## 2.4 Departmental responsibilities before, during, and after the meeting

A successful Pre-S&OP Meeting requires preparation. Hindi puwedeng doon pa lang maghahanap ng data ang departments.

| Department | Before meeting | During meeting | After meeting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | Submit demand and customer priorities | Explain demand changes | Communicate approved allocation |
| Marketing | Submit promotions and market outlook | Explain demand opportunities | Update commercial plans |
| Operations | Submit capacity and production plan | Validate production feasibility | Execute approved production plan |
| Supply Chain | Submit inventory and supply projections | Discuss supply gaps | Update supply plan |
| Procurement | Validate material availability | Present supplier alternatives | Execute procurement actions |
| Finance | Prepare revenue and cost analysis | Validate financial assumptions | Update financial forecast |
| S&OP Analyst | Consolidate and analyze data | Present gaps and scenarios | Update reports and decision log |
| S&OP Manager | Prepare agenda and align stakeholders | Facilitate discussions | Monitor actions and escalations |

**Practical reminder:** Each department owns the accuracy of its functional data. The S&OP Analyst owns the consolidation and analytical presentation, but should not independently invent or change departmental commitments.

# 3. Pre-S&OP Inputs

## 3.1 What information is needed?

Think of Pre-S&OP as a business reconciliation exercise.

Para makapag-decide, kailangan nating malaman:

1. How much do customers need?
2. How much can the company supply?
3. What resources are available?
4. What will happen to inventory?
5. What is the financial impact?
6. What issues remain unresolved?

Let's examine the major inputs.

### A. Consensus Demand Plan

Ito ang output ng Demand Review (Topic 8.3).

It represents the agreed demand expectation after considering:

- Historical sales.
- Statistical forecasts.
- Customer orders.
- Sales pipeline.
- Promotions.
- Seasonality.
- New product introductions.
- Product discontinuations.

Example:

| Product | Consensus demand |
|---|---:|
| Product A | 5,000 units |
| Product B | 4,000 units |
| Product C | 3,000 units |
| **Total** | **12,000 units** |

Ang 12,000 units ang demand requirement na gagamitin natin sa reconciliation.

Importante: Demand is not automatically equal to the quantity that the company can produce.

### B. Proposed Supply Plan

Ito naman ang output ng Supply Review (Topic 8.4).

It identifies how much the company can realistically supply using available resources.

Kasama rito ang:

- Current finished goods inventory.
- Planned production.
- Available raw materials.
- Production capacity.
- Supplier availability.
- Manufacturing lead time.
- Planned maintenance.
- Workforce availability.

Example:

| Supply component | Available quantity |
|---|---:|
| Beginning finished goods inventory | 1,500 |
| Planned production | 8,500 |
| **Total available supply** | **10,000** |

Notice na 10,000 lang ang available supply kahit 12,000 ang demand.

May 2,000-unit gap.

### C. Inventory Projections

Hindi sapat na malaman lang kung ilang units ang available ngayon.

Kailangan nating malaman kung ano ang magiging inventory after considering demand and planned production.

For example:

- Beginning inventory: 1,500 units.
- Planned production: 8,500 units.
- Expected shipments: 10,000 units.

Ending inventory:

1,500 + 8,500 - 10,000 = 0 units.

This means the company will consume all available inventory.

Possible consequence: No remaining buffer against unexpected orders, production delays, or quality losses.

### D. Capacity and Material Availability

Two different constraints ito.

**Capacity constraint:** Hindi sapat ang available production capability.

**Material constraint:** Hindi sapat ang raw materials to support the required production.

Example:

| Resource | Available | Required for full demand |
|---|---:|---:|
| Production capacity | 10,000 | 10,500 |
| Critical raw material-supported production | 8,500 | 10,500 |

The production requirement of 10,500 units assumes that the company will use its 1,500 units of beginning finished goods inventory.

Both constraints need to be evaluated separately.

### E. Financial Forecast and Budget Comparison

Ito ang contribution ng Module 7: Financial Planning and Reconciliation.

Hindi sapat na feasible ang operational plan. Kailangan ding malaman kung financially acceptable ito.

Important inputs include:

- Revenue forecast.
- Cost of goods sold (COGS).
- Gross profit.
- Gross margin.
- Operating expenses.
- Budget variance.
- Profitability by product.
- Financial impact of proposed actions.

Example:

| Financial metric | Budget | Current forecast |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | ₱5,700,000 | ₱5,000,000 |
| Revenue gap | | -₱700,000 |

The operational supply constraint is now connected to a financial problem.

The 2,000-unit supply gap may translate into lost or delayed revenue.

### F. Scenario Analysis and Unresolved Issues

Scenario analysis evaluates alternative ways to address the same business problem.

For example:

- Scenario 1: Maintain existing production capacity.
- Scenario 2: Introduce overtime.
- Scenario 3: Expedite raw materials.
- Scenario 4: Use subcontract manufacturing.
- Scenario 5: Prioritize selected customers or products.

The purpose is not simply to generate many options.

The purpose is to identify which alternative provides the best balance of customer service, operational feasibility, cost, and profitability.

### G. Risks, Opportunities, and Previous Action Items

The team must also review previous commitments.

Examples:

| Item | Description | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Risk | Supplier material delay | Open |
| Risk | Production line near maximum capacity | Open |
| Opportunity | Additional customer orders | Under evaluation |
| Action item | Procurement to negotiate expedited delivery | In progress |
| Action item | Operations to validate overtime capacity | Completed |

A Pre-S&OP Meeting should not repeatedly discuss the same problem without tracking what happened to previous commitments.

# 4. Pre-S&OP Meeting Activities

Now that we understand the inputs, let's discuss what actually happens inside the meeting.

## 4.1 Typical meeting flow

1. **Review previous action items**

   Ano ang natapos? Ano ang delayed? Sino ang responsible?

2. **Present the consolidated business outlook**

   Review consensus demand, proposed supply, inventory, and financial forecasts.

3. **Identify demand-supply gaps**

   Determine where demand exceeds available supply or where excess supply exists.

4. **Review operational constraints**

   Analyze capacity, manpower, raw materials, suppliers, and production limitations.

5. **Review financial implications**

   Evaluate revenue, cost, profitability, and budget variances.

6. **Evaluate alternative scenarios**

   Compare the financial and operational consequences of different actions.

7. **Resolve cross-functional disagreements**

   Agree on assumptions, responsibilities, priorities, and feasible actions.

8. **Develop recommendations**

   Identify which issues can be resolved within existing authority and which require executive approval.

## 4.2 Reconciling demand and supply

Ito ang pinaka-basic na analytical activity.

The team compares the consensus demand plan against the proposed supply plan.

| Product | Demand | Supply | Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 5,000 | 4,000 | 1,000 |
| B | 4,000 | 3,500 | 500 |
| C | 3,000 | 2,500 | 500 |
| **Total** | **12,000** | **10,000** | **2,000** |

The team now knows:

- Product A has the largest shortage.
- Products B and C also have shortages.
- Total shortage is 2,000 units.

But remember: Hindi automatic na Product A agad ang priority dahil pinakamalaki ang shortage.

We still need to evaluate customer importance, revenue, profitability, contractual commitments, and available alternatives.

## 4.3 Reviewing operational and financial gaps

May dalawang perspectives na kailangang pagsamahin.

**Operational perspective:**

- Can production meet the demand?
- Are raw materials sufficient?
- Can the supplier deliver on time?
- Is overtime possible?
- Will additional production affect quality?

**Financial perspective:**

- How much revenue is at risk?
- How much will additional production cost?
- Will overtime reduce profitability?
- Is subcontracting economically reasonable?
- Will the proposed action exceed the budget?

Example:

Operations proposes overtime to increase production.

Finance asks:

"How much additional cost will this create, and will the additional revenue justify it?"

This is where operational analysis becomes business analysis.

## 4.4 Evaluating trade-offs

A trade-off means choosing between alternatives where improving one business outcome may affect another.

Examples:

| Decision | Potential benefit | Potential disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| Overtime | Higher production output | Higher labor cost |
| Expedited materials | Faster material availability | Higher procurement cost |
| Subcontracting | Additional supply capacity | Higher unit cost and quality risk |
| Inventory utilization | Immediate customer fulfillment | Lower inventory buffer |
| Customer prioritization | Protects strategic accounts | Other customers may experience delays |

The objective is not necessarily to maximize production.

It is to maximize overall business performance within acceptable operational and financial constraints.

## 4.5 Resolving cross-functional disagreements

Let's say Sales wants Product A prioritized because of a major customer commitment.

Operations wants Product B prioritized because it is easier to manufacture.

Finance wants Product C prioritized because it has a higher contribution margin.

Who is correct?

Potentially, all three have valid reasons.

The Pre-S&OP team should evaluate:

1. Customer commitments and service requirements.
2. Production feasibility.
3. Product profitability.
4. Material availability.
5. Financial impact.
6. Strategic business priorities.

The decision should be supported by agreed criteria rather than departmental preference.

## 4.6 Prioritizing business issues

Not every issue needs Executive S&OP attention.

A useful classification is:

| Classification | Meaning | Typical handling |
|---|---|---|
| Operational issue | Can be addressed within existing authority | Departmental action |
| Cross-functional issue | Requires coordination between functions | Resolve in Pre-S&OP |
| Strategic issue | Requires senior management direction | Escalate to Executive S&OP |
| Financial approval | Exceeds delegated spending authority | Escalate to authorized approver |

For example:

- Correcting a production schedule: usually operational.
- Coordinating Procurement and Operations on material allocation: cross-functional.
- Approving significant capital expenditure: potentially executive-level.

This classification helps keep Executive S&OP focused on decisions that genuinely require leadership.

# 5. Analysis and Calculations

Ito ang technical portion ng Pre-S&OP.

As an S&OP Analyst, kailangan mong marunong mag-compute, mag-interpret, at mag-connect ng operational data sa financial results.

## 5.1 Establishing the baseline assumptions

**Illustrative manufacturing data**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---:|
| Total demand | 12,000 units |
| Beginning FG inventory | 1,500 units |
| Production capacity | 10,000 units |
| Raw material-supported production | 8,500 units |
| Selling price | ₱500/unit |
| Standard production cost | ₱300/unit |
| Revenue budget | ₱5,700,000 |
| Critical material standard lead time | 8 weeks |

Our three products have the following demand:

| Product | Demand |
|---|---:|
| A | 5,000 |
| B | 4,000 |
| C | 3,000 |
| **Total** | **12,000** |

For simplicity, assume all products have the same selling price and standard production cost. We will introduce product-specific financial differences in more advanced exercises.

## 5.2 Calculation 1: Demand-Supply Gap

**Purpose:** Determine how much customer demand cannot be fulfilled using the available supply.

Formula:

\[
\text{Demand-Supply Gap} = D-S
\]

Where:

- \(D\) = Total demand (units)
- \(S\) = Available supply (units)

Calculation:

\[
=12,000-10,000
\]

\[
=\boxed{2,000\text{ units}}
\]

**Result: 2,000 units of uncovered demand.**

The company has sufficient supply for only 83.33% of the expected demand. However, this calculation alone does not tell us why the shortage exists. We must investigate capacity, materials, inventory, and other constraints.

## 5.3 Calculation 2: Projected Inventory

**Purpose:** Determine how much inventory will remain after fulfilling expected demand.

Formula:

\[
EI=BI+P+R-D
\]

Where:

- \(EI\) = Ending inventory
- \(BI\) = Beginning inventory
- \(P\) = Production
- \(R\) = Other receipts
- \(D\) = Demand or shipments

Assume:

- Beginning inventory = 1,500 units.
- Production = 8,500 units.
- Other receipts = 0.
- Shipments = 10,000 units.

Calculation:

\[
EI=1,500+8,500+0-10,000
\]

\[
\boxed{EI=0}
\]

Interpretation:

The company will consume all available finished goods inventory.

This is a potential inventory risk because the company has no remaining stock buffer.

**Important:** In actual S&OP systems, inventory projections should consider time buckets, scheduled receipts, safety stock, allocation, and backlog. The simple formula is a starting point.

## 5.4 Calculation 3: Capacity Shortage

**Purpose:** Determine whether available production capability can support the required production quantity.

First, calculate the production requirement:

\[
\text{Required Production}=D-BI
\]

\[
=12,000-1,500
\]

\[
=10,500\text{ units}
\]

Available production capacity:

\[
=10,000\text{ units}
\]

Capacity shortage:

\[
\text{Capacity Shortage}
=\text{Required Production}-\text{Capacity}
\]

\[
=10,500-10,000
\]

\[
\boxed{500\text{ units}}
\]

Interpretation:

Even if the company has sufficient materials, its existing production capacity is 500 units short of the required production quantity.

### Capacity utilization

Another useful calculation:

\[
\text{Capacity Utilization}
=\frac{\text{Required Production}}{\text{Available Capacity}}\times100
\]

\[
=\frac{10,500}{10,000}\times100
\]

\[
=\boxed{105\%}
\]

The company needs 105% of its existing capacity to fulfill the entire demand.

This indicates that additional capacity, overtime, or another supply source is required.

## 5.5 Calculation 4: Material Shortage

Available raw materials can support 8,500 units of production.

Required production is 10,500 units.

\[
\text{Material Shortage}
=10,500-8,500
\]

\[
\boxed{2,000\text{ units}}
\]

This reveals something important:

The company has two separate constraints:

| Constraint | Shortage |
|---|---:|
| Raw material-supported production | 2,000 units |
| Existing production capacity | 500 units |

Even if Procurement solves the 2,000-unit material shortage, Operations still needs 500 additional units of production capacity.

**This is why solving only one constraint may not solve the overall demand-supply gap.**

## 5.6 Calculation 5: Revenue and Cost Impact

Now let's translate the operational shortage into financial impact.

### A. Expected revenue

\[
Revenue=D\times SP
\]

Where:

- \(D\) = Demand quantity
- \(SP\) = Selling price per unit

\[
=12,000\times ₱500
\]

\[
=\boxed{₱6,000,000}
\]

### B. Revenue from available supply

\[
=10,000\times ₱500
\]

\[
=\boxed{₱5,000,000}
\]

### C. Revenue gap

\[
=₱6,000,000-₱5,000,000
\]

\[
=\boxed{₱1,000,000}
\]

The potential revenue associated with the uncovered 2,000 units is ₱1 million.

Strictly speaking, this is not necessarily lost revenue. It may be delayed revenue if the backlog is fulfilled in a future period. Actual lost revenue depends on whether customers cancel, switch suppliers, or defer their orders.

### D. Production cost

Assuming 8,500 units of internal production:

\[
Production\ Cost=8,500\times ₱300
\]

\[
=\boxed{₱2,550,000}
\]

### E. Contribution before other expenses

For this simplified example:

\[
Contribution=Revenue-Variable\ Production\ Cost
\]

\[
=₱5,000,000-₱2,550,000
\]

\[
=\boxed{₱2,450,000}
\]

We are treating the ₱300 standard cost as the relevant variable production cost for this illustration. Actual company accounting may include fixed manufacturing overhead and other cost components.

## 5.7 Calculation 6: Financial Variance Against Budget

**Purpose:** Determine whether the projected financial performance meets the business target.

Formula:

\[
Variance=Forecast-Budget
\]

Revenue budget = ₱5,700,000.

Forecast revenue = ₱5,000,000.

\[
Variance=₱5,000,000-₱5,700,000
\]

\[
=\boxed{-₱700,000}
\]

Percentage variance:

\[
Variance\%=\frac{Forecast-Budget}{Budget}\times100
\]

\[
=\frac{-700,000}{5,700,000}\times100
\]

\[
=\boxed{-12.28\%}
\]

Interpretation:

The current supply-constrained forecast is 12.28% below the revenue budget.

This is an issue that Pre-S&OP must address.

## 5.8 Calculation 7: Scenario Comparison

Ito ang isa sa pinakamahalagang activities sa Pre-S&OP.

Hindi lang natin gustong malaman ang current situation. Gusto nating malaman kung ano ang mangyayari kung pipili tayo ng different business alternatives.

Let's compare three scenarios.

### Scenario 1: Maintain Existing Capacity

- Beginning FG inventory: 1,500 units.
- Production: 8,500 units.
- Total supply: 10,000 units.
- Uncovered demand: 2,000 units.

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Revenue | ₱5,000,000 |
| Production cost | ₱2,550,000 |
| Additional action cost | ₱0 |
| Contribution | ₱2,450,000 |

This is our baseline.

### Scenario 2: Expedite Materials + Overtime

Proposed actions:

- Obtain additional materials for 2,000 units.
- Increase production from 8,500 to 10,500 units.
- Add 500 units of overtime capacity.
- Use the existing 1,500 units of FG inventory.

Illustrative additional costs:

| Additional cost | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Material expedite premium | 2,000 × ₱40 | ₱80,000 |
| Overtime premium | 500 × ₱80 | ₱40,000 |
| **Total additional cost** | | **₱120,000** |

The standard production cost becomes:

\[
10,500\times ₱300=₱3,150,000
\]

Total relevant cost:

\[
₱3,150,000+₱120,000=₱3,270,000
\]

Revenue:

\[
12,000\times ₱500=₱6,000,000
\]

Contribution:

\[
₱6,000,000-₱3,270,000
=\boxed{₱2,730,000}
\]

### Scenario 3: Subcontract 2,000 Units

Assume:

- Internal production remains at 8,500 units.
- Existing FG inventory contributes 1,500 units.
- A qualified subcontractor supplies 2,000 units.
- Subcontracting cost is ₱390 per unit.

Subcontracting cost:

\[
2,000\times ₱390=₱780,000
\]

Internal production cost:

\[
8,500\times ₱300=₱2,550,000
\]

Total relevant cost:

\[
₱2,550,000+₱780,000=₱3,330,000
\]

Contribution:

\[
₱6,000,000-₱3,330,000
=\boxed{₱2,670,000}
\]

### Consolidated scenario comparison

All figures are illustrative and assume that all 12,000 units can be delivered and recognized as revenue in the period under Scenarios 2 and 3.

| Financial metric | Scenario 1 | Scenario 2 | Scenario 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Units supplied | 10,000 | 12,000 | 12,000 |
| Revenue | ₱5.00M | ₱6.00M | ₱6.00M |
| Production / supply cost | ₱2.55M | ₱3.15M | ₱3.33M |
| Additional action cost | ₱0 | ₱0.12M | ₱0 |
| Contribution | ₱2.45M | ₱2.73M | ₱2.67M |
| Revenue vs budget | -₱0.70M | +₱0.30M | +₱0.30M |
| Contribution improvement vs baseline | Baseline | +₱280K | +₱220K |

**Contribution comparison**

- Scenario 1: ₱2.45 million
- Scenario 2: ₱2.73 million
- Scenario 3: ₱2.67 million

Amounts are in thousands of pesos when shown in a chart.

### Which scenario is better?

Purely from the simplified financial comparison:

- Scenario 1 has the lowest contribution.
- Scenario 2 produces the highest contribution.
- Scenario 3 produces slightly lower contribution than Scenario 2.

But we cannot immediately conclude that Scenario 2 should be approved.

Remember the critical material has an 8-week standard lead time.

The S&OP team must validate:

- Can Procurement actually expedite the material?
- Will it arrive before the required production date?
- Can Operations accommodate the additional 500 units?
- Will overtime affect quality or equipment reliability?
- Is subcontracting approved and qualified?
- Can the subcontractor meet the delivery schedule?

A financially attractive scenario that cannot be executed is not a valid supply plan.

**Professional S&OP principle: A scenario must be operationally feasible and financially evaluated before it becomes a recommendation.**

## 5.9 How operational and financial analysis work together

This is the most important connection to Module 7.

The analysis follows this sequence:

1. **Customer Demand:** Establish the demand requirement.
2. **Supply Constraints:** Identify material, capacity, and inventory limitations.
3. **Scenario Alternatives:** Evaluate overtime, expediting, subcontracting, and other options.
4. **Operational evaluation:** Assess production output, capacity, delivery, inventory, and feasibility.
5. **Financial evaluation:** Assess revenue, production cost, additional expenses, contribution, and budget.
6. **Pre-S&OP Recommendation:** Present the recommended action, alternatives, risks, and required approvals.

The key idea:

Operations tells us what is possible.

Finance tells us what the possibilities mean financially.

Pre-S&OP combines both to determine what makes the most business sense.

# 6. Practical Manufacturing Application

Now let's simulate how an actual Pre-S&OP Meeting might happen.

## 6.1 Business situation: ABC Electronics Manufacturing

Assume the company manufactures three electronic components.

During the current monthly S&OP cycle, Sales identifies an increase in customer demand.

### Demand Review output (Topic 8.3)

| Product | Previous demand | New demand | Increase |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 4,000 | 5,000 | +1,000 |
| B | 3,500 | 4,000 | +500 |
| C | 2,500 | 3,000 | +500 |
| **Total** | **10,000** | **12,000** | **+2,000** |

The Demand Review establishes 12,000 units as the consensus demand plan.

### Supply Review output (Topic 8.4)

Operations and Supply Chain evaluate the requirements.

| Supply factor | Result |
|---|---|
| Beginning FG inventory | 1,500 units |
| Production capacity | 10,000 units |
| Material-supported production | 8,500 units |
| Planned production | 8,500 units |
| Total available supply | 10,000 units |
| Uncovered demand | 2,000 units |

The Supply Review confirms that existing resources cannot cover the complete demand plan.

### Financial Integration output (Module 7)

Finance reports:

- Revenue budget: ₱5.7 million.
- Current constrained revenue forecast: ₱5.0 million.
- Revenue gap: ₱700,000 below budget.
- Standard production cost: ₱300 per unit.
- Selling price: ₱500 per unit.

Now the Pre-S&OP team has the information needed to evaluate alternatives.

## 6.2 Identifying unresolved issues

The S&OP Analyst prepares an issue register.

| Issue | Department involved | Business impact |
|---|---|---|
| 2,000-unit material shortage | Procurement | Supply shortfall |
| 500-unit capacity shortage | Operations | Production limitation |
| 8-week material lead time | Procurement | Delivery risk |
| Zero projected FG inventory | Supply Chain | Inventory exposure |
| ₱700K revenue gap vs budget | Finance / Sales | Financial underperformance |

Notice how each issue is connected.

The analyst should avoid treating them as isolated problems.

For example, solving the material shortage alone does not resolve the capacity shortage.

## 6.3 Developing alternative scenarios

The team evaluates:

| Scenario | Proposed action | Main concern |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Maintain current resources | 2,000 units uncovered |
| 2 | Expedite material and use overtime | Supplier timing and overtime feasibility |
| 3 | Subcontract 2,000 units | Cost and quality qualification |

The analyst then prepares the financial comparison from Section 5.

## 6.4 Cross-functional meeting discussion

Let's simulate the discussion.

**Sales Manager:**

"We need to fulfill all 12,000 units. We have confirmed customer requirements, and failure to deliver could affect our customer relationship."

**Operations Manager:**

"Our current production capability cannot support the full requirement. We need an additional 500 units of capacity, even if the materials become available."

**Procurement Manager:**

"Our critical material has an 8-week lead time. We need to confirm whether the supplier can expedite the required quantity."

**Finance Manager:**

"Scenario 2 has the highest projected contribution, but we need to confirm that the expedite and overtime costs are complete and that the material will arrive on time."

**S&OP Analyst:**

"Based on the current assumptions, Scenario 2 provides ₱280,000 more contribution than the baseline, while Scenario 3 provides ₱220,000 more. However, Scenario 2 remains dependent on supplier confirmation and additional capacity approval."

This is how an analyst should participate.

Hindi lang siya nagbabasa ng numbers. He or she explains what the numbers mean, what assumptions support them, and what conditions must be satisfied.

## 6.5 Formulating the recommendation

Based on the illustrative data:

**Primary recommendation:** Pursue Scenario 2, subject to confirmed supplier delivery and overtime capacity.

**Alternative:** Use Scenario 3 if the required materials cannot be expedited within the necessary production window, subject to subcontractor qualification and approval.

**Fallback:** If neither alternative is feasible, maintain the constrained supply plan and establish customer prioritization and backlog recovery arrangements.

This is a more useful recommendation than simply saying:

"We have a shortage of 2,000 units."

It tells management what to do, why, how much it may cost, and what happens if the preferred option fails.

## 6.6 Matters requiring executive approval

Some decisions may exceed the authority of the Pre-S&OP participants.

Examples:

1. Approval of additional overtime budget.
2. Approval of expedited procurement spending.
3. Approval of subcontract manufacturing.
4. Acceptance of lower contribution margins.
5. Prioritization of strategic customers.
6. Revision of revenue or financial targets, when appropriate.

The Pre-S&OP team prepares these matters for Executive S&OP rather than assuming it can approve everything itself.

# 8. Professional S&OP Practice

Now let's shift from understanding the meeting to performing the actual work of an S&OP Analyst.

This is where your analytical and manufacturing background becomes relevant.

## 8.1 How to prepare a Pre-S&OP presentation

As an analyst, you should follow a structured preparation process.

**Step 1: Collect functional inputs**

Request the latest approved outputs from:

- Demand Planning.
- Sales.
- Operations.
- Procurement.
- Supply Chain.
- Finance.

Make sure that the information covers the same planning horizon and time buckets.

For example, do not compare a monthly demand plan against a quarterly production plan without reconciling their periods.

**Step 2: Validate data**

Check:

- Are all products included?
- Are units of measurement consistent?
- Are beginning inventories correct?
- Are planned receipts included?
- Are demand assumptions updated?
- Are capacity assumptions realistic?
- Are financial figures using the same currency and period?

One incorrect input can distort the entire reconciliation.

**Step 3: Consolidate information**

Create a consolidated view.

| Product | Demand | Supply | Gap | Ending inventory |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 5,000 | 4,000 | 1,000 | 0 |
| B | 4,000 | 3,500 | 500 | 0 |
| C | 3,000 | 2,500 | 500 | 0 |

**Step 4: Identify exceptions**

An exception is a condition that requires attention because it deviates from the plan, target, or acceptable threshold.

Examples:

- Demand exceeds supply.
- Inventory falls below safety stock.
- Capacity utilization exceeds available capacity.
- Material coverage is insufficient.
- Revenue forecast falls below budget.

**Step 5: Perform scenario analysis**

Evaluate possible corrective actions and quantify their operational and financial consequences.

**Step 6: Prepare recommendations**

Every major issue should have:

- Problem statement.
- Supporting data.
- Root cause or constraint.
- Business impact.
- Proposed action.
- Financial implication.
- Responsible owner.
- Required decision.

## 8.2 How to communicate business gaps

A common mistake among analysts is presenting too many numbers without explaining their business meaning.

Compare these two presentations.

### Weak presentation

"We have 12,000 units of demand and only 10,000 units of supply."

This presents information but does not explain the consequences or proposed actions.

### Professional presentation

"The current supply plan covers 83.33% of the 12,000-unit consensus demand, leaving a 2,000-unit shortage. The primary constraints are a 2,000-unit material shortage and 500 units of additional capacity required. The resulting constrained revenue forecast is ₱700,000 below budget. We recommend evaluating expedited procurement and overtime, with qualified subcontracting as an alternative."

This communicates the problem, root constraints, financial impact, and recommendation.

A good S&OP analyst answers four questions:

1. What happened?
2. Why did it happen?
3. What is the business impact?
4. What should we do?

## 8.3 How to challenge assumptions using data

As an analyst, hindi sapat na tanggapin mo lahat ng information na ibinibigay ng departments.

You need to validate assumptions professionally.

Examples:

| Department statement | Analyst's validation question |
|---|---|
| Sales: Demand will increase by 20%. | What customer orders or market information support the increase? |
| Operations: Capacity is 10,000 units. | Is this demonstrated output or theoretical rated capacity? |
| Procurement: Materials will arrive next month. | Is there a confirmed supplier delivery date? |
| Finance: Overtime is financially acceptable. | Have all incremental costs been included? |
| Supply Chain: Inventory is sufficient. | Does it include allocated, blocked, and quality-hold inventory? |

Notice the approach.

Hindi mo sinasabing mali ang department. You're asking for evidence to establish whether the assumption is reliable.

## 8.4 How to document meeting decisions

Every important discussion should be documented.

A practical action tracker:

| Issue / Action | Owner | Due date | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confirm material expedite | Procurement | Nov 20 | Open |
| Validate overtime capacity | Operations | Nov 19 | Open |
| Confirm subcontractor qualification | Quality | Nov 21 | Open |
| Validate scenario financials | Finance | Nov 22 | In progress |

You should also distinguish:

- **Decision:** An approved direction.
- **Action item:** A task assigned to someone.
- **Assumption:** A condition used in the analysis.
- **Risk:** Something that may negatively affect the plan.
- **Escalation:** An issue requiring higher-level authority.

These terms are related but are not interchangeable.

## 8.5 Recommended Pre-S&OP presentation structure

For professional S&OP work, I recommend organizing your presentation in this order:

| Slide | Content | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Executive Summary | Present overall business condition |
| 2 | Demand Outlook | Explain expected customer requirements |
| 3 | Supply Outlook | Explain available supply |
| 4 | Demand-Supply Gap | Identify uncovered demand |
| 5 | Inventory and Capacity | Show operational constraints |
| 6 | Financial Performance | Compare forecast versus budget |
| 7 | Scenario Analysis | Compare alternative actions |
| 8 | Risks and Opportunities | Identify major concerns |
| 9 | Recommendations | Present preferred actions |
| 10 | Decisions Required | Identify matters for Executive S&OP |

**Professional tip:** Put the most important business issue in the first slide. Do not force executives to read ten slides before discovering that the company has a major revenue or supply problem.

What decisions should leadership approve?

# 11. Connection to Topic 8.6: Executive S&OP Meeting

Ito ang transition natin.

Sa Topic 8.5, natutunan nating mag-analyze ng business problems, mag-evaluate ng alternatives, at mag-prepare ng recommendations.

Sa succeeding topic, pag-aaralan naman natin kung paano ginagamit ng senior management ang mga recommendations na ito para gumawa ng business decisions.

| Pre-S&OP (8.5) | Executive S&OP (8.6) |
|---|---|
| Analyzes alternatives | Reviews recommendations |
| Evaluates financial impact | Evaluates business implications |
| Resolves cross-functional issues | Resolves strategic conflicts |
| Prepares recommendations | Approves or rejects proposals |
| Identifies decisions required | Makes authorized decisions |

**The fundamental difference:**

Pre-S&OP prepares the business decision. Executive S&OP makes the decision.

## Final lesson takeaway

A good S&OP Analyst does not simply report that demand is higher than supply.

A professional S&OP Analyst explains why the gap exists, quantifies its operational and financial consequences, evaluates possible solutions, and presents a recommendation that management can act on.
