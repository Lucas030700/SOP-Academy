# Module 8 – The S&OP Process, Governance & Management
## Topic 8.7: Roles and Responsibilities

**Learning Objective:** Understand who participates in S&OP, what each department is responsible for, who has the authority to make decisions, and how all departments work together to create and execute one integrated business plan.

---

# 1. TOPIC OVERVIEW

### 1.1 What are Roles and Responsibilities in S&OP?

Imagine a manufacturing company receiving more customer orders than originally forecasted.

Sales wants to accept all orders because it wants to increase revenue.

Production wants additional time because its existing capacity is already occupied.

Procurement reports that some raw materials will arrive late.

Warehouse reports that existing inventory is insufficient.

Finance warns that producing additional units will increase operating expenses.

**Who should decide what the company should do?**

Should Sales decide? Production? Finance? Or the General Manager?

This is where S&OP Roles and Responsibilities become important.

S&OP requires multiple departments to contribute information, evaluate alternatives, recommend solutions, and make decisions according to their respective authority.

The objective is not to allow every department to make independent decisions. It is to establish clear ownership and accountability while ensuring that everyone works toward the same business objectives.

### 1.2 Why is this topic important?

Without clearly defined responsibilities:

- Sales might commit to customer orders without checking production capacity.
- Production might increase output without considering actual demand.
- Procurement might purchase excessive raw materials.
- Finance might discover budget problems only after commitments have been made.
- Management might receive conflicting reports from different departments.
- Important decisions might remain unresolved because nobody knows who has the authority to approve them.

Therefore, an effective S&OP organization must establish three things:

1. **Ownership:** Who is responsible for each activity?
2. **Authority:** Who can make or approve decisions?
3. **Accountability:** Who is answerable for the results?

# 2. LAYMAN'S EXPLANATION

### 2.1 Think of S&OP as managing a manufacturing team

Imagine you are managing a factory that produces electronic components.

Every department has a different responsibility.

Each department has its own expertise.

However, the company cannot operate successfully if each department only focuses on its individual objectives.

For example:

- Sales wants to maximize revenue.
- Production wants stable operations and efficient utilization.
- Procurement wants reliable material availability at reasonable cost.
- Finance wants the business to remain profitable.
- Inventory wants sufficient stock without excessive inventory investment.

These objectives can sometimes conflict.

S&OP provides a common process where these departments discuss their requirements and agree on a coordinated business plan.

**The central principle: Every department owns its functional responsibilities, but the organization shares responsibility for achieving the overall business plan.**

# 3. TECHNICAL EXPLANATION

### 3.1 S&OP Organizational Structure

In professional organizations, S&OP typically operates through a cross-functional governance structure.

There are three important organizational layers.

| Organizational layer | Main responsibility |
|---|---|
| Executive Governance | Strategic direction, major decisions, and approval |
| S&OP Process Management | Coordination, integration, facilitation, and governance |
| Functional Departments | Data provision, analysis, recommendations, and execution |

Let's examine each.

**A. Executive Governance**

Usually includes:

- General Manager
- Business Unit Director
- Operations Director
- Commercial Director
- Finance Director
- Supply Chain Director

Their responsibility is to resolve decisions that exceed the authority of individual departments.

Examples:

- Approving additional production capacity.
- Authorizing significant capital expenditures.
- Prioritizing customers during severe shortages.
- Approving major inventory investments.
- Accepting financial trade-offs.

**B. S&OP Process Management**

Usually led by an S&OP Manager, S&OP Leader, or S&OP Process Owner.

Responsibilities include:

- Managing the monthly S&OP calendar.
- Coordinating departmental participation.
- Ensuring data availability.
- Facilitating S&OP meetings.
- Identifying unresolved issues.
- Consolidating recommendations.
- Monitoring decisions and action items.

The S&OP Process Owner manages the process, but does not automatically have authority to approve every business decision.

**C. Functional Departments**

These are the departments responsible for providing the information and executing the decisions.

Examples:

- Sales
- Demand Planning
- Supply Planning
- Production
- Procurement
- Inventory Management
- Finance
- Marketing
- Product Management

Their participation ensures that the business plan reflects actual operational and commercial conditions.

### 3.2 Why does S&OP require cross-functional participation?

Because business decisions affect multiple departments simultaneously.

Consider this relationship:

**Example: Increasing monthly production**

- **Sales:** Customer demand increases by 20%.
- **Demand Planning:** Updates the demand forecast.
- **Supply Planning:** Checks whether production can accommodate the increase.
- **Procurement:** Checks raw material availability.
- **Production:** Checks machines, labor, and capacity.
- **Inventory:** Checks available stocks.
- **Finance:** Evaluates additional costs and profitability.
- **Executive Leadership:** Approves the business trade-off when decisions exceed delegated authority.

Notice that increasing production is not simply a Production Department decision.

It requires coordinated information from several departments.

# 4. DETAILED DISCUSSION: KEY ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

This is the most important section of the topic.

We will examine the responsibilities of each department and how they contribute to S&OP.

## 4.1 Sales and Commercial Teams

**Primary responsibility: Represent customer demand and commercial commitments.**

Sales is responsible for understanding what customers intend to purchase and communicating commercial opportunities and risks.

Key responsibilities:

1. Provide customer orders and sales information.
2. Identify changes in customer requirements.
3. Communicate major customer opportunities.
4. Identify potential cancellations and order changes.
5. Provide information about customer priorities.
6. Explain significant differences between actual sales and forecast.
7. Communicate customer delivery requirements.

**Example:**

A customer originally orders 10,000 units per month but suddenly increases the requirement to 15,000 units.

Sales must communicate:

- What caused the increase?
- Is the increase temporary or permanent?
- Is the customer committed to purchasing the additional quantity?
- When are the additional units required?
- What happens if the company cannot meet the requested delivery?

Sales should not independently promise additional production capacity or delivery commitments that have not been validated.

## 4.2 Demand Planning

**Primary responsibility: Develop and maintain the demand forecast.**

Demand Planning transforms commercial information and historical data into a structured estimate of future demand.

Key responsibilities:

1. Consolidate historical demand.
2. Analyze demand trends and seasonality.
3. Generate statistical forecasts.
4. Incorporate Sales and Marketing inputs.
5. Identify demand risks and opportunities.
6. Measure forecast accuracy and bias.
7. Maintain the agreed demand plan.

Important KPIs:

- Forecast Accuracy
- Forecast Bias
- MAPE or WAPE
- Demand Variability

**Example:**

Historical sales indicate that average monthly demand is 10,000 units.

Sales provides information about a potential new customer order of 3,000 additional units.

Demand Planning evaluates whether this is a realistic demand increase and incorporates the validated assumption into the forecast.

Important distinction:

Sales provides commercial intelligence. Demand Planning owns the forecasting process.

## 4.3 Supply Planning and Production

These functions are closely connected but not necessarily the same role.

**Supply Planning: Determine how demand can be fulfilled.**

Responsibilities:

- Evaluate production capacity.
- Determine production requirements.
- Identify capacity constraints.
- Evaluate available inventory.
- Develop feasible supply scenarios.
- Identify alternative production arrangements.
- Recommend supply allocation.

**Production: Execute the approved manufacturing plan.**

Responsibilities:

- Schedule manufacturing activities.
- Allocate machines and labor.
- Monitor production performance.
- Manage production efficiency.
- Report production delays.
- Execute production according to the agreed schedule.

Important KPIs:

- Capacity Utilization
- Production Attainment
- Schedule Adherence
- Manufacturing Throughput
- OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness)

**Example:**

Demand increases from 10,000 to 15,000 units.

Supply Planning determines that existing production capacity can only support 12,000 units.

Production confirms that an additional shift could increase capacity to 14,000 units.

The remaining 1,000-unit gap requires another solution.

Notice that Supply Planning identifies the feasibility and alternatives, while Production executes the manufacturing schedule.

## 4.4 Procurement and Supplier Management

**Primary responsibility: Ensure material and supplier availability.**

Procurement ensures that required materials can be obtained at the right quantity, cost, quality, and timing.

Responsibilities:

1. Review material requirements.
2. Check supplier capacity and availability.
3. Monitor supplier lead times.
4. Identify material shortages.
5. Evaluate alternative suppliers.
6. Negotiate delivery schedules.
7. Communicate procurement risks.

Important KPIs:

- Supplier On-Time Delivery
- Purchase Price Variance
- Supplier Lead Time
- Material Availability
- Supplier Quality Performance

**Example:**

Production requires an additional 5,000 units of raw material.

However, the supplier can only deliver 3,000 units within the required period.

Procurement must determine whether:

- The supplier can expedite the remaining quantity.
- Another qualified supplier is available.
- Existing material can be reallocated.
- Production can use an approved alternative material.

Procurement recommends solutions, but significant additional spending may require Finance and executive approval.

## 4.5 Inventory and Warehouse Management

**Primary responsibility: Maintain inventory visibility and physical stock availability.**

Although these functions are related, their responsibilities are different.

**Inventory Management**

- Monitor raw material, WIP, and finished goods.
- Maintain inventory accuracy.
- Monitor safety stock.
- Identify excess and obsolete inventory.
- Evaluate inventory coverage.
- Report stock shortages.

**Warehouse Management**

- Manage physical storage.
- Receive and issue materials.
- Maintain stock locations.
- Execute picking and dispatching.
- Ensure proper material handling.

Important KPIs:

- Inventory Accuracy
- Days of Supply (DOS)
- Inventory Turnover
- Stockout Rate
- Excess and Obsolete Inventory

**Example:**

The inventory system reports 5,000 units available.

However, physical verification indicates that only 4,200 units are usable.

Inventory Management must investigate the discrepancy and provide the correct usable inventory position.

This matters because Supply Planning must use reliable inventory information when determining production requirements.

## 4.6 Finance

**Primary responsibility: Evaluate the financial implications of the S&OP plan.**

Finance ensures that operational plans are financially sustainable.

Responsibilities:

1. Evaluate revenue projections.
2. Analyze production costs.
3. Review inventory investment.
4. Evaluate additional labor and overtime costs.
5. Assess procurement spending.
6. Compare the S&OP plan against budget.
7. Translate operational scenarios into financial outcomes.

Important KPIs:

- Revenue
- Gross Margin
- Operating Expenses
- Working Capital
- Inventory Value
- Budget Variance

**Example:**

Production proposes adding another shift to accommodate additional demand.

Finance evaluates:

- Additional labor expenses.
- Utility expenses.
- Material costs.
- Potential revenue increase.
- Expected contribution margin.

The objective is not simply to increase production. It is to determine whether the additional production creates sufficient business value.

## 4.7 Product Management and Marketing

**Primary responsibility: Provide product strategy and market intelligence.**

Product Management focuses on product lifecycle, portfolio decisions, and product requirements.

Marketing focuses on market demand, campaigns, customer segments, and promotional activities.

Responsibilities include:

- Communicating new product launches.
- Providing product lifecycle information.
- Identifying phase-outs and discontinued products.
- Communicating promotional activities.
- Evaluating market trends.
- Providing expected demand impacts.
- Identifying product substitution opportunities.

**Example:**

Marketing plans a promotional campaign that is expected to increase demand for a specific product by 30%.

This information must be communicated to Demand Planning before the campaign begins.

Otherwise, the company might experience unexpected shortages.

## 4.8 S&OP Manager / Process Owner

**Primary responsibility: Own and coordinate the S&OP process.**

This is one of the most important roles in S&OP governance.

The S&OP Manager ensures that the process works consistently across departments.

Responsibilities:

1. Establish the S&OP calendar.
2. Coordinate departmental activities.
3. Ensure required information is available.
4. Facilitate Demand Review, Supply Review, and Pre-S&OP.
5. Consolidate cross-functional issues.
6. Monitor unresolved decisions.
7. Prepare Executive S&OP materials.
8. Track decisions and action items.
9. Monitor process compliance.
10. Drive continuous improvement of the S&OP process.

**Important distinction:**

The S&OP Manager owns the process, not necessarily the functional plans.

For example:

- Demand Planning owns the demand forecast.
- Supply Planning owns the supply plan.
- Finance owns financial evaluation.
- Executive Leadership approves major business decisions.
- S&OP Manager ensures these plans are integrated.

The S&OP Manager is similar to a coordinator and governance leader who ensures that the different departmental plans become one coherent business plan.

## 4.9 Executive Leadership

**Primary responsibility: Make strategic decisions and approve major business trade-offs.**

Executives are responsible for ensuring that the integrated plan supports overall business objectives.

Responsibilities:

- Review the consolidated business plan.
- Resolve major demand-supply imbalances.
- Approve significant resource changes.
- Resolve conflicts between departments.
- Approve strategic inventory decisions.
- Evaluate financial and operational risks.
- Assign executive-level accountability.

**Example:**

The company cannot fulfill all customer demand because of limited production capacity.

Possible solutions:

1. Approve overtime.
2. Outsource some production.
3. Prioritize strategic customers.
4. Delay lower-priority orders.
5. Invest in additional equipment.

These decisions may require executive approval because they affect the company's financial performance, customer commitments, and strategic priorities.

# 5. RESPONSIBILITIES ACROSS THE S&OP CYCLE

Now let's connect each department to the actual monthly S&OP process.

Remember, departments participate at different stages depending on the information and decisions required.

| S&OP stage | Main contributors | Main responsibilities |
|---|---|---|
| Data Gathering | All functional departments | Provide, validate, and consolidate information |
| Demand Review | Sales, Demand Planning, Marketing | Review demand, assumptions, and forecast changes |
| Supply Review | Supply Planning, Production, Procurement, Inventory | Evaluate supply feasibility and constraints |
| Pre-S&OP | S&OP Manager, Functional Leaders, Finance | Resolve conflicts and develop recommendations |
| Executive S&OP | Executive Leadership, S&OP Manager, Department Heads | Review scenarios and make strategic decisions |
| Implementation | All functional departments | Execute the approved plan and monitor results |

### 5.1 Data Gathering and Preparation

Each department provides its own data.

| Department | Required information |
|---|---|
| Sales | Customer orders, sales pipeline, customer changes |
| Demand Planning | Historical demand, forecast, forecast accuracy |
| Production | Capacity, production output, downtime |
| Procurement | Supplier availability, lead times, material shortages |
| Inventory | Raw materials, WIP, finished goods |
| Finance | Revenue, costs, budget, financial projections |
| Marketing | Campaigns, promotions, new product launches |

The S&OP Analyst or designated process team consolidates and validates these inputs.

### 5.2 Demand Review

Primary participants:

- Sales
- Demand Planning
- Marketing
- Product Management

Their objective is to establish a consensus demand plan.

Key questions:

- Is the forecast realistic?
- Are customer orders increasing?
- Are there major demand changes?
- Are promotions affecting demand?
- What are the risks and opportunities?

Demand Planning typically maintains the forecast, while commercial stakeholders provide assumptions and validate business opportunities.

### 5.3 Supply Review

Primary participants:

- Supply Planning
- Production
- Procurement
- Inventory Management
- Warehouse Management
- Finance

Their objective is to determine whether the company can support the demand plan.

Key questions:

- Is production capacity sufficient?
- Are materials available?
- Is inventory adequate?
- Are suppliers capable of supporting demand?
- Are additional costs required?
- What supply alternatives are available?

### 5.4 Pre-S&OP

The Pre-S&OP meeting integrates the demand and supply perspectives.

Participants typically include functional managers, planners, Finance, and the S&OP Process Owner.

Responsibilities:

- Review the demand and supply gap.
- Evaluate alternative scenarios.
- Compare operational and financial impacts.
- Resolve issues within delegated authority.
- Identify decisions requiring executive approval.
- Prepare recommendations.

The S&OP Manager facilitates the discussion and ensures that unresolved issues are properly documented.

### 5.5 Executive S&OP

Executive Leadership evaluates the integrated business plan.

Responsibilities:

- Review major performance indicators.
- Evaluate unresolved demand-supply issues.
- Assess financial implications.
- Approve strategic decisions.
- Assign decision owners.
- Confirm the agreed business direction.

The executives are not expected to solve every operational problem.

They focus on issues that require higher-level authority or strategic trade-offs.

### 5.6 Plan Approval, Communication, and Follow-Through

After approval:

- S&OP Manager communicates the approved plan.
- Demand Planning updates the demand plan.
- Supply Planning updates the supply plan.
- Production executes the manufacturing schedule.
- Procurement executes purchasing and supplier arrangements.
- Inventory teams monitor inventory requirements.
- Finance updates financial projections.
- S&OP Analyst tracks performance and action items.

The process does not end after Executive S&OP.

The approved plan must be translated into operational activities.

# 6. DECISION-MAKING AUTHORITY AND ACCOUNTABILITY

This section introduces an important professional concept.

**Being responsible for an activity does not automatically mean having the authority to approve its outcome.**

For example:

A Supply Planner may be responsible for developing a production capacity recovery scenario.

However, that planner may not have the authority to approve additional capital expenditure.

We need to distinguish four concepts.

## 6.1 Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed

These four concepts are commonly represented by RACI.

| Classification | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| R - Responsible | Performs the work | Supply Planner develops the supply scenario |
| A - Accountable | Owns the final outcome and ensures completion | Supply Planning Manager approves the functional supply plan |
| C - Consulted | Provides input before a decision | Production and Procurement provide feasibility information |
| I - Informed | Receives updates or results | Relevant department stakeholders receive the approved plan |

Let's simplify:

- **Responsible:** Sino ang gagawa?
- **Accountable:** Sino ang ultimately answerable?
- **Consulted:** Sino ang kailangang tanungin?
- **Informed:** Sino ang kailangang sabihan?

### 6.2 Who prepares, reviews, recommends, approves, and executes?

These are different stages of decision-making.

| Activity | Meaning | Typical owner |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare | Develop information or a proposed plan | Functional planner |
| Review | Check accuracy and feasibility | Functional manager |
| Recommend | Propose a preferred solution | Functional leaders / Pre-S&OP |
| Approve | Authorize the decision | Authorized management |
| Execute | Implement the approved decision | Operational teams |

**Important:** These are typical arrangements, not universal rules. Approval authority depends on company policy, financial thresholds, and delegated authority.

### 6.3 Operational versus Executive Decision Authority

Consider the following examples.

| Decision | Typical authority |
|---|---|
| Adjust daily production sequencing within approved limits | Production Supervisor |
| Update the demand forecast | Demand Planning |
| Recommend supplier expediting | Procurement |
| Reallocate inventory within approved allocation rules | Supply Chain Management |
| Approve overtime within an authorized budget | Authorized Operations Manager |
| Approve major capital investment | Executive Leadership |
| Change strategic customer priorities | Commercial and Executive Leadership |
| Approve major financial trade-offs | Authorized Executive / Business Leadership |

The important principle is:

**Operational teams manage execution within approved boundaries. Executives decide on major business trade-offs beyond those boundaries.**

# 7. CROSS-FUNCTIONAL COLLABORATION

### 7.1 How departments exchange information

S&OP requires continuous information exchange.

For example:

1. Sales communicates increasing customer requirements.
2. Demand Planning evaluates and updates the forecast.
3. Supply Planning evaluates production feasibility.
4. Procurement identifies material shortages.
5. Finance evaluates the financial implications.
6. S&OP Manager coordinates unresolved issues.
7. Executive Leadership approves major trade-offs.

The information should flow between departments before meetings, not only during meetings.

### 7.2 Managing conflicting departmental objectives

This is a common challenge in real organizations.

Consider the following situation:

| Department | Preferred objective |
|---|---|
| Sales | Fulfill 100% of customer demand |
| Production | Maintain stable production and avoid excessive overtime |
| Procurement | Avoid expensive emergency purchasing |
| Inventory | Avoid excessive inventory investment |
| Finance | Maintain profitability and budget compliance |

Each objective is reasonable from its own departmental perspective.

However, optimizing one department can negatively affect another.

For example:

Sales might request additional production, but producing those units requires expensive overtime and emergency material purchases.

The company must evaluate the total business impact rather than simply satisfying the preference of one department.

### 7.3 Resolving disagreements

A practical escalation process:

1. **Identify the disagreement.** Clearly define the problem, affected departments, and business impact.
2. **Validate the information.** Check whether the disagreement is caused by incorrect data, different assumptions, or genuine resource constraints.
3. **Develop alternatives.** Each department provides feasible solutions.
4. **Evaluate trade-offs.** Compare service level, revenue, cost, inventory, capacity, and risk.
5. **Escalate when necessary.** If the issue exceeds functional authority, bring it to Pre-S&OP or Executive S&OP.
6. **Assign ownership.** Every approved action must have a clear owner, deadline, and expected result.

### 7.4 Action Item Ownership

An action item should never be recorded simply as:

"Procurement to investigate material shortage."

That is incomplete.

A proper action item should identify:

| Element | Example |
|---|---|
| Action | Request expedited delivery |
| Owner | Procurement Manager |
| Due date | November 20 |
| Expected output | Confirmed revised supplier delivery date |
| Status | In progress |
| Escalation | Supply Chain Director if supplier cannot meet the required date |

The S&OP Analyst or Process Owner tracks the action, but the assigned owner remains accountable for completing it.

# 8. PRACTICAL MANUFACTURING CASE STUDY

## Case: Unexpected Increase in Customer Demand

Let's use a manufacturing example involving electronic components.

A company manufactures MLCC products.

The company originally planned to produce 100,000 units per month.

However, a major customer unexpectedly increases its demand.

### Initial versus updated business situation

| Measure | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Original monthly demand | 100,000 units |
| Updated monthly demand | 130,000 units |
| Existing capacity | 110,000 units |
| Raw material availability | 115,000 units supported |

**Current constraints:**

- Demand exceeds production capacity by 20,000 units.
- Raw materials support only 115,000 units.
- Additional overtime and emergency purchases will increase costs.
- Finance has limited budget flexibility.

The company must determine how to address the additional 30,000 units.

### 8.1 What should each department contribute?

| Department | Required contribution |
|---|---|
| Sales | Confirm customer commitment, priority, and delivery requirements |
| Demand Planning | Validate the 130,000-unit demand forecast |
| Supply Planning | Develop supply scenarios and identify the 20,000-unit capacity gap |
| Production | Evaluate overtime, additional shifts, and machine availability |
| Procurement | Investigate expedited raw materials and supplier alternatives |
| Inventory | Identify available stocks and potential reallocations |
| Warehouse | Confirm material handling and finished goods dispatch capability |
| Finance | Evaluate additional costs and profitability |
| Marketing / Product Management | Identify other demand changes and product priorities |
| S&OP Manager | Coordinate discussions and consolidate recommendations |
| Executive Leadership | Approve major trade-offs and resource allocation |

### 8.2 Who recommends solutions?

Suppose the functional teams develop three scenarios.

| Scenario | Production capacity | Material availability | Financial impact |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| A: Existing resources | 110,000 | 115,000 | No major additional cost |
| B: Overtime + expedited materials | 125,000 | 125,000 | Higher operating costs |
| C: Additional shift + supplier support | 130,000 | 130,000 | Highest additional cost |

These scenarios are illustrative.

The functional teams should evaluate whether each option is technically feasible, operationally sustainable, and financially acceptable.

### 8.3 Who makes the decision?

The decision-making flow might look like this:

**1. Functional Teams**

Prepare data, identify constraints, and develop alternatives.

**2. Pre-S&OP**

Evaluate scenarios and recommend the preferred business solution.

**3. Executive S&OP**

Review financial and operational trade-offs and approve the decision within delegated authority.

**4. Functional Execution**

Production implements the schedule, Procurement secures materials, and other teams execute their assigned actions.

### 8.4 What is the S&OP Analyst's contribution?

The S&OP Analyst supports this entire process by:

- Consolidating the demand and supply data.
- Validating the 130,000-unit forecast.
- Calculating the capacity gap.
- Comparing the three scenarios.
- Preparing financial and operational comparison tables.
- Highlighting the major constraints.
- Recording management decisions.
- Tracking assigned actions.

The analyst does not necessarily decide which scenario the company must select.

Instead, the analyst provides reliable information so management can make an informed decision.

# 9. S&OP ANALYST'S ROLE

Let's examine this separately because it is particularly important when developing professionally in an S&OP position.

The S&OP Analyst is generally a supporting analytical and coordination role within the S&OP governance structure.

However, the actual scope depends on the company's organizational design.

### 9.1 Data Consolidation and Validation

The analyst gathers information from different systems and departments.

Examples:

- Actual sales
- Demand forecasts
- Production output
- Inventory balances
- Supplier performance
- Capacity information
- Financial data

The analyst must ensure that the data is accurate, consistent, and comparable.

For example, Sales might report 130,000 units while the Supply Planning file contains 125,000 units because of different assumptions or reporting dates.

The analyst should investigate the discrepancy rather than simply choosing one number.

### 9.2 KPI Monitoring and Reporting

The analyst prepares performance reports.

| KPI | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Forecast Accuracy | How closely forecasts match actual demand |
| Forecast Bias | Whether forecasts consistently overestimate or underestimate |
| Schedule Adherence | Whether production follows the agreed schedule |
| Capacity Utilization | How much available capacity is being used |
| Inventory DOS | How many days current inventory can support demand |
| OTIF | Whether orders are delivered on time and in full |
| Revenue Attainment | Actual revenue versus target |

The analyst must understand what the KPI means and what business action might be necessary.

### 9.3 Coordinating Information

The analyst acts as an information bridge between departments.

For example:

- Follow up missing Sales assumptions.
- Request updated capacity information.
- Clarify procurement shortages.
- Coordinate revised forecasts.
- Consolidate Finance inputs.

This does not mean the analyst owns every department's data.

The functional department remains responsible for the accuracy of its source information.

### 9.4 Tracking Decisions, Risks, and Actions

The analyst maintains visibility of:

- Decisions made.
- Decisions pending.
- Identified risks.
- Agreed countermeasures.
- Action owners.
- Due dates.
- Completion status.

This prevents important decisions from disappearing after meetings.

### 9.5 Supporting Management Decisions

The analyst should be able to answer questions such as:

- How much additional demand are we expecting?
- How much capacity is available?
- What is the material shortage?
- What is the financial impact?
- What happens if we do nothing?
- Which scenario has the lowest operational risk?
- What decision is required from management?

**Professional perspective:**

An S&OP Analyst is not merely someone who prepares Excel reports.

The role involves translating business data into meaningful operational insights that support cross-functional decisions.

# 10. PRACTICAL RACI MATRIX

Now let's put the concepts together.

A RACI Matrix is a responsibility assignment tool used to clarify the involvement of different roles in a process.

It identifies who:

- Performs the activity.
- Owns the outcome.
- Provides input.
- Receives information.

### 10.1 Understanding the RACI Matrix

| Letter | Classification | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| R | Responsible | Performs the work |
| A | Accountable | Owns the outcome |
| C | Consulted | Provides input |
| I | Informed | Receives updates |

A useful RACI design principle is to assign one clearly identifiable Accountable owner for each activity.

There may be multiple Responsible contributors.

### 10.2 Manufacturing S&OP RACI Example

Let's use the following organizational roles:

- SALES: Sales Manager
- DEMAND: Demand Planning Manager
- SUPPLY: Supply Planning Manager
- PROD: Production Manager
- PROC: Procurement Manager
- INV: Inventory Manager
- FIN: Finance Manager
- S&OP: S&OP Manager
- EXEC: Executive Leadership

**Illustrative governance model. Actual assignments must follow company policies and delegated authority.**

| S&OP Activity | Sales | Demand | Supply | Prod | Proc | Inv | Fin | S&OP | Exec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Collect sales data | R | C | I | I | I | I | C | A | I |
| Prepare demand forecast | C | R/A | C | I | I | I | C | I | I |
| Validate demand assumptions | R | A | C | I | I | I | C | C | I |
| Develop supply plan | I | C | R/A | C | C | C | C | I | I |
| Evaluate production capacity | I | I | C | R/A | I | C | C | I | I |
| Evaluate material availability | I | I | C | C | R/A | C | I | I | I |
| Validate inventory | I | I | C | C | C | R/A | I | I | I |
| Evaluate financial impact | C | C | C | C | C | C | R/A | I | I |
| Coordinate Pre-S&OP | C | C | C | C | C | C | C | R/A | I |
| Approve major business trade-offs | C | C | C | C | C | C | C | R | A |
| Execute approved plan | R | R | R | R | R | R | R | A* | I |
| Monitor S&OP action items | R | R | R | R | R | R | R | A | I |

*Note: The final two rows are simplified examples. In an actual company, execution accountability should be assigned to the appropriate functional manager for each individual action. The S&OP Manager coordinates and monitors implementation rather than automatically becoming accountable for every operational deliverable.*

### 10.3 How to interpret the matrix

Let's examine three rows.

**Example 1: Prepare Demand Forecast**

- Demand Planning = R/A
- Sales = C
- Supply Planning = C
- Other departments = I

This means Demand Planning prepares and owns the demand forecast.

Sales provides customer information, while Supply Planning provides relevant supply considerations.

**Example 2: Evaluate Production Capacity**

- Production = R/A
- Supply Planning = C

Production owns the evaluation of its actual manufacturing capabilities.

Supply Planning uses the information to develop the overall supply plan.

**Example 3: Approve Major Business Trade-offs**

- Executive Leadership = A
- S&OP Manager = R

The S&OP Manager coordinates the approval process and presents the required information.

Executive Leadership owns the final approval decision.

### 10.4 Common RACI mistakes

| Mistake | Why it is problematic |
|---|---|
| Assigning everyone as Responsible | Nobody has clear ownership |
| Having no Accountable person | Decisions may remain unresolved |
| Assigning multiple Accountable owners without distinction | Conflicting authority |
| Treating Consulted as Approver | Input is confused with decision authority |
| Assigning the S&OP Analyst as owner of all data | Functional data ownership becomes unclear |
| Making the S&OP Manager accountable for all operational execution | Process ownership is confused with functional accountability |
| Not updating the RACI | Responsibilities become outdated when the organization changes |

Remember:

**RACI defines participation and ownership. It does not automatically establish the company's approval limits or financial authority.**

# 11. BUSINESS PERSPECTIVE AND DECISION-MAKING

Let's now think like an S&OP professional.

The purpose of establishing responsibilities is not simply to organize meetings or assign tasks.

It is to improve the quality and speed of business decisions.

### 11.1 Impact on Business Performance

| Functional responsibility | Potential business impact |
|---|---|
| Sales provides accurate customer requirements | Better customer service |
| Demand Planning maintains reliable forecasts | Better demand visibility |
| Supply Planning evaluates constraints | More feasible production plans |
| Production executes approved schedules | Better production performance |
| Procurement secures materials | Reduced material shortages |
| Inventory maintains accurate stock | Better inventory availability |
| Finance evaluates costs | Improved profitability |
| S&OP Manager coordinates the process | Better cross-functional alignment |
| Executive Leadership approves trade-offs | Better strategic decision-making |

### 11.2 Five Governance Principles to Remember

1. **Functional ownership:** Each department owns its functional data, processes, and deliverables.
2. **Process ownership:** The S&OP Manager ensures that the complete S&OP process operates properly.
3. **Decision authority:** Decisions must be made by individuals with the appropriate delegated authority.
4. **Execution accountability:** Approved actions must have clearly assigned owners.
5. **Shared business objectives:** Departments must optimize the overall business outcome rather than individual departmental performance alone.

### 11.3 What makes an S&OP organization effective?

An effective S&OP organization has:

- Clearly defined responsibilities.
- Reliable information exchange.
- Consistent meeting participation.
- Appropriate decision-making authority.
- Transparent escalation procedures.
- Documented decisions.
- Clear action ownership.
- Continuous performance monitoring.

Without these elements, S&OP can become a series of meetings where departments present reports but fail to make coordinated business decisions.

# 12. KEY TAKEAWAYS

Before proceeding to the exercises, make sure you understand these concepts.

| Concept | What you should remember |
|---|---|
| Cross-functional participation | S&OP requires different departments because business decisions affect multiple functions. |
| Functional ownership | Each department owns its respective business responsibilities. |
| Process ownership | The S&OP Manager manages the overall S&OP process. |
| Executive governance | Executives approve major strategic decisions and business trade-offs. |
| Responsible | The person or team performing the work. |
| Accountable | The person who owns the final outcome. |
| Consulted | The people who provide input. |
| Informed | The people who receive updates. |
| RACI Matrix | A tool for clarifying roles and responsibilities. |
| S&OP Analyst | Consolidates information, analyzes performance, coordinates inputs, and supports decisions. |

**The most important distinction in Topic 8.7:**

- Functional responsibility is about owning departmental activities.
- Process ownership is about managing the S&OP process.
- Decision authority is about having the right to approve decisions.
- Execution accountability is about ensuring approved actions are completed.

These four concepts must not be confused.
