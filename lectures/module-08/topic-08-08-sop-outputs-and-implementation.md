## 1. Topic Overview

### What are S&OP Outputs and Implementation?

Sa previous topics, pinag-aralan natin kung paano gumagana ang S&OP process, paano nagkakaroon ng demand and supply alignment, sino ang involved, at paano gumagawa ng decisions ang management.

Pero may isang importanteng tanong:

**After the Executive S&OP meeting, what happens next?**

Halimbawa, nag-meeting ang management at nag-agree sila na:

- Increase monthly product demand fulfillment from 10,000 to 12,000 units.
- Increase production capacity by 20%.
- Purchase additional raw materials.
- Maintain a minimum finished goods inventory of 2,000 units.
- Approve additional overtime expenses.

Maganda ang decisions. Pero paano kung walang nakakaalam kung sino ang bibili ng additional materials? Paano kung hindi na-update ng Production ang capacity plan? Paano kung Sales continues using the old forecast?

Kahit maganda ang S&OP decisions, walang business benefit kung hindi ito naipapatupad.

Dito pumapasok ang **S&OP Outputs and Implementation**.

**Definition:**

S&OP Outputs and Implementation refer to the approved plans, management decisions, documented actions, and follow-through activities generated by the S&OP process to ensure that business objectives are translated into coordinated operational activities.

### Why is this topic important?

May tatlong pangunahing purposes:

1. **Alignment:** Ensure na lahat ng departments ay gumagamit ng parehong approved business plan.
2. **Accountability:** Ensure na may responsible person at deadline ang bawat management decision.
3. **Execution:** Ensure na ang approved plans ay nagiging actual business activities at measurable results.

## 2. Layman's Explanation

### Think of S&OP as planning a large company event.

Imagine na ikaw ang organizer ng isang company event with an expected attendance of 1,000 people.

Before the event, kailangan mong mag-decide tungkol sa:

- Expected attendees
- Food requirements
- Venue capacity
- Number of staff
- Budget
- Equipment requirements

After the management meeting, approved na ang plan:

| Requirement | Approved Plan |
|---|---:|
| Expected attendees | 1,000 |
| Food packages | 1,100 |
| Staff | 30 |
| Venue capacity | 1,200 |
| Budget | ₱500,000 |

These are your **planning outputs**.

Then, management makes decisions:

- Approve additional food budget.
- Hire 5 additional staff.
- Reserve additional equipment.

These are your **management decisions**.

Finally, someone must execute them:

- Procurement orders additional food.
- HR hires additional staff.
- Operations reserves equipment.
- Finance releases the approved budget.

These are your **execution activities**.

Notice the difference?

| Category | Main question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Planning Output | What is the plan? | 1,100 food packages |
| Management Decision | What did management approve? | Approve additional food budget |
| Execution Activity | What must people actually do? | Procurement places an order |

Ganito rin ang nangyayari sa manufacturing company.

Ang S&OP ay hindi natatapos sa paggawa ng plan. Kailangan itong maipasa sa departments at maisagawa.

**Remember this sequence:**

1. **PLAN:** What does the business need? Demand, supply, inventory, capacity and financial plans.
2. **APPROVE:** What has management decided? Approved targets, priorities, resources and trade-offs.
3. **IMPLEMENT:** Who will do what, and when? Production, procurement, inventory and commercial actions.
4. **MONITOR:** Are we achieving the approved plan? Actual vs. plan, deviations, corrective actions and escalation.

This is the fundamental logic of Topic 8.8.

## 3. Technical Explanation

### 3.1 The three categories of S&OP outputs

Sa professional S&OP environment, importanteng paghiwalayin ang tatlong categories.

**A. Planning Outputs**

Ito ang numerical plans and projections na nabuo through demand planning, supply planning, inventory planning, and financial reconciliation.

Examples:

- Consensus demand forecast
- Production volume plan
- Inventory projection
- Capacity requirements
- Procurement requirements
- Financial forecast

These outputs answer:

*What is the company's agreed business plan?*

**B. Management Decisions**

Ito ang decisions made by management to resolve issues, approve resources, or establish business priorities.

Examples:

- Approve additional production capacity.
- Prioritize high-margin products.
- Authorize overtime.
- Approve additional inventory investment.
- Accept a temporary customer service risk.
- Approve outsourcing to a third-party manufacturer.

These answer:

*What decisions must the organization make to support the plan?*

**C. Execution Activities**

Ito ang actual tasks performed by departments to implement the approved plan.

Examples:

- Procurement issues purchase orders.
- Production updates its manufacturing schedule.
- Warehouse prepares additional storage capacity.
- Sales communicates delivery commitments.
- Finance releases the approved budget.

These answer:

*What must each department do to make the plan happen?*

### 3.2 Understanding the relationship

Let's use a manufacturing example.

A company expects demand to increase from 10,000 to 12,000 units next month.

| Category | Example |
|---|---|
| Planning Output | Demand plan increases to 12,000 units. |
| Planning Output | Supply plan indicates 12,000 units can be produced. |
| Management Decision | Approve additional overtime and raw material spending. |
| Execution Activity | Procurement orders additional raw materials. |
| Execution Activity | Production schedules additional shifts. |
| Monitoring Output | Actual production versus approved production plan. |

Important distinction:

**An approved S&OP plan is not the same as a detailed operational execution schedule.**

The S&OP plan establishes the agreed business direction, targets, volumes, constraints, and priorities.

The operational schedule determines the detailed execution, such as:

- Which production line will run?
- Which product will be manufactured first?
- What date and time will production start?
- Which machine will be assigned?
- What is the daily production quantity?
- Which raw materials must be issued?

S&OP generally operates at an aggregated planning level, while operational planning translates those targets into detailed schedules.

The level of detail and planning horizon vary by organization and industry.

### 3.3 The S&OP output hierarchy

**Level 1: Executive S&OP Plan**

Management-approved business direction:

- Monthly demand and supply volumes
- Financial targets
- Inventory objectives
- Capacity decisions

**Level 2: Functional Plans**

Department-level implementation plans:

- Production volume allocation
- Procurement requirements
- Inventory replenishment
- Resource and staffing requirements

**Level 3: Detailed Operational Schedules**

Specific execution instructions:

- Daily production schedules
- Machine assignments
- Purchase order delivery dates
- Material issue schedules

**Level 4: Actual Execution and Monitoring**

Actual activities and performance:

- Actual production
- Actual material consumption
- Actual inventory
- Actual customer shipments

This hierarchy is important because the Executive S&OP meeting does not normally assign every individual production order or machine schedule.

Instead, it approves the direction and establishes the boundaries within which the operational teams work.

## 4. Detailed Discussion

### 4.1 Key S&OP Deliverables

Let's discuss the major deliverables one by one.

#### A. Consensus Demand Plan

Ito ang agreed demand forecast ng organization.

Hindi ito basta Sales forecast lamang. Pinag-uusapan at ina-align ito across relevant departments using available information.

Typical inputs include:

- Historical sales
- Customer orders
- Market demand
- Sales pipeline
- Customer forecasts
- New product launches
- Promotions
- Market trends

Example:

| Product | Previous Forecast | Approved Demand Plan |
|---|---:|---:|
| Product A | 5,000 | 6,000 |
| Product B | 3,000 | 3,500 |
| Product C | 2,000 | 2,500 |
| **Total** | **10,000** | **12,000** |

The consensus demand plan becomes the demand reference for supply planning.

**Important:** Consensus demand is not necessarily the same as customer orders. It represents the organization's agreed expectation of future demand.

#### B. Approved Supply and Production Plan

Ito naman ang agreed supply response to the demand plan.

The supply plan considers:

- Available production capacity
- Equipment availability
- Labor requirements
- Production efficiency
- Material availability
- Supplier constraints
- Planned downtime
- Existing inventory

Example:

| Product | Demand | Planned Production | Existing Inventory | Projected Available Supply |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 6,000 | 5,500 | 500 | 6,000 |
| B | 3,500 | 3,200 | 300 | 3,500 |
| C | 2,500 | 2,000 | 500 | 2,500 |
| **Total** | **12,000** | **10,700** | **1,300** | **12,000** |

Assumption: All beginning inventory is usable and available for customer demand, with no additional losses or adjustments.

Notice that the company does not necessarily need to produce all 12,000 units because it already has 1,300 units in inventory.

The important consideration is whether projected supply can support the demand.

#### C. Inventory Projections and Targets

The S&OP process also establishes the expected inventory position.

There are two important concepts:

- **Inventory Target:** The desired inventory level that the company wants to maintain.
- **Inventory Projection:** The expected inventory balance based on forecast demand, supply, and planned movements.

Basic inventory equation:

\[
Ending\ Inventory = Beginning\ Inventory
+ Production + Receipts - Demand
\]

Example:

| Item | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Beginning Inventory | 2,000 |
| Planned Production | 12,000 |
| Planned Receipts | 0 |
| Forecast Demand | 11,000 |
| **Projected Ending Inventory** | **3,000** |

The projected ending inventory is 3,000 units.

Kung ang inventory target ay 2,500 units, may projected excess na 500 units.

This can trigger a discussion about whether production should be reduced, inventory targets adjusted, or additional demand opportunities pursued.

#### D. Capacity and Resource Plans

The capacity plan determines whether the organization has sufficient resources to support the approved demand and production requirements.

Resources may include:

- Machines and production lines
- Direct labor
- Working shifts
- Production hours
- Maintenance availability
- Warehouse capacity
- Supplier capacity

Example:

| Metric | Available | Required |
|---|---:|---:|
| Monthly machine hours | 1,000 | 1,150 |
| Direct labor hours | 1,200 | 1,350 |
| Production lines | 4 | 4 |

The company has a machine capacity shortage of 150 hours and a labor shortage of 150 hours.

Possible management decisions:

1. Approve overtime.
2. Add another shift.
3. Improve production efficiency.
4. Transfer production to another facility.
5. Outsource selected production volumes.

The capacity plan helps management understand what resources must change to support the business plan.

#### E. Financial Projections and Business Outlook

Hindi lang operational feasibility ang concern ng S&OP. Financial feasibility is equally important.

Typical financial outputs include:

- Revenue projections
- Cost of goods sold
- Gross margin
- Operating expenses
- Inventory investment
- Overtime expenses
- Procurement spending
- Profitability projections

Example:

| Financial Metric | Previous Plan | Revised Plan |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | ₱10M | ₱12M |
| Production Cost | ₱6M | ₱7.2M |
| Gross Profit | ₱4M | ₱4.8M |
| Gross Margin | 40% | 40% |

Although revenue increases by ₱2 million, the company must also consider the additional production costs.

A demand increase is not automatically a good business decision if the additional volume generates insufficient profitability.

#### F. Demand-Supply Gaps, Risks, and Opportunities

This output identifies situations where the demand plan and supply plan are not aligned.

Example:

| Product | Demand | Available Supply | Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 6,000 | 6,000 | 0 |
| B | 3,500 | 3,000 | -500 |
| C | 2,500 | 2,700 | +200 |
| **Total** | **12,000** | **11,700** | **-300** |

Interpretation:

- Product A is balanced.
- Product B has a shortage of 500 units.
- Product C has an excess of 200 units.
- Overall, the company has a shortage of 300 units.

However, hindi ibig sabihin na puwedeng gamitin ang 200 excess units of Product C to satisfy the 500-unit shortage of Product B.

Different products have different specifications, customer requirements, and demand.

Possible actions:

- Increase production of Product B.
- Reallocate available capacity.
- Prioritize strategic customers.
- Evaluate alternative suppliers.
- Negotiate delivery schedules.

#### G. Scenario Evaluations and Management Decisions

Scenario evaluation helps management compare alternative business plans before approving one.

Example:

A company expects an additional 2,000 units of demand.

| Scenario | Production Response | Additional Cost | Potential Outcome |
|---|---|---:|---|
| A | Overtime | ₱200,000 | Higher production with limited structural changes |
| B | Additional shift | ₱350,000 | More production capacity |
| C | Outsourcing | ₱500,000 | Additional external supply |
| D | Reject additional demand | ₱0 | Potential lost sales |

These are illustrative assumptions, not standard industry costs.

Management evaluates each scenario based on:

- Financial impact
- Customer service
- Operational feasibility
- Lead time
- Risk
- Long-term capacity implications

The selected scenario becomes part of the approved S&OP plan.

### 4.2 Plan Approval and Communication

After the Executive S&OP meeting, kailangan i-finalize ang approved plan.

Hindi sapat na may verbal agreement lang during the meeting.

There must be an official record of the approved business plan.

**Typical approval process:**

1. Review the final demand and supply plans.
2. Review unresolved gaps and risks.
3. Confirm management decisions.
4. Document approved assumptions and exceptions.
5. Obtain the required management approval.
6. Publish the approved plan.
7. Distribute the relevant outputs to functional departments.

#### Who receives the approved plan?

| Department | Information Required | Primary Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | Consensus demand, product priorities | Align customer commitments |
| Operations | Production volumes, capacity targets | Prepare production resources |
| Supply Chain | Supply plan, inventory projections | Balance supply and inventory |
| Procurement | Material requirements, purchasing priorities | Secure materials |
| Finance | Revenue, costs, inventory investment | Align financial outlook |
| Warehouse | Inventory targets, inbound/outbound volumes | Prepare storage and handling |
| Management | Overall plan, risks, decisions | Monitor business performance |

Notice that hindi necessarily pare-pareho ang report na matatanggap ng bawat department.

For example, Procurement needs material requirements and supplier priorities. Finance needs financial projections and spending implications.

But all departments must work from the same approved planning assumptions and version.

#### Why is version control important?

Imagine this situation:

- Sales uses demand forecast Version 3: 12,000 units.
- Production uses Version 2: 10,000 units.
- Procurement uses Version 1: 9,000 units.
- Finance uses Version 3: 12,000 units.

Ano ang possible result?

Production may not have enough capacity, Procurement may purchase insufficient raw materials, and Finance may report a financial outlook based on a plan that Operations cannot support.

This is a planning alignment failure.

A good S&OP plan should have:

| Information | Example |
|---|---|
| Plan Name | November 2026 S&OP Plan |
| Version | V3.0 |
| Approval Date | November 25, 2026 |
| Planning Horizon | December 2026 – May 2027 |
| Status | Approved |
| Approving Authority | Executive S&OP Team |
| Key Assumptions | 20% demand increase |
| Plan Owner | S&OP / Planning Team |

The dates and details here are illustrative.

**Key principle:** There should be one official approved planning baseline, even if different departments use different detailed operational schedules.

### 4.3 Implementation of Approved Plans

This is where planning becomes actual work.

The approved plan must be translated into departmental actions.

Let's examine the relationship.

| Approved S&OP Direction | Department | Implementation Activity |
|---|---|---|
| Increase production by 2,000 units | Operations | Revise production plan |
| Secure additional materials | Procurement | Issue purchase orders |
| Increase inventory target | Supply Chain | Update replenishment strategy |
| Increase working hours | HR / Operations | Arrange staffing and overtime |
| Approve additional spending | Finance | Update budget and spending authorization |
| Fulfill additional customer demand | Sales | Align customer commitments |

#### Action ownership and accountability

Every significant action should have an owner and deadline.

A decision without an assigned owner is likely to become an unresolved discussion.

Consider this example:

| Action Item | Owner | Deadline | Expected Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purchase additional raw materials | Procurement Manager | Nov. 28 | Confirmed supplier orders |
| Increase machine capacity | Operations Manager | Dec. 1 | Approved capacity plan |
| Arrange overtime | Production Supervisor | Dec. 2 | Confirmed manpower schedule |
| Update inventory targets | Supply Chain Analyst | Nov. 27 | Updated inventory plan |
| Validate financial impact | Finance Analyst | Nov. 29 | Revised cost projection |

A useful action tracker should also include:

- Action ID
- Related management decision
- Responsible owner
- Supporting department
- Due date
- Current status
- Completion evidence
- Risks or blockers
- Escalation status

#### How should operational schedules align with S&OP?

Ito ang isa sa pinakamahalagang concepts sa topic.

Let's say the approved S&OP plan requires 12,000 units next month.

The Production department still needs to determine:

- Which product will run on Line 1?
- Which product will run on Line 2?
- What is the daily production target?
- When will maintenance occur?
- How many operators are needed per shift?
- When will materials be issued?

These details belong to detailed operational planning.

**Example: Different planning levels**

| Planning Level | Example |
|---|---|
| **S&OP** | Produce 12,000 units in December. |
| **Production Planning** | Allocate 12,000 units across production lines and weeks. |
| **Production Scheduling** | Assign specific products, machines, shifts, dates, and sequences. |
| **Shop-floor Execution** | Operators manufacture products according to released production orders. |

**Important:** The detailed schedule may change due to machine breakdowns, material delays, or urgent customer orders. However, these changes should not silently invalidate the approved S&OP direction.

If the changes materially affect the approved business plan, they must be evaluated and escalated through the appropriate planning and decision-making process.

### 4.4 Managing Changes After Plan Approval

An approved plan is not a guarantee that actual business conditions will remain unchanged.

Manufacturing operations are exposed to uncertainties.

Examples:

- Unexpected customer demand increase
- Supplier delivery delay
- Equipment breakdown
- Unexpected yield deterioration
- Labor shortage
- Quality issues
- Raw material price increases

The organization must distinguish between normal execution adjustments and changes requiring management approval.

| Situation | Typical Response |
|---|---|
| Minor daily production delay | Production adjusts detailed schedule |
| Small material shortage | Procurement checks alternatives |
| Unexpected machine downtime | Operations evaluates recovery plan |
| Major capacity shortage | Escalate to supply planning / S&OP leadership |
| Significant demand increase | Reassess demand-supply balance |
| Major financial impact | Finance and management review |

The principle is:

**Operational flexibility is allowed, but material changes to the approved business plan require appropriate review and authorization.**

### 4.5 Execution Monitoring and Follow-through

After implementation begins, the organization must monitor whether the approved plan is actually being achieved.

There are two important comparisons:

1. Approved Plan vs. Actual Performance
2. Planned Actions vs. Actual Implementation Status

Example:

| KPI | Approved Plan | Actual | Variance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Production | 12,000 | 11,200 | -800 |
| Sales Shipments | 11,000 | 10,500 | -500 |
| Ending Inventory | 3,000 | 2,700 | -300 |
| Raw Material Receipts | 15,000 | 13,000 | -2,000 |

The analyst must investigate the reasons behind these deviations.

For example:

- Why was production 800 units below plan?
- Was it caused by machine downtime?
- Did Procurement receive insufficient materials?
- Was the actual production yield lower than expected?
- Did the company consume more raw materials than planned?

A variance is not automatically a problem caused by poor performance. It is an indication that actual conditions differ from the plan and require investigation.

#### Corrective actions

Once the deviation is understood, the organization can determine the appropriate response.

| Deviation | Possible Corrective Action |
|---|---|
| Production below plan | Add recovery shifts |
| Supplier delay | Expedite delivery or source alternatives |
| Demand higher than forecast | Reassess capacity and inventory |
| Inventory below target | Increase replenishment |
| Higher production costs | Review efficiency and resource utilization |

#### Escalation

Not every deviation requires executive intervention.

The escalation depends on:

- Financial impact
- Customer impact
- Duration
- Severity
- Risk to future demand fulfillment
- Whether the issue exceeds the department's authority

For example, a two-hour production delay may be handled by the supervisor.

But a two-week equipment breakdown that threatens customer deliveries may require senior management intervention.

### 4.6 Relationship Between S&OP and S&OE

S&OP and S&OE are closely connected, but they are not the same process.

| Dimension | S&OP | S&OE |
|---|---|---|
| Full Name | Sales & Operations Planning | Sales & Operations Execution |
| Primary Purpose | Align the business plan | Manage short-term execution |
| Focus | Demand, supply, capacity, inventory, finance | Actual orders, shortages, production, delivery |
| Planning Level | Aggregated | Detailed |
| Time Horizon | Commonly monthly, with a rolling horizon | Commonly daily or weekly |
| Main Participants | Cross-functional management and planning teams | Operational and functional teams |
| Output | Approved integrated business plan | Execution decisions and corrective actions |

*Note: The exact meeting frequency and time horizon vary by company.*

Example:

**S&OP decision:**

The company approves production of 12,000 units next month.

**S&OE activity:**

During Week 1, a critical machine breaks down, reducing production capacity by 500 units.

The S&OE team evaluates:

- Can production be transferred to another line?
- Can another shift recover the lost volume?
- Are there enough materials?
- Which customer orders must be prioritized?

If the problem can be resolved within existing approved capacity and policies, S&OE manages the recovery.

If the issue requires substantial additional investment, changes the business outlook, or creates a major supply-demand imbalance, it may need to be escalated for S&OP or management review.

We will study the full distinction and interaction in Topic 8.9.

### 4.7 S&OP Documentation and Reporting

A professional S&OP process must be supported by proper documentation.

Why?

Because management decisions must remain traceable even after the meeting ends.

Typical S&OP documentation includes:

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Approved S&OP Plan | Official planning baseline |
| Demand Plan Report | Documents agreed demand |
| Supply Plan Report | Documents planned supply |
| Inventory Projection | Shows expected inventory |
| Capacity Report | Identifies resource availability and constraints |
| Financial Summary | Shows business and financial implications |
| Risk and Opportunity Register | Records significant business risks |
| Decision Log | Documents management approvals |
| Action Tracker | Tracks owners and deadlines |
| Actual vs. Plan Dashboard | Monitors implementation performance |

#### What should a Decision Log contain?

A decision log records what management agreed to do.

Example:

| Field | Sample Entry |
|---|---|
| Decision ID | DEC-001 |
| Meeting | November Executive S&OP |
| Issue | Insufficient production capacity |
| Decision | Approve additional overtime |
| Expected Impact | Additional 2,000 units |
| Financial Impact | ₱200,000 |
| Approver | Executive Management |
| Effective Period | December |
| Status | Approved |

#### What should an Action Tracker contain?

The action tracker follows the implementation of each approved decision.

Example:

| ID | Action | Owner | Due Date | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ACT-001 | Confirm overtime schedule | Operations | Dec. 2 | In Progress |
| ACT-002 | Order additional materials | Procurement | Nov. 28 | Completed |
| ACT-003 | Update inventory target | Supply Chain | Nov. 29 | Completed |
| ACT-004 | Validate revised cost | Finance | Nov. 30 | Pending |

A decision log answers:

**What did management decide?**

An action tracker answers:

**Are we implementing what management decided?**

These two documents should be linked.

#### Data consistency and traceability

As an S&OP analyst, you must ensure that reports use consistent data.

Important controls include:

1. Consistent product codes and descriptions.
2. Consistent units of measurement.
3. Consistent planning periods.
4. Correct inventory and demand cut-off dates.
5. Clear distinction between actuals and forecasts.
6. Consistent approved plan versions.
7. Documented assumptions and adjustments.
8. Traceable sources of reported data.

For example, if one report uses pieces while another uses thousands of pieces, the numbers may appear inconsistent even if the underlying data is correct.

Similarly, a December demand plan should not accidentally be compared against November actuals.

These may seem like simple data issues, but they can lead to incorrect management decisions.

## 5. Practical Examples and Applications

### 5.1 Manufacturing Case Study: Approved Demand Increase and Capacity Adjustment

Let's build a realistic manufacturing scenario.

Imagine that you are working as an S&OP Analyst in a manufacturing company producing electronic components.

The company receives information that a major customer will increase its demand next month.

Initially, the approved monthly demand is 10,000 units. The customer now expects 12,000 units.

The challenge is that the company must increase production while maintaining inventory targets, securing raw materials, and controlling additional costs.

### Stage 1: Initial Business Situation

The company has the following position:

| Metric | Initial Plan |
|---|---:|
| Monthly Demand | 10,000 units |
| Monthly Production Capacity | 11,000 units |
| Beginning Inventory | 2,000 units |
| Target Ending Inventory | 3,000 units |
| Available Raw Materials | 10,500 units |
| Production Yield | 95% |

For simplicity, assume one unit of finished goods requires one unit of raw material input, and production yield is 95%.

The company receives a revised customer forecast of 12,000 units.

This creates a new planning requirement.

### Stage 2: Demand-Supply Evaluation

The Demand Planning team updates the forecast.

| Metric | Original | Revised |
|---|---:|---:|
| Demand | 10,000 | 12,000 |
| Beginning Inventory | 2,000 | 2,000 |
| Target Ending Inventory | 3,000 | 3,000 |

How much finished goods production is required?

Using the inventory equation:

\[
Production = Demand + Ending\ Inventory - Beginning\ Inventory
\]

\[
Production = 12,000 + 3,000 - 2,000
\]

\[
\boxed{Production = 13,000\ units}
\]

The company must produce 13,000 units to satisfy the expected demand and maintain its inventory target.

But available capacity is only 11,000 units.

<badge>Capacity Gap: 2,000 units</badge>

This is the issue that must be addressed during supply planning and Executive S&OP.

### Stage 3: Raw Material Requirement

Now, let's consider production yield.

The company needs 13,000 good finished units, but production yield is 95%.

The raw material input requirement is:

\[
Raw\ Material =
\frac{Required\ Good\ Output}{Yield}
\]

\[
=\frac{13,000}{0.95}
\]

\[
\boxed{Raw\ Material = 13,684.21\ units}
\]

Available raw materials are only 10,500 units.

Therefore:

\[
Material\ Shortage = 13,684.21 - 10,500
\]

\[
\boxed{Material\ Shortage = 3,184.21\ units}
\]

The company has two problems:

1. Insufficient production capacity.
2. Insufficient raw materials.

Increasing production capacity alone will not solve the problem because the company cannot manufacture without sufficient materials.

This is why S&OP requires cross-functional planning.

### Stage 4: Scenario Evaluation

The Supply Planning team evaluates three possible alternatives.

| Scenario | Proposed Solution | Capacity | Additional Cost |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| A | Regular operations only | 11,000 | ₱0 |
| B | Overtime and additional shifts | 13,000 | ₱200,000 |
| C | Outsource production | 13,000 | ₱350,000 |

These costs are illustrative.

Management evaluates the scenarios.

Scenario A cannot satisfy the required production volume.

Scenario B can achieve the required production through additional working hours.

Scenario C can also achieve the target but has higher estimated costs.

Management selects Scenario B, subject to material availability and operational feasibility.

### Stage 5: Executive S&OP Decisions

During the Executive S&OP meeting, management approves the following:

| Decision | Approved Direction |
|---|---|
| Demand Plan | Increase to 12,000 units |
| Production Plan | Produce 13,000 units |
| Capacity | Approve additional shifts |
| Procurement | Purchase additional 3,200 units of raw materials, rounded for planning |
| Inventory | Maintain 3,000 units of ending inventory |
| Additional Expense | Approve ₱200,000 overtime budget |
| Customer Priority | Prioritize the major customer's confirmed requirements |

Notice that management does not need to decide which exact machine will produce each batch.

That level of detail belongs to operational planning.

### Stage 6: Departmental Implementation

After approval, the S&OP Analyst distributes the approved plan.

Each department receives its responsibilities.

| Department | Action | Owner | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | Confirm revised customer demand | Sales Manager | Nov. 27 |
| Operations | Prepare 13,000-unit production plan | Operations Manager | Nov. 29 |
| Procurement | Purchase additional materials | Procurement Manager | Nov. 28 |
| Supply Chain | Update inventory projections | Supply Chain Planner | Nov. 27 |
| HR | Arrange additional manpower | HR Representative | Dec. 1 |
| Finance | Update overtime budget | Finance Analyst | Nov. 29 |

The S&OP Analyst records these actions in the action tracker.

### Stage 7: Actual Execution Monitoring

Suppose the company begins production.

At the end of the month, the actual results are:

| Metric | Approved Plan | Actual | Variance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Demand | 12,000 | 12,000 | 0 |
| Production | 13,000 | 12,200 | -800 |
| Customer Shipments | 12,000 | 11,800 | -200 |
| Ending Inventory | 3,000 | 2,400 | -600 |
| Raw Material Receipts | 13,700 | 13,700 | 0 |

The company successfully received the required materials, but actual production was 800 units below plan.

Customer shipments were also 200 units below demand.

The ending inventory was 600 units below the target.

This indicates an execution problem.

### Stage 8: Investigating the Deviation

The S&OP Analyst investigates the reason for the production shortage.

The Operations team reports:

- Unexpected equipment downtime.
- Lower-than-expected production efficiency.
- Additional quality-related production losses.

The Procurement team confirms that materials were received according to plan.

Therefore, the primary issue was not procurement. It was production execution.

The company now evaluates corrective actions.

| Issue | Proposed Action | Responsible Department |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment downtime | Improve maintenance and recovery schedule | Engineering |
| Lower production efficiency | Review operating parameters and performance | Operations |
| Inventory below target | Develop replenishment plan | Supply Chain |
| Unfulfilled customer demand | Coordinate revised delivery commitment | Sales |

Management may also need to reassess the following month's supply plan if the inventory shortfall creates a continuing risk.

### Stage 9: Business Performance Review

At the end of the cycle, management evaluates whether the approved plan delivered the expected results.

Important questions:

1. Was the demand increase correctly forecasted?
2. Was the approved capacity sufficient?
3. Were materials received on time?
4. Did the additional overtime produce the expected volume?
5. Was customer demand fulfilled?
6. Was the inventory target maintained?
7. Did the additional expenditure generate sufficient business value?

This is how S&OP implementation creates a continuous feedback loop.

**Important lesson:** A successful S&OP process does not require every actual result to perfectly match the approved plan. It requires the organization to identify deviations, understand their causes, take appropriate action, and improve future planning.

## 6. Formulas and Calculations

Let's consolidate the important calculations used in this topic.

### 6.1 Production Requirement

\[
Production = Demand + Target\ Ending\ Inventory - Beginning\ Inventory
\]

Example:

- Demand = 12,000 units
- Target ending inventory = 3,000 units
- Beginning inventory = 2,000 units

\[
Production = 12,000 + 3,000 - 2,000
\]

\[
=13,000\ units
\]

Purpose: Determine how much finished goods production is required to meet demand and achieve the inventory target.

### 6.2 Production Requirement Considering Yield

\[
Required\ Input =
\frac{Required\ Good\ Output}{Yield}
\]

Example:

\[
=\frac{13,000}{0.95}
=13,684.21\ units
\]

Purpose: Determine the required production input when some units are lost through defects or process yield losses.

### 6.3 Capacity Gap

\[
Capacity\ Gap = Required\ Capacity - Available\ Capacity
\]

Example:

\[
=13,000-11,000
\]

\[
=2,000\ units
\]

A positive result indicates insufficient capacity. A negative result indicates available capacity exceeds the requirement.

### 6.4 Plan vs. Actual Variance

\[
Variance = Actual - Plan
\]

Example:

\[
=12,200-13,000
\]

\[
=-800\ units
\]

A negative variance means actual production is below the approved plan.

### 6.5 Plan Attainment

\[
Plan\ Attainment(\%) =
\frac{Actual}{Plan}\times100
\]

Example:

\[
=\frac{12,200}{13,000}\times100
\]

\[
=93.85\%
\]

Interpretation: The company achieved 93.85% of its approved production target.

### 6.6 Inventory Target Attainment

\[
Inventory\ Attainment(\%) =
\frac{Actual\ Ending\ Inventory}{Target\ Inventory}
\times100
\]

Example:

\[
=\frac{2,400}{3,000}\times100
\]

\[
=80\%
\]

Interpretation: Actual ending inventory is only 80% of the desired target.

### 6.7 Action Completion Rate

\[
Action\ Completion(\%) =
\frac{Completed\ Actions}{Total\ Due\ Actions}
\times100
\]

Example:

Suppose 10 action items are due and 8 are completed.

\[
=\frac{8}{10}\times100=80\%
\]

Interpretation: The organization completed 80% of the actions due for the reporting period.

Important: Do not automatically count an overdue action as completed simply because the responsible department says it is almost finished. Completion should have appropriate evidence.

## 7. Business Perspective and Decision-Making

### 7.1 Why does implementation matter to the business?

Consider two companies.

**Company A: Meeting-oriented S&OP**

- Conducts monthly meetings.
- Prepares demand and supply reports.
- Discusses business risks.
- Approves additional production capacity.
- Does not assign action owners.
- Does not monitor implementation.

**Company B: Execution-oriented S&OP**

- Conducts monthly meetings.
- Prepares demand and supply reports.
- Documents management decisions.
- Assigns action owners and deadlines.
- Communicates approved plans.
- Monitors actual-versus-plan performance.
- Escalates major deviations.

Both companies conduct S&OP meetings.

However, Company B has a stronger process because management decisions are connected to measurable implementation.

### 7.2 Impact on important business areas

| Business Area | Impact of Effective S&OP Implementation |
|---|---|
| Demand | Better alignment between customer requirements and business plans |
| Supply | Better production and supply coordination |
| Inventory | Reduced risk of shortages and excess inventory |
| Capacity | Better utilization of machines and manpower |
| Procurement | Improved purchasing priorities and material availability |
| Finance | Better visibility of spending and profitability |
| Customer Service | Improved ability to meet customer commitments |
| Management | Better accountability and decision traceability |

### 7.3 What should an S&OP Analyst pay attention to?

As an S&OP Analyst, your job is not simply to prepare a monthly presentation.

You must help ensure that the approved plan remains visible, consistent, and actionable.

For example:

If management approves an increase in production, you should be able to determine:

- Was the production plan updated?
- Was the additional material requirement communicated?
- Did Procurement place the required purchase orders?
- Was additional capacity approved and arranged?
- Did Finance incorporate the additional expenses?
- Is actual production progressing according to plan?
- Does the company need management intervention?

This requires both analytical skills and coordination skills.

### 7.4 Common mistakes in S&OP implementation

| Common Mistake | Business Consequence |
|---|---|
| No official approved plan | Departments use different planning numbers |
| No action owners | Decisions are not implemented |
| No deadlines | Actions remain unresolved |
| No version control | Outdated forecasts are used |
| S&OP plan treated as production schedule | Operational teams lack necessary execution details |
| No actual-versus-plan monitoring | Deviations remain undetected |
| No escalation process | Major business risks are not addressed |
| Reports contain inconsistent data | Management makes decisions using incorrect information |

One particularly important mistake is treating the S&OP process as a monthly reporting exercise.

S&OP is a management process that must influence actual business decisions.

## 8. Key Takeaways

Here are the concepts you should remember before proceeding.

| Concept | What You Should Remember |
|---|---|
| S&OP Outputs | Plans, projections, decisions, and documented actions |
| Consensus Demand Plan | Agreed expectation of future demand |
| Supply Plan | How the company intends to fulfill demand |
| Inventory Projection | Expected inventory position |
| Capacity Plan | Required resources versus available resources |
| Financial Projection | Financial implications of the integrated plan |
| Decision Log | Records what management approved |
| Action Tracker | Records who must implement each decision |
| Plan Approval | Establishes the official planning baseline |
| Version Control | Ensures departments use consistent approved information |
| Implementation | Converts decisions into departmental activities |
| Execution Monitoring | Measures progress and actual performance |
| Escalation | Brings significant issues to the appropriate decision-makers |
| S&OE | Manages short-term operational execution and disruptions |

**The most important distinction in Topic 8.8:**

- Planning outputs tell the organization what it intends to achieve.
- Management decisions authorize what needs to happen.
- Implementation translates those decisions into departmental actions.
- Execution monitoring determines whether the organization is achieving its objectives.

Remember this entire chain:

**Plan → Approve → Communicate → Implement → Monitor → Correct.**
